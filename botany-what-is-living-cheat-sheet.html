What Is Living?

A quick, simple revision guide to the characteristics of living organisms, with the NCERT distinctions and common exam traps.

Life = organised systems + metabolism + response
🎯 Practise 30 MCQs

1. The core idea

Living organisms are self-replicating, evolving and self-regulating interactive systems capable of responding to external stimuli.

A living system shows coordinated life processes — not just one visible feature.
Cellular organisation

Living organisms are made of one or more cells arranged in an organised way.

Metabolism

The sum total of chemical reactions taking place in a living system.

Consciousness

Sensing the environment and responding to changes or stimuli.

Growth and reproduction

Important characteristics of life, but neither is an absolute test for every individual.

2. Growth — living vs non-living

Growth = increase in mass and increase in number of individuals.
Living organismsNon-living objects
Growth occurs from within through cell division and/or cell enlargement.Some objects grow by external addition or deposition of material.
Growth is associated with metabolism and organised biological processes.Crystal size can increase without becoming alive.
Exam point: Increase in size alone cannot define life. Crystals can grow by external deposition.

3. Reproduction — important exception

Reproduction produces new individuals and helps maintain continuity of a species.

Sexual reproduction

Formation of offspring involving fusion of gametes.

Asexual reproduction

New individuals arise from a single parent without gamete fusion.

NCERT examples: Mules and sterile worker bees are living but generally do not reproduce. Therefore, reproduction is not an essential defining feature of every individual organism.

4. Metabolism — a defining feature

Metabolism includes all biochemical reactions in a living organism. It has two broad components:

Anabolism · builds up

Synthesis of complex molecules from simpler substances. Example: protein synthesis.

Catabolism · breaks down

Breakdown of complex molecules into simpler products. Example: cellular respiration.

Metabolism = Anabolism + Catabolism
Metabolic reactions can be demonstrated outside the body in cell-free systems, but an isolated reaction by itself is not a living organism.

5. Consciousness — response to stimuli

In biology, consciousness broadly means the ability to sense environmental changes and respond to them. It is not limited to human self-awareness.

StimulusExample response
LightPhototropic bending of a plant shoot
TouchLeaves of Mimosa pudica fold when touched
GravityRoots generally grow towards gravity
ChemicalsRoots can grow towards useful chemical signals

7. NEET / NCERT exam traps

1

Crystal growth ≠ life. Non-living crystals can increase in size by deposition.

2

Every living individual need not reproduce. Remember mule and sterile worker bee.

3

Metabolism is broader than respiration. It includes all anabolic and catabolic reactions.

4

Plants are responsive. Consciousness in this context includes sensing and responding to environmental stimuli.

5

Movement alone does not define life. Look for coordinated cellular and metabolic activity.

8. 20-second memory recap

Growth: increase in mass and number. Reproduction: continuity of species, but not possible in every individual. Metabolism: anabolism + catabolism. Consciousness: sense and respond. Cellular organisation: organised cell structure. Together, these help identify living systems.
