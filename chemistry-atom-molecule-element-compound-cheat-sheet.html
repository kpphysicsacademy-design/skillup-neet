1. Atom

An atom is the smallest particle of an element that takes part in a chemical reaction and retains the chemical identity of that element.
Subatomic particlesProton: +1 charge; neutron: no charge; electron: −1 charge.
Atomic nucleusContains protons and neutrons; electrons occupy regions around it.
For a neutral atom: number of protons = number of electrons. Atomic number (Z) = number of protons.

2. Molecule

A molecule is an electrically neutral group of two or more atoms held together by chemical bonds and capable of independent existence.
TypeExamplesAtoms per molecule
HomoatomicH₂, O₂, O₃, P₄, S₈Same element
HeteroatomicH₂O, CO₂, NH₃, CH₄Different elements
Not every element exists as a molecule: noble gases such as He and Ne commonly exist as individual atoms.

3. Atomicity

Atomicity is the number of atoms present in one molecule of an element.

MonoatomicHe, Ne, Ar — 1 atom
DiatomicH₂, N₂, O₂, Cl₂ — 2 atoms
TriatomicO₃ — 3 atoms
PolyatomicP₄, S₈ — more than 3 atoms

4. Element

An element is a pure substance made up of only one type of atom and cannot be decomposed into simpler substances by ordinary chemical methods.

Examples: Hydrogen (H), oxygen (O), carbon (C), sodium (Na), iron (Fe), copper (Cu).

An element may exist as single atoms (He), diatomic molecules (O₂), or larger molecules (S₈).

5. Compound

A compound is a pure substance formed when two or more different elements chemically combine in a fixed proportion by mass.
CompoundFormulaElements present
WaterH₂OHydrogen + oxygen
Carbon dioxideCO₂Carbon + oxygen
AmmoniaNH₃Nitrogen + hydrogen
Sodium chlorideNaClSodium + chlorine
Calcium carbonateCaCO₃Calcium + carbon + oxygen
A compound has properties different from the elements from which it is formed. Its elements are not separated by ordinary physical methods.

7. Chemical symbols and formulae

SymbolOne or two letters represent an element. First letter uppercase; second letter, if present, lowercase.
FormulaShows the elements and relative number of atoms in a molecule or formula unit.
Examples: Co = cobalt; CO = carbon monoxide. These are not the same.
FormulaMeaningTotal atoms
H₂O2 H + 1 O3
CO₂1 C + 2 O3
H₂SO₄2 H + 1 S + 4 O7
Ca(OH)₂1 Ca + 2 O + 2 H5

A subscript applies to the symbol immediately before it; a bracket multiplier applies to everything inside the brackets.

8. Atomic mass and molecular mass

Relative atomic mass (Aᵣ): average mass of an atom compared with 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom.
Relative molecular mass (Mᵣ): sum of the relative atomic masses of all atoms in a molecule.
Mᵣ(H₂O) = 2(1) + 16 = 18
Mᵣ(CO₂) = 12 + 2(16) = 44
For ionic compounds such as NaCl, use the term formula mass rather than molecular mass because they form an ionic lattice, not discrete molecules.

9. Atom, molecule and formula unit

TermWhat it representsExample
AtomOne atom of an elementNa, He
MoleculeA bonded group of atoms that exists as a discrete unitO₂, H₂O
Formula unitSimplest whole-number ratio of ions in an ionic solidNaCl, MgO
NaCl is not a discrete molecule: its formula represents a 1:1 ratio of Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions in the crystal lattice.

10. Read a chemical formula

Number of atoms of an element = subscript × any multiplier outside its bracket.
FormulaAtom countTotal atoms
NH₃N = 1; H = 34
Al₂(SO₄)₃Al = 2; S = 3; O = 1217
Ca(OH)₂Ca = 1; O = 2; H = 25
Coefficient vs subscript: 2H₂O means 2 water molecules: 4 hydrogen atoms and 2 oxygen atoms (6 atoms total). The coefficient multiplies the entire formula.

11. Common exam confusions

  • O₂ is a molecule of an element; it is not a compound.
  • H₂O is a molecule of a compound.
  • He is an element that commonly exists as individual atoms.
  • O₃ is ozone: a triatomic molecule and an allotrope of oxygen.
  • NaCl is an ionic compound represented by a formula unit, not a molecule.
  • Co is cobalt; CO is carbon monoxide. Capitalisation changes the meaning.

12. Must-remember facts

  • Atoms are the basic units of elements in chemical reactions.
  • Molecules can contain atoms of the same element or different elements.
  • O₂ is a molecule of an element; H₂O is a molecule of a compound.
  • Ozone (O₃) is an allotrope of oxygen and is triatomic.
  • Compounds have fixed composition; mixtures can have variable composition.
  • Symbols are case-sensitive: Co and CO represent different things.
  • In a chemical formula, subscripts show atom counts; do not confuse them with coefficients.

13. Quick self-check

Q1. Which is a molecule of an element? O₂

Q3. Is water an element or compound? Compound

Q4. How many total atoms are in H₂SO₄? Seven

Q5. Is air a compound? No, it is a mixture
