Key concepts

AtomSmallest unit of an element that takes part in a chemical reaction. Contains protons, neutrons and electrons.
MoleculeTwo or more atoms chemically bonded; may contain same or different elements.
ElementPure substance containing only one type of atom. Examples: Fe, O₂, He.
CompoundDifferent elements chemically combined in a fixed proportion. Examples: H₂O, CO₂, NaCl.

Atomicity & formulae

MonoatomicHe (1 atom)
DiatomicH₂, O₂ (2)
TriatomicO₃ (3)
PolyatomicP₄, S₈ (more than 3)

Formula examples: H₂O = 2 H + 1 O (3 atoms); CO₂ = 1 C + 2 O (3); Ca(OH)₂ = 1 Ca + 2 O + 2 H (5).

Coefficient multiplies the whole formula: 2H₂O = 4 H + 2 O.

Mass & important terms

Molecular mass = sum of the atomic masses of all atoms in a molecule.
H₂O = 2(1) + 16 = 18 u
CO₂ = 12 + 2(16) = 44 u

Formula unit: simplest ion ratio in an ionic solid, e.g. NaCl. It is not a discrete molecule.

Remember for exams

  • O₂ is a molecule of an element, not a compound.
  • H₂O is a compound; O₃ is triatomic ozone.
  • He commonly exists as individual atoms.
  • NaCl is an ionic compound represented by a formula unit.
  • Co = cobalt; CO = carbon monoxide (case matters).
