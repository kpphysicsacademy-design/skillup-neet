🔢 Avogadro Constant

Avogadro constant tells us how many specified particles are present in one mole. Learn the value, use two formulas and calculate particle numbers instantly.

1. Definition

One mole contains exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ specified elementary entities.

Nₐ = 6.02214076 × 10²³ mol⁻¹

Entities may be atoms, molecules, ions, electrons or formula units. Always state which particle is being counted.

2. Two formulas

Number of particles: N = n × Nₐ
Number of moles: n = N ÷ Nₐ

n = amount in moles; N = number of entities; Nₐ = Avogadro constant.

3. Quick examples

1 mol of helium

Contains 6.022 × 10²³ helium atoms.

0.5 mol of water

Molecules = 0.5 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 3.011 × 10²³ molecules.

1 mol of oxygen gas (O₂)

Contains 6.022 × 10²³ O₂ molecules, equal to 1.2044 × 10²⁴ oxygen atoms.

1 mol of calcium chloride (CaCl₂)

Contains 6.022 × 10²³ formula units and 1.2044 × 10²⁴ chloride ions.

4. Remember for exams

  • Unit of Avogadro constant: mol⁻¹.
  • One mole always contains the same number of specified entities.
  • One molecule of O₂ contains two oxygen atoms.
  • One formula unit of CaCl₂ contains two chloride ions.
  • Do not confuse number of molecules with number of atoms.
