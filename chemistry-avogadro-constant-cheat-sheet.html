CHEMISTRY · MOLE CONCEPT

Avogadro Constant

The bridge between the number of particles and the amount of substance. Understand it in seconds, then calculate atoms, molecules, ions or formula units.

Nₐ = 6.02214076 × 10²³ mol⁻¹

One idea to remember

One mole always contains the same number of specified entities. The entity must be named: atoms, molecules, ions, electrons or formula units.

Particles (N) = Moles (n) × Nₐ
Moles (n) = Particles (N) ÷ Nₐ
1 mol6.022 × 10²³ entities
0.5 mol3.011 × 10²³ entities
2 mol1.2044 × 10²⁴ entities

Understand with examples

1 mol of He

6.022 × 10²³ helium atoms. Helium is monatomic.

0.5 mol of H₂O

3.011 × 10²³ water molecules.

1 mol of O₂

6.022 × 10²³ molecules or 1.2044 × 10²⁴ oxygen atoms.

1 mol of CaCl₂

6.022 × 10²³ formula units or 1.2044 × 10²⁴ chloride ions.

Exam method: solve in 3 steps

1

Identify the entity. Is the question asking for molecules, atoms, ions or formula units?

2

Convert using Nₐ. Multiply moles by Nₐ, or divide particles by Nₐ.

3

Apply the atom / ion count. Multiply by the subscript or number of ions in one formula unit when needed.

Remember: Avogadro constant has the unit mol⁻¹. One mole contains a fixed number of specified entities, not a fixed mass or volume.
