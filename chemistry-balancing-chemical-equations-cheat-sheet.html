Balancing Chemical Equations

Make the number of atoms of every element equal on both sides without changing any chemical formula.

1. Golden rule

\[\text{Atoms on reactant side}=\text{Atoms on product side}\]

Change only the coefficients in front of formulas. Never change subscripts.

2. Four simple steps

1Write the correct formulas for reactants and products.
2Count each type of atom on both sides.
3Adjust coefficients to balance one element at a time. Leave H and O towards the end when helpful.
4Recount all atoms and reduce coefficients to the smallest whole-number ratio.

3. Worked example

Balance: Fe + O₂ → Fe₂O₃

Balance oxygen first: use 2Fe₂O₃ to make 6 oxygen atoms, then 3O₂.

Now balance iron with 4Fe.

\[\boxed{4Fe+3O_2\longrightarrow2Fe_2O_3}\]

Check: Fe = 4 on each side; O = 6 on each side.

4. Quick reminders

  • Coefficient 1 is not written.
  • Multiply every coefficient if fractions are used.
  • Use the smallest whole-number coefficients.
  • In hydrocarbon combustion, balance C, then H, then O.
Remember: Balanced equations obey the law of conservation of mass.
