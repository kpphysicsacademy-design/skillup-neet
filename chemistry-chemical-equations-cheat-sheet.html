CHEMISTRY · CHEMICAL REACTIONS

Chemical Equations

Represent chemical reactions with symbols and formulas. Learn how to read, balance and interpret equations.

1. Basic form

\[\text{Reactants}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{Products}\]

Reactants are written on the left; products are written on the right. The arrow means “yields” or “forms”.

\[2H_2+O_2\longrightarrow2H_2O\]

2. How to balance

1Write the correct chemical formulas for all reactants and products.
2Count atoms of each element on both sides.
3Add whole-number coefficients to balance each element.
4Check that all atoms (and total charge, if ionic) balance. Use the smallest whole-number coefficients.

3. Worked example

Balance hydrogen reacting with oxygen to form water.

Unbalanced: H₂ + O₂ → H₂O

Balance oxygen by placing 2 before H₂O, then balance hydrogen by placing 2 before H₂:

\[\boxed{2H_2+O_2\longrightarrow2H_2O}\]

Both sides now contain 4 hydrogen atoms and 2 oxygen atoms.

4. Symbols to remember

  • (s) solid · (l) liquid · (g) gas · (aq) aqueous solution
  • Δ above arrow: heat supplied
  • ↑ gas released · ↓ precipitate formed
  • Coefficients show relative amounts; never change subscripts while balancing.

5. Key rule

Law of conservation of mass: A balanced chemical equation has the same number of atoms of each element on both sides. Therefore, total mass is conserved.
