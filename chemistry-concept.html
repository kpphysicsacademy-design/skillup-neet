Atom, Molecule, Element & Compound
Learn the four basic ideas separately, then compare them. Practice is independent: completing this lesson does not automatically open another concept.

⚛️ 1. Atom

An atom is the smallest unit of an element that retains the chemical identity of that element.

Examples: H, He, Na and Cl represent atoms of hydrogen, helium, sodium and chlorine.
An atom contains a nucleus with protons and neutrons, surrounded by electrons.

🔗 2. Molecule

A molecule is an electrically neutral group of two or more atoms held together by chemical bonds and capable of independent existence.

Examples: H₂, O₂ and H₂O are molecules.
H₂: molecule of an element because both atoms are hydrogen.
H₂O: molecule of a compound because it contains hydrogen and oxygen.

🧩 3. Element

An element is a pure substance consisting of only one kind of atom and cannot be decomposed into simpler substances by ordinary chemical methods.

Examples: H₂, O₂, Fe and Cu are elemental substances.
An element may exist as individual atoms or as molecules containing identical atoms.

🧪 4. Compound

A compound is a pure substance formed when two or more different elements combine chemically in a definite proportion.

Examples: H₂O, CO₂ and NaCl.
A compound has properties different from those of the elements from which it is formed.

🔍 5. Compare them

Atom: one basic unit of an element.
Molecule: bonded atoms forming a discrete neutral unit.
Element: pure substance containing one kind of atom.
Compound: pure substance containing different elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio.

🧠 Fast exam rules

O₂ → element + molecule.
H₂O → compound + molecule.
Na → element + atom.
NaCl → compound; it is an ionic solid rather than a discrete molecule in the solid state.

🎯 Common trap

Do not confuse element with atom. An atom is a particle; an element is a pure substance/category defined by its atomic number.

Do not call every compound a molecule. Ionic compounds such as NaCl form extended ionic lattices in the solid state.
