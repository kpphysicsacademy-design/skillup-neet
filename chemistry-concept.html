Dalton Atomic Theory
Understand the historical atomic model, its main postulates, how it explains chemical combination, and where modern atomic theory differs.

⚛️ 1. Why Dalton proposed an atomic theory

Dalton developed his atomic theory in the early 19th century to explain the laws of chemical combination using the idea that matter is composed of atoms.

Key idea: chemical reactions rearrange atoms rather than creating or destroying them in ordinary chemical processes.

🔹 2. Main postulates

1. Matter is composed of very small particles called atoms.
2. Atoms of the same element were proposed to be identical in mass and properties, while atoms of different elements were proposed to differ.
3. Atoms combine in simple whole-number ratios to form compounds.
4. Atoms are rearranged during chemical reactions; the atoms themselves were not created or destroyed in the reaction.

🧮 3. Connection with chemical combination

Dalton's theory provided an atomic explanation for fixed composition and whole-number combinations.

Compound → atoms of different elements + definite whole-number ratio
Example: Water has hydrogen and oxygen in a definite composition. The molecular formula H₂O shows a 2:1 atom ratio.

📏 4. Connection with multiple proportions

When two elements form more than one compound, the masses of one element that combine with a fixed mass of the other can occur in simple whole-number ratios.

Example: Carbon and oxygen form CO and CO₂. For a fixed mass of carbon, the oxygen masses that combine are in a simple 1:2 ratio.

🔬 5. What modern chemistry changed

Dalton's model was historically important, but some original statements are not fully correct today.

Atoms are divisible: atoms contain subatomic particles such as electrons, protons and neutrons.
Same element can have different masses: isotopes have the same atomic number but different mass numbers.
Nuclear processes: in nuclear reactions, one element can be transformed into another, so the simple “atoms cannot be created or destroyed” statement is limited to ordinary chemical reactions.

🧠 Exam traps

Correct: Dalton's theory explains chemical combination using atoms and whole-number ratios.
Trap: “All atoms of an element have identical mass” is not valid in modern chemistry because isotopes exist.
Trap: Do not confuse Dalton's historical postulates with the modern quantum-mechanical description of atoms.

🎯 Quick recall

Matter → atoms → combination in whole-number ratios → compounds → rearrangement during chemical reactions.
