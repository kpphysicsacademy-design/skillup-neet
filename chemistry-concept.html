Matter and its Nature
Build one focused concept from definition to particles, states, properties and changes. Learn independently, then practice 10 concept-specific MCQs.

🎯 1. What is matter?

Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space (has volume).

Examples: water, air, a book, oxygen, salt and iron.
Light, sound and a shadow are not treated as matter because they do not have mass and occupy space as material substances do.

🔬 2. Matter is particulate

Matter is made of extremely small particles. Depending on the substance, these can be atoms, molecules or ions.

Atom: basic particle of an element that retains its chemical identity.
Molecule: two or more atoms bonded together as a discrete unit.
Ion: a charged atom or group of atoms.

↔️ 3. Particles have spaces

Particles are separated by spaces. These spaces are smallest in closely packed solids and much larger in gases.

Solid: closely packed particles → very low compressibility.
Liquid: particles remain close but can move past one another.
Gas: particles are far apart → high compressibility.

⚡ 4. Particles are always moving

Particles possess kinetic energy and undergo continuous motion. Increasing temperature generally increases their average kinetic energy.

Higher temperature → higher average kinetic energy → generally faster particle motion

🧲 5. Particles attract each other

Particles experience attractive interactions. The effective balance between attraction and particle motion helps explain the behaviour of different states.

Solid: strong effective attraction and restricted movement.
Liquid: particles stay close but can flow.
Gas: particles are widely separated and interactions are much less significant under ordinary conditions.

🧊 6. States of matter

Solid: definite shape + definite volume.
Liquid: no definite shape + definite volume.
Gas: no definite shape + no definite volume.

🧪 7. Physical vs chemical nature

A physical property can be observed or measured without changing chemical identity.

Physical: density, colour, melting point, boiling point.

A chemical property describes the ability to undergo chemical change.

Chemical: flammability, reaction with oxygen, reaction with acids.

🔄 8. Changes in matter

A physical change does not form a new chemical substance. A chemical change forms one or more new substances.

Melting ice: physical change — H₂O remains H₂O.
Burning paper: chemical change — new substances are formed.
