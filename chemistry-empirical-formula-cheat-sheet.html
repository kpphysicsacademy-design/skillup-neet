CHEMISTRY · MOLE CONCEPT

Empirical Formula

The simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound. Use this quick method to solve formula-based questions.

1. Key formulas

From percentage composition:

\[\text{Moles of element}=\frac{\text{Given mass or percentage}}{\text{Atomic mass}}\]
\[\text{Simplest ratio}=\frac{\text{Each mole value}}{\text{Smallest mole value}}\]

To find the molecular formula:

\[n=\frac{\text{Molar mass}}{\text{Empirical formula mass}}\]
\[\text{Molecular formula}=(\text{Empirical formula})_n\]

2. Four quick steps

1Assume 100 g if percentages are given. Then percentage values become grams.
2Divide each element's mass by its atomic mass to get moles.
3Divide all mole values by the smallest value.
4Convert to the smallest whole-number ratio and write the subscripts.

3. Worked example

A compound contains 40% C, 6.67% H and 53.33% O.

Assume 100 g:

C = 40/12 = 3.33 mol
H = 6.67/1 = 6.67 mol
O = 53.33/16 = 3.33 mol

Divide by the smallest (3.33):
\[C:H:O=1:2:1\]
Empirical formula:
\[\boxed{CH_2O}\]

4. Fraction shortcuts

When a ratio is not a whole number, multiply every ratio by:

  • 1.5 = 3/2 → multiply every ratio by 2
  • 1.33 or 1.67 → multiply every ratio by 3
  • 1.25 or 1.75 → multiply every ratio by 4
  • 1.2 or 1.8 → multiply every ratio by 5

Do not round awkward ratios directly; convert them to whole numbers by multiplying the complete ratio.

5. Remember

Empirical formula = simplest ratio. Molecular formula = actual number of atoms in one molecule. They may be the same or different.

\[\text{Glucose: }C_6H_{12}O_6\quad\Rightarrow\quad CH_2O\]
