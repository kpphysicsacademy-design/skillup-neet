⚖️ Limiting Reagent

Quick revision • Formula • 4-step method • Worked examples

CORE IDEA

What is the limiting reagent?

The reactant that is consumed completely first and restricts the amount of product formed is called the limiting reagent. The other reactant(s) are present in excess.

Limiting reagent → controls theoretical yield
MOST IMPORTANT FORMULA

Compare moles with coefficients

\[\frac{\text{Available moles}}{\text{Balanced coefficient}}\]

Calculate this value for every reactant. The smallest value identifies the limiting reagent.

⚠️ Always balance the chemical equation first. Compare mole amounts, not masses directly.
4-STEP METHOD

How to solve any question

1
Balance the chemical equation.
2
Convert each reactant quantity into moles: \(n=\frac{m}{M}\).
3
Divide each available mole value by its balanced coefficient.
4
The smallest ratio is limiting. Use it to calculate theoretical product.
WORKED EXAMPLE 1

Hydrogen and oxygen

For \(2H_2+O_2\rightarrow2H_2O\), suppose 2 mol \(H_2\) and 2 mol \(O_2\) are available.

\(H_2:\;2/2=1\)

\(O_2:\;2/1=2\)

Smallest ratio = 1, so H₂ is the limiting reagent.

2 mol H₂ produces 2 mol H₂O. Oxygen remaining = 1 mol.

WORKED EXAMPLE 2

Nitrogen and hydrogen

For \(N_2+3H_2\rightarrow2NH_3\), 1 mol \(N_2\) and 2 mol \(H_2\) are available.

\(N_2:\;1/1=1\)

\(H_2:\;2/3=0.667\)

Smallest ratio = 0.667, so H₂ is limiting.

Ammonia formed = \(2\times(2/3)=4/3\) mol \(NH_3\).

EXAM QUICK RECALL

Remember these points

Limiting reagentConsumed completely first
Excess reagentRemains after reaction
Theoretical yieldMaximum product possible
Exact proportionNo reactant left over
  • Limiting reagent determines maximum product.
  • Increasing only the excess reagent does not increase theoretical yield.
  • Percentage yield = (actual yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100.
