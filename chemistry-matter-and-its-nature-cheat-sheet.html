Matter and Its Nature

A compact revision sheet for key definitions, classifications, properties, units and formulae.

1. Matter – core idea

Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space.
MassAmount of matter in a body.
VolumeSpace occupied by a body.

2. States of matter

StateShapeVolumeKey feature
SolidFixedFixedParticles closely packed; difficult to compress
LiquidNot fixedFixedFlows; takes container shape
GasNot fixedNot fixedHighly compressible; fills container
Liquids and gases are fluids because they can flow.

3. Pure substances and mixtures

TypeMeaningExample
ElementOne type of atomIron
CompoundElements chemically combined in a fixed ratioWater
Homogeneous mixtureUniform composition throughoutAir; salt solution
Heterogeneous mixtureNon-uniform compositionSand and water
Brass = Copper + Zinc
Bronze = Copper + Tin

Milk is a colloid. Mixtures can generally be separated by physical methods.

4. Particle model and changes of state

SolidParticles close; vibrate in place.
LiquidParticles close; slide past each other.
GasParticles far apart; move freely.
HeatingUsually increases particle motion.
ChangeName
Solid → LiquidMelting
Liquid → GasVaporisation
Gas → LiquidCondensation
Liquid → SolidFreezing
Solid → GasSublimation

A change of state is a physical change; the substance remains the same.

5. Physical vs chemical change

Physical changeNo new substance is formed.
Example: melting ice.
Chemical changeOne or more new substances are formed.
Example: rusting iron.

6. Intensive vs extensive properties

IntensiveExtensive
Independent of amount of substanceDepends on amount of substance

8. Density formula

Density (ρ) = Mass (m) ÷ Volume (V)
ρ = m / V

Density is intensive. Keep units consistent.

Example: 20 g ÷ 5 cm³ = 4 g/cm³.

9. Separation methods

MethodSeparation
FiltrationInsoluble solid from liquid
EvaporationDissolved solid from solution
DistillationSolvent recovery / liquids with different boiling points
Separating funnelImmiscible liquids
ChromatographyComponents of dissolved mixtures

10. Quick recall

  • Matter has mass and occupies space.
  • Solids have fixed shape and volume.
  • Liquids have fixed volume but no fixed shape.
  • Gases have neither fixed shape nor fixed volume.
  • Elements and compounds are pure substances.
  • Homogeneous mixtures are uniform throughout.
  • Mass is extensive; density is intensive.
