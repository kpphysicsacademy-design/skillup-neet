1. What is matter?

Anything that has mass and occupies space.

Matter = Mass + Space

2. Three states of matter

🧱 SolidFixed shape and fixed volume.
💧 LiquidFixed volume; takes the shape of its container.
☁️ GasNo fixed shape or volume; easily compressed.
🌊 FluidLiquids and gases can flow.

3. Pure substances and mixtures

TypeMeaningExample
ElementOne type of atomIron
CompoundElements chemically joined in a fixed ratioWater
Homogeneous mixtureUniform throughoutAir, salt solution
Heterogeneous mixtureNot uniform throughoutSand + water
Brass = Copper + Zinc
Bronze = Copper + Tin

Milk is a colloid. Mixtures can generally be separated by physical methods.

4. Physical or chemical change?

Physical changeNo new substance forms.
Example: melting ice.
Chemical changeNew substance forms.
Example: rusting iron.

5. Intensive and extensive properties

IntensiveDoes not depend on amount.
Density, temperature.
ExtensiveDepends on amount.
Mass, volume.

6. SI units to remember

QuantityUnitSymbol
MassKilogramkg
Amount of substanceMolemol
TemperatureKelvinK
ForceNewtonN

7. Density

Density = Mass ÷ Volume
ρ = m / V

Density is an intensive property. Use matching units for mass and volume.
