Matter and Its Nature

A quick revision sheet covering the key facts and definitions in this concept question bank.

🧠 Start 30-question Quiz

1. What is matter?

Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space.

Matter = has mass + occupies space

Matter can exist in different physical states and undergo physical or chemical changes.

2. Three common states of matter

SolidDefinite shape and volume. Particles are closely packed; intermolecular forces are generally strongest.
LiquidDefinite volume, but no definite shape. Takes the shape of its container and can flow.
GasNo definite shape or volume. Highly compressible because particles are far apart.
FluidLiquids and gases can flow, so both are fluids.

3. Pure substances and mixtures

TypeKey ideaExample
ElementCannot be decomposed into simpler substances by ordinary chemical methodsOne type of atom
CompoundElements chemically combined in a fixed ratioWater
Homogeneous mixtureUniform composition throughoutAir, salt solution, brass
Heterogeneous mixtureNon-uniform composition / distinct partsSand and water
Brass = copper + zinc | Bronze = copper + tin

Mixture components retain their individual properties and can generally be separated by physical methods.

Milk: a colloid; microscopically it is a heterogeneous mixture.

4. Physical change vs chemical change

Physical changeNo new substance is formed. Examples: melting ice, evaporation of water, boiling water, dissolving sugar in water.
Chemical changeNew substance(s) form. Examples: rusting of iron, fermentation / formation of curd from milk, burning methane.
Quick check: Melting and evaporation change the state, not the chemical identity of water. Rusting forms new substances.

5. Intensive and extensive properties

IntensiveExtensive
Does not depend on amount of matterDepends on amount of matter
Density, temperature, pressureMass, volume, amount of substance

1 newton = 1 kg·m·s⁻².

7. Density

Density (ρ) = Mass ÷ Volume → ρ = m / V
  • If mass doubles and volume stays the same, density doubles.
  • Density is an intensive property.
  • Use consistent units when calculating density.

8. Last-minute memory points

  • Liquids and gases are fluids.
  • Gas is highly compressible.
  • Solids generally have the strongest intermolecular forces.
  • Water is a compound; air is a homogeneous mixture.
  • Sand + water is heterogeneous.
  • Milk is a colloid.
  • Mass and volume are extensive; density and temperature are intensive.
  • Mole, kilogram and kelvin are SI base units.
