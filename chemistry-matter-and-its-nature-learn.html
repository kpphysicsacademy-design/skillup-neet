1What is matter?

Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space.

MATTER = MASS + OCCUPIES SPACE

Matter can exist in different physical states and undergo physical or chemical changes.

2Three common states of matter

🧱 SolidDefinite shape and volume. Particles are closely packed; intermolecular forces are generally strongest.
💧 LiquidDefinite volume, but no definite shape. Takes the shape of its container and can flow.
☁️ GasNo definite shape or volume. Highly compressible because particles are far apart.
🌊 FluidLiquids and gases can flow, so both are called fluids.
Remember: Solid → fixed shape and volume. Liquid → fixed volume only. Gas → neither fixed shape nor fixed volume.

3Pure substances and mixtures

TypeKey ideaExample
ElementCannot be decomposed into simpler substances by ordinary chemical methodsOne type of atom
CompoundElements chemically combined in a fixed ratioWater
Homogeneous mixtureUniform composition throughoutAir, salt solution, brass
Heterogeneous mixtureNon-uniform composition / distinct partsSand and water
Brass = Copper + Zinc
Bronze = Copper + Tin

Mixture components retain their individual properties and can generally be separated by physical methods.

Milk: a colloid; microscopically it is a heterogeneous mixture.

4Physical change vs chemical change

🔵 Physical change

No new substance is formed.

Examples: melting ice, evaporation of water, boiling water, dissolving sugar in water.
🟠 Chemical change

New substance(s) form.

Examples: rusting of iron, fermentation / formation of curd from milk, burning methane.
Quick check: Melting and evaporation change the state, not the chemical identity of water. Rusting forms new substances.

7Density

Density (ρ) = Mass ÷ Volume
ρ = m / V
  • If mass doubles and volume stays the same, density doubles.
  • Density is an intensive property.
  • Use consistent units when calculating density.
