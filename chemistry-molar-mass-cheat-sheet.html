Molar Mass

Molar mass connects the mass you measure in grams with the amount of substance in moles. Calculate a compound's molar mass, then convert grams, moles and particles.

M = m ÷ n   |   m = n × M

1. Core idea

Molar mass (M) is the mass of one mole of a specified substance.

Molar mass = mass (g) ÷ amount (mol)

Unit: g mol⁻¹. For a molecular substance, molar mass has the same numerical value as molecular mass in u. For ionic substances, use formula mass.

2. Calculate molar mass from a formula

Add the atomic masses of all atoms in the chemical formula. The calculator accepts element symbols, subscripts, brackets and hydrates.

MOLAR MASS18.015 g/mol

H₂O = 2 × 1.008 + 15.999

Use correct capitalization: Na (sodium), Cl (chlorine), Fe (iron). Radioactive elements use conventional representative mass numbers.

3. Mass ↔ Moles ↔ Particles calculator

CONVERSION RESULT2 mol

n = m ÷ M = 36 ÷ 18

For particle conversions, the result is the number of entities (atoms, molecules, ions or formula units). Nₐ = 6.02214076 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

4. Worked examples

Water · H₂O

2(1.008) + 15.999 = 18.015 g/mol

Carbon dioxide · CO₂

12.011 + 2(15.999) = 44.009 g/mol

Sodium chloride · NaCl

22.990 + 35.45 = 58.44 g/mol

Sulfuric acid · H₂SO₄

2(1.008) + 32.06 + 4(15.999) = 98.072 g/mol

5. Exam method

  1. Write the correct formula and count every atom, including bracket multipliers.
  2. Multiply each atom count by its relative atomic mass.
  3. Add the contributions to get molar mass in g/mol.
  4. Use n = m/M or m = nM for mass–mole conversions.
  5. For particles, multiply moles by Avogadro constant.
