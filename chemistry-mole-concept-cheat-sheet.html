⚛️ Mole Concept

Learn the three essential conversions: mass ↔ moles ↔ particles. Use the calculator to solve your own numerical problems.

1. What is one mole?

One mole contains Avogadro's number of specified particles.

1 mol = 6.022 × 10²³ particles

Particles can be atoms, molecules, ions or formula units.

2. Three formulas to remember

Moles = Mass (g) ÷ Molar mass (g/mol)
Mass (g) = Moles × Molar mass (g/mol)
Particles = Moles × 6.022 × 10²³

3. Quick examples

  • 18 g H₂O ÷ 18 g/mol = 1 mol H₂O.
  • 2 mol CO₂ × 44 g/mol = 88 g CO₂.
  • 0.5 mol × 6.022 × 10²³ = 3.011 × 10²³ molecules.
  • 1 mol O₂ contains 2 mol oxygen atoms.

4. Remember

  • Write the unit in every step.
  • For O₂, 1 molecule contains 2 oxygen atoms.
  • For H₂O, each molecule contains 2 hydrogen atoms and 1 oxygen atom.
  • For ionic compounds, count formula units rather than molecules.
