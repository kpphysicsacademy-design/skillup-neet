⚛️ Mole Concept

Connect microscopic particles with measurable mass. Revise the mole, Avogadro constant, molar mass and the key conversions used in numerical problems.

📌 1. What is one mole?

One mole is the amount of substance containing exactly the Avogadro number of specified elementary entities.

1 mol = 6.02214076 × 10²³ entities

Entities may be atoms, molecules, ions, electrons or formula units. Always identify what the question is counting.

🧮 2. Essential formulas

n = given mass / molar mass
N = n × NA
n = N / NA

n = amount in mol · N = number of entities · Nₐ = Avogadro constant.

⚖️ 3. Molar mass

Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance.

M = mass of 1 mol (g mol⁻¹)
  • H₂O = 18 g mol⁻¹
  • CO₂ = 44 g mol⁻¹
  • O₂ = 32 g mol⁻¹
  • Na = 23 g mol⁻¹

🔬 4. Count atoms in molecules

Multiply the number of molecules by the number of atoms of the required element in each molecule.

Moles of atoms = moles of molecules × subscript

For 1 mol O₂, there are 2 mol of O atoms. For 1 mol H₂O, there are 2 mol H atoms and 1 mol O atoms.

🧠 Worked examples

Example 1 · Moles from mass

Find moles in 36 g of water (M = 18 g mol⁻¹).

n = 36 / 18 = 2 mol

Example 2 · Particles from moles

How many molecules are in 0.5 mol of CO₂?

N = 0.5 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 3.011 × 10²³ molecules.

Example 3 · Mass from moles

Find the mass of 2 mol of CO₂ (M = 44 g mol⁻¹).

Mass = 2 × 44 = 88 g.

Example 4 · Atoms in molecules

How many oxygen atoms are present in 1 mol of O₂?

1 mol O₂ contains 2 mol O atoms = 1.2044 × 10²⁴ O atoms.

🧪 5. Important distinctions

  • 1 mol of O₂ means 6.022 × 10²³ O₂ molecules.
  • 1 mol of O₂ contains 2 mol of oxygen atoms.
  • For ionic compounds, count formula units rather than molecules.
  • Use the molar mass of the complete chemical formula.

🎯 6. Exam quick recall

  • SI unit of amount of substance: mole (mol).
  • Avogadro constant: 6.02214076 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.
  • Always write the entity: atoms, molecules, ions or formula units.
  • Check units before selecting a formula.
  • For gases, use a stated molar volume or gas equation when required; do not assume a volume unless conditions are given.
