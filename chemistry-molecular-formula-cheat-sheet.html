CHEMISTRY · MOLE CONCEPT

Molecular Formula

The molecular formula shows the actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule.

1. Key formula

Find the multiplier first:

\[n=\frac{\text{Molar mass}}{\text{Empirical formula mass}}\]

Then multiply every subscript in the empirical formula by n:

\[\text{Molecular formula}=(\text{Empirical formula})_n\]

2. Three quick steps

1Calculate the empirical formula mass.
2Divide molar mass by empirical formula mass to get n.
3Multiply all empirical subscripts by n.

3. Worked example

Empirical formula = CH₂O; molar mass = 180 g mol⁻¹.

Empirical formula mass = 12 + 2(1) + 16 = 30

\[n=\frac{180}{30}=6\]

Multiply each subscript by 6:

\[\boxed{C_6H_{12}O_6}\]

The molecular formula is glucose.

4. Remember

Empirical formula: simplest whole-number ratio.
Molecular formula: actual number of atoms in one molecule.

If n = 1, both formulas are the same. n must be a positive whole number.
