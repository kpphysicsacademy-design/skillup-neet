📘 What is molecular mass?

Molecular mass is the sum of the atomic masses of all atoms present in one molecule.

Molecular mass = Σ (n × Aᵣ)

Here, n is the number of atoms and Aᵣ is the relative atomic mass. The commonly used unit is unified atomic mass unit (u).

🧭 Three-step method

  1. Write the correct molecular formula.
  2. Multiply each element's atomic mass by its subscript.
  3. Add all the contributions to get the total.
Remember: No subscript means 1 atom. A bracket multiplier applies to every atom inside the brackets.

🧮 Worked examples

Water — H₂O
2(1) + 1(16) = 18 u
Carbon dioxide — CO₂
1(12) + 2(16) = 44 u
Sulfuric acid — H₂SO₄
2(1) + 1(32) + 4(16) = 98 u
Glucose — C₆H₁₂O₆
6(12) + 12(1) + 6(16) = 180 u

🧠 Common atomic masses

H = 1 uC = 12 uN = 14 uO = 16 uNa = 23 uMg = 24 uAl = 27 uS = 32 uCl = 35.5 uCa = 40 u

These are commonly rounded values for practice. Use the values supplied in a question when they are specified.

🎯 Important exam reminders

  • Molecular mass is expressed in u; molar mass is expressed in g mol⁻¹.
  • For a molecular substance, these values have the same numerical value but different units.
  • For ionic compounds such as NaCl and CaCO₃, use the term formula mass.
  • For diatomic molecules such as H₂, N₂ and O₂, multiply the atomic mass by 2.
  • For Mg(OH)₂, the bracket multiplier 2 applies to both O and H.
Exam shortcut: SUBSCRIPT × ATOMIC MASS → ADD ALL CONTRIBUTIONS.
