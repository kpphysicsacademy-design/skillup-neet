Colourful quick notes, worked calculations and an interactive molecular mass simulator.

📘 Definition

Molecular mass is the sum of the atomic masses of all atoms present in one molecule.

Σ (number of atoms × atomic mass)

Unit: unified atomic mass unit (u).

🧭 Three-step method

  1. Write the molecular formula.
  2. Multiply each atomic mass by its subscript.
  3. Add all contributions.
No subscript means one atom. A bracket multiplier applies to every atom inside the brackets.

🧮 Worked examples

H₂O: 2(1) + 16 = 18 u
CO₂: 12 + 2(16) = 44 u
H₂SO₄: 2(1) + 32 + 4(16) = 98 u
C₆H₁₂O₆: 72 + 12 + 96 = 180 u

🧠 Common atomic masses

H = 1C = 12N = 14O = 16Na = 23Mg = 24Al = 27S = 32Cl = 35.5Ca = 40

⚠️ Exam reminders

  • Molecular mass is expressed in u; molar mass is expressed in g mol⁻¹.
  • They have the same numerical value for a molecular substance.
  • For ionic compounds such as NaCl and CaCO₃, use the term formula mass.
  • For O₂, N₂ and H₂, remember each molecule contains two atoms.
Remember: SUBSCRIPT × ATOMIC MASS, then ADD.
