Stoichiometric Calculations

A quick method to convert given quantities into moles, apply equation ratios and calculate the required amount.

1. Essential formulas

\[n=\frac{m}{M}\qquad m=n\times M\]

Convert mass to moles, or moles to mass.

\[\frac{n_A}{a}=\frac{n_B}{b}\]

For aA + bB → products, a and b are the balanced equation coefficients.

\[\%\text{ yield}=\frac{\text{Actual}}{\text{Theoretical}}\times100\]
\[\%\text{ purity}=\frac{\text{Mass of pure substance}}{\text{Mass of sample}}\times100\]

2. Calculation method

1Balance the chemical equation first.
2Convert the known mass into moles using n = m/M.
3Use the coefficient ratio to calculate unknown moles.
4Convert the answer into the required unit (g, mol or particles).

3. Worked example

For 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, calculate the mass of water formed from 16 g O₂ with excess H₂.

Step 1: Moles O₂ = 16/32 = 0.5 mol.

Step 2: O₂ : H₂O = 1 : 2, so H₂O = 1 mol.

Step 3: Mass H₂O = 1 × 18 = 18 g.

\[\boxed{\text{Water formed}=18\;\mathrm{g}}\]

4. Limiting reagent

Convert each reactant to moles, then compare the available mole amounts with the balanced equation ratio. The reactant consumed first is the limiting reagent; it determines the theoretical yield.

Exam reminder: Keep units throughout the calculation and use the balanced equation coefficients—not subscripts—as mole ratios.
