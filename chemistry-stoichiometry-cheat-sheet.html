CHEMISTRY · MOLE CONCEPT

Stoichiometry

Use a balanced chemical equation to calculate the amounts of reactants and products in a reaction.

1. Key formulas

\[n=\frac{m}{M}\]

Moles = mass ÷ molar mass

\[\frac{n_A}{a}=\frac{n_B}{b}\]

For aA + bB → products, use the balanced coefficients as mole ratios.

\[M=\frac{n}{V\;(\mathrm{L})}\]

Molarity = moles of solute ÷ volume of solution in litres.

\[\%\text{ yield}=\frac{\text{Actual yield}}{\text{Theoretical yield}}\times100\]

2. Four quick steps

1Write and balance the chemical equation.
2Convert the given mass or solution data into moles.
3Apply the coefficient mole ratio to find required moles.
4Convert moles into the required mass, volume or particles.

3. Worked example

For 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, calculate water formed from 4 g H₂ with excess oxygen.

Moles of H₂ = 4/2 = 2 mol

H₂ : H₂O = 2 : 2

\[2\;\mathrm{mol\ H_2}\rightarrow2\;\mathrm{mol\ H_2O}\]

Mass of water = 2 × 18 = 36 g.

4. Limiting reagent & yield

  • Limiting reagent: consumed first; determines the maximum product.
  • Excess reagent: remains after the reaction.
  • Theoretical yield: maximum product calculated from the limiting reagent.
  • Actual yield: product obtained experimentally.
Exam tip: Convert each reactant to moles and compare available amounts with the balanced equation ratio to identify the limiting reagent.
