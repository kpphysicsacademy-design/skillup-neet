Circular Arrangements

Learn the round-table rule, explore rotations in a virtual lab, and test your understanding.

1. The key idea

In a line, every position is distinct. Around a round table, rotating everyone together does not create a new arrangement. So fix one person as a reference point and arrange the remaining people.

Circular arrangements of n distinct objects = (n − 1)!
5 people (5 − 1)! = 4! = 24
6 people (6 − 1)! = 5! = 120
n peopleLinear n! ÷ n rotations = (n − 1)!

Important: ordinary round-table seating identifies rotations, but clockwise and anticlockwise orders are still different unless the problem explicitly identifies reflections (as in necklace arrangements).

3. Worked examples

  1. Six friends at a round table: fix one friend; arrange the other five. Answer: 5! = 120.
  2. A and B sit together among six people: treat AB as one block. There are five units around the circle, giving 4! circular orders; A and B can switch places. Answer: 2 × 4! = 48.
  3. A and B do not sit together among six people: total 5! = 120; together = 48. Apart = 120 − 48 = 72.
  4. Necklace with five distinct beads, rotations and reflections equivalent: for distinct beads and n greater than 2, count (n − 1)! / 2 = 4! / 2 = 12.
