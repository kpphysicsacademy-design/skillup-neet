Angle Subtended by a Chord

Brief Class 9 lesson · Understand the circle theorems before practising.

📝 Practise Quiz🧪 Open Simulator

1. What is an angle subtended by a chord?

A chord AB joins two points A and B on a circle. If a point P lies on the circumference, joining P to A and B forms the angle ∠APB subtended by chord AB.

The angle is formed by the two chords PA and PB meeting at P.

2. Angle at the centre and circumference

The angle subtended by a chord at the centre is twice the angle subtended by the same chord at a point on the remaining circumference.
∠AOB = 2 × ∠APB

Here O is the centre. Both angles stand on the same chord AB, with P on the remaining arc.

Example: If ∠AOB = 110°, then ∠APB = 110° ÷ 2 = 55°.

3. Angles in the same segment

Angles subtended by the same chord in the same segment of a circle are equal.
∠APB = ∠AQB

If P and Q lie on the same arc side relative to chord AB, both angles stand on the same chord in the same segment.

4. Angle in a semicircle

The angle subtended by a diameter at any point on the circumference is a right angle.
∠ACB = 90° when AB is a diameter

This follows because the angle at the centre on a diameter is 180°, and the circumference angle is half of it.

5. Cyclic quadrilateral

Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary.
∠A + ∠C = 180°
∠B + ∠D = 180°

A cyclic quadrilateral has all four vertices on the same circle.

Remember these checks

  • Identify the chord and the arc on which each angle stands.
  • The central angle is twice the circumference angle on the same arc.
  • Angles in the same segment are equal.
  • A diameter subtends 90° at the circumference.
  • Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°.
