Area of a Triangle · Brief Learn

Quick Class 9 revision with a diagram, key relationships and a worked example.

Area formula

The area of a triangle is half the product of its base and the perpendicular height corresponding to that base.

\\(\\text{Area}=\\frac{1}{2}\\times b\\times h\\)

Remember: Height is the shortest perpendicular distance from the opposite vertex to the line containing the chosen base. It is not necessarily a sloping side.

Choosing the base and height

  • Any side may be chosen as the base.
  • Use the perpendicular height corresponding to that base.
  • For an obtuse triangle, the perpendicular height may meet the extended base line outside the triangle.
  • Area is measured in square units such as cm² or m².

Connection with a parallelogram

A diagonal divides a parallelogram into two triangles of equal area. Both triangles have the same base length and the same perpendicular height.

\\(A_{\\triangle}=\\frac{1}{2}bh\\qquad A_{\\text{parallelogram}}=bh\\)

Therefore, each triangle has half the area of the parallelogram formed with the same base and height.

Quick worked example

A triangle has base 12 cm and perpendicular height 7 cm. Find its area.

\\(A=\\frac{1}{2}\\times12\\times7=42\\text{ cm}^2\\)

If the area is 42 cm² and the base is 12 cm, then its corresponding height is 7 cm.

How area changes

  • If the base doubles while height stays fixed, area doubles.
  • If both base and height are doubled, area becomes four times as large.
  • Triangles on the same base and between the same parallel lines have equal areas.
