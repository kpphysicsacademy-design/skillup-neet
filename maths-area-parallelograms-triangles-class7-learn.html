Area of Parallelograms and Triangles

Understand how base and perpendicular height determine the area of these two plane figures.

📝 Practice quiz🧮 Open simulator

1. Area of a parallelogram

Imagine cutting a triangular piece from one side of a parallelogram and moving it to the other side. It forms a rectangle of equal area. The rectangle's length is the parallelogram's base and its breadth is the perpendicular height.

Area = base × perpendicular height
A = b × h
Example: Base = 8 cm and height = 5 cm.
Area = 8 × 5 = 40 cm².

2. Area of a triangle

Two congruent copies of a triangle can be arranged to form a parallelogram with the same base and height. One triangle therefore occupies half of that parallelogram's area.

Area = ½ × base × perpendicular height
A = ½bh
Example: Base = 10 cm and height = 6 cm.
Area = ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 cm².

3. Identify the correct height

Height is the shortest perpendicular distance between the chosen base and its opposite parallel side. It meets the base at 90°. A sloping side is not automatically the height.

  • Choose a base.
  • Find the perpendicular distance to the opposite parallel side (or its extension).
  • Use the same length unit for base and height.

4. Rearranging the formulas

Parallelogram: h = A ÷ b
Triangle: h = 2A ÷ b
b = 2A ÷ h

Area is measured in square units such as cm² or m². Convert measurements to the same unit before calculating.

5. Remember

  • Same base and height means a triangle has half the area of a parallelogram.
  • Doubling one dimension while keeping the other fixed doubles the area.
  • Doubling both base and height makes the area four times as large.
  • Area uses square units, not linear or cubic units.
