Core idea

What is the Argand plane?

A complex number z = a + bi is represented by the point (a, b): the real part a lies on the horizontal axis, and the imaginary part b lies on the vertical axis.

\(z=a+bi\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad (a,b)\)

Real axis

Horizontal axis. It shows \(\operatorname{Re}(z)=a\).

Imaginary axis

Vertical axis. It shows \(\operatorname{Im}(z)=b\).

Modulus

Distance from the origin: \(|z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\).

Conjugate

\(\bar z=a-bi\), the reflection of \(z\) across the real axis.

Worked examples

Example 1: \(z=3+2i\) is plotted at \((3,2)\), three units right and two units up.

Example 2: \(z=-2-3i\) is plotted at \((-2,-3)\), in the third quadrant.

Example 3: for \(z=3+4i\), the modulus is \(|z|=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5\).

Axis points do not belong to any quadrant. The origin represents \(z=0\).

Try these investigations
