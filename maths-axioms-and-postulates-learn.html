Axioms and Postulates

Learn the logical building blocks of Euclid’s geometry: definitions, common notions, postulates and theorems.

✏️ Quiz🧮 Simulator
01 • BASIC LANGUAGE

Definitions and undefined terms

A definition explains the meaning of a term. In geometry, point, line and plane are treated as basic undefined terms; other ideas are described using them.

Dimensions: a solid has 3 dimensions, a surface has 2, a line has 1, and a point has 0.
02 • AXIOMS / COMMON NOTIONS

General truths accepted without proof

1. Things equal to the same thing are equal to one another.
2. If equals are added to equals, the wholes are equal.
3. If equals are subtracted from equals, the remainders are equal.
4. Things that coincide are equal.
5. The whole is greater than the part.
6. Things that are double of the same things are equal.
7. Things that are halves of the same thing are equal.

Axioms (common notions) are general principles used across mathematics, not only geometry.

03 • EUCLID’S POSTULATES

Geometry-specific assumptions

1. A straight line may be drawn from any one point to any other point.

2. A terminated straight line can be produced indefinitely in a straight line.

3. A circle can be drawn with any centre and any radius.

4. All right angles are equal to one another.

5. If a transversal makes the interior angles on the same side sum to less than two right angles, the two lines meet on that side when extended.
04 • AXIOM VS POSTULATE

Know the difference

Axiom: a general accepted truth used throughout mathematics.

Postulate: an accepted starting assumption specific to geometry.
Both are accepted without proof within the system. A theorem is different: it must be established by logical reasoning from definitions, axioms, postulates and earlier results.
05 • THEOREM AND REASONING

How a proof is built

Example: If AB = PQ and PQ = XY, then AB = XY.

AB = PQ (given)
PQ = XY (given)
Therefore AB = XY, because things equal to the same thing are equal to one another (Axiom 1).

Quick revision checklist

• Definitions explain terms.
• Axioms are general common notions.
• Postulates are geometry-specific assumptions.
• Euclid listed five postulates.
• Theorems require proof.
• State the axiom or postulate that justifies each proof step.
