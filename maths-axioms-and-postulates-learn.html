01 • FOUNDATIONS

Definitions and basic terms

A definition explains the meaning of a mathematical term. Point, line and plane are treated as basic undefined terms in modern geometry; we understand them through their properties and relationships.

Point

Indicates an exact position; it has no dimensions.

Line

Has length but no breadth; it extends in both directions.

Plane

A flat surface extending in two dimensions.

Solid

A three-dimensional object with length, breadth and height.

02 • EUCLID’S COMMON NOTIONS

Seven axioms

Axioms are general mathematical truths accepted without proof. They are useful beyond geometry.

1
Things equal to the same thing are equal to one another.
Example: if a = c and b = c, then a = b.
2
If equals are added to equals, the wholes are equal.
Example: if a = b, then a + k = b + k.
3
If equals are subtracted from equals, the remainders are equal.
Example: if a = b, then a − k = b − k.
4
Things that coincide with one another are equal.
Figures that exactly cover one another have equal size and shape.
5
The whole is greater than the part.
A complete segment is longer than its proper sub-segment.
6
Doubles of equal things are equal.
If a = b, then 2a = 2b.
7
Halves of equal things are equal.
If a = b, then a/2 = b/2.
03 • GEOMETRY-SPECIFIC ASSUMPTIONS

Euclid’s five postulates

Postulates are accepted starting assumptions specifically about geometry.

1Joining two points

A straight line may be drawn from any one point to any other point.

2Extending a line

A terminated straight line can be produced indefinitely in a straight line.

3Drawing a circle

A circle can be drawn with any centre and any radius.

4Equality of right angles

All right angles are equal to one another; each measures 90°.

5Parallel postulate (angle form)

If a transversal cuts two lines so that the interior angles on the same side add to less than two right angles (180°), the two lines meet on that side when extended.

Remember: a sum below 180° means the lines meet on that side. A sum of 180° is the parallel-line case in the equivalent parallel criterion.
04 • DO NOT CONFUSE

Axiom, postulate and theorem

Axiom

A general accepted truth used throughout mathematics.

Postulate

An accepted assumption specific to geometry.

Definition

Explains the meaning of a mathematical term.

Theorem

A statement established by a logical proof.

05 • APPLY THE LOGIC

Worked example: use Axiom 1

Given:
AB = PQ
PQ = XY

Since AB and XY are both equal to PQ,
AB = XY

Reason: Things equal to the same thing are equal to one another (Axiom 1).
In a proof, write the statement first and then name the reason: given information, definition, axiom, postulate or an already proved theorem.
