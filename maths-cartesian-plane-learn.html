Cartesian Plane

Understand the two perpendicular axes, four quadrants and how an ordered pair locates a point.

Point P = (x, y)
📘 Brief Learn🧪 Simulator

1. What is the Cartesian plane?

Two perpendicular number lines divide a flat plane into four regions. The horizontal line is the x-axis, the vertical line is the y-axis, and their meeting point is the origin O(0,0).

2. Ordered pairs and coordinates

Abscissa (x)First coordinate; right is positive and left is negative.
Ordinate (y)Second coordinate; up is positive and down is negative.
Example: P(3,2) means 3 units right of the y-axis and 2 units above the x-axis. The order matters: (3,2) differs from (2,3).

3. Signs in the four quadrants

Quadrant I(+, +)
Quadrant II(−, +)
Quadrant III(−, −)
Quadrant IV(+, −)

Quadrants are numbered anticlockwise starting from the upper-right region.

4. Points on the axes

x-axis: (a, 0) because y = 0.
y-axis: (0, b) because x = 0.
Origin: (0, 0) lies on both axes.
Points on an axis do not belong to any quadrant.

5. Plot a point in three steps

  1. Start at the origin (0,0).
  2. Move horizontally according to x (right if positive, left if negative).
  3. Move vertically according to y (up if positive, down if negative), then mark the point.
Example: To plot A(−4,3), move 4 units left and 3 units up. A lies in Quadrant II.

6. Reading a graph and distances

Drop perpendiculars from a point to the two axes to read its coordinates. The distance from the y-axis is |x| units; the distance from the x-axis is |y| units.

For B(−5,−2), the distance from the y-axis is 5 units and from the x-axis is 2 units.

7. Check yourself

  1. Which quadrant contains (−2,−6)?
  2. What is the ordinate of (4,−3)?
  3. Does (0,5) lie in a quadrant?

Answers: Quadrant III; −3; no, it lies on the y-axis.

Quick revision

  • x-axis is horizontal; y-axis is vertical.
  • Origin = (0,0).
  • Write x first, then y.
  • Sign pattern: (+,+), (−,+), (−,−), (+,−).
  • Coordinates with a zero lie on an axis.
