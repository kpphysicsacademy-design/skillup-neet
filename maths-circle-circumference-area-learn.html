CLASS 7 · CIRCLES

Circle Circumference and Area

Learn the parts of a circle, distinguish boundary length from surface area, and practise with worked examples.

📝 Practice quiz🧮 Open simulator

1. Radius and diameter

The radius (r) is the distance from the centre to the circle. The diameter (d) passes through the centre and joins two points on the circle.

d = 2r   and   r = d ÷ 2
Example: If radius = 7 cm, diameter = 2 × 7 = 14 cm.

2. Circumference: distance around

Circumference is the length of the circular boundary. The ratio of circumference to diameter is the constant π (pi), approximately 3.14 or 22/7.

C = 2πr = πd
Example: Radius = 7 cm and π = 22/7.
C = 2 × 22/7 × 7 = 44 cm.

3. Area: surface inside

Area measures the surface enclosed by the circle. It depends on the square of the radius.

A = πr²
Example: Radius = 7 cm and π = 22/7.
A = 22/7 × 7 × 7 = 154 cm².

4. Semicircles and quarter-circles

Semicircle area = ½πr²
Quarter-circle area = ¼πr²

For the perimeter of a semicircle, include both the curved half-circumference and the straight diameter.

Example: Semicircle with radius 7 cm: curved arc = πr = 22 cm. Full perimeter = 22 + 14 = 36 cm.

5. How changes in radius affect a circle

  • If radius doubles, circumference doubles.
  • If radius doubles, area becomes four times as large.
  • If radius triples, circumference becomes three times and area becomes nine times.
  • Use linear units (cm, m) for circumference and square units (cm², m²) for area.
