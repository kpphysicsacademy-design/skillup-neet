Line Symmetry

Discover how a shape can be divided into two matching mirror-image halves. Explore symmetry lines in triangles, quadrilaterals and regular polygons.

1. What is a line of symmetry?

A mirror line that makes both halves match

A line of symmetry divides a figure into two parts that exactly overlap when folded along that line. The two parts are mirror images of each other.

Think: Fold a paper square vertically through its centre. If the edges match exactly, the fold is a line of symmetry.

2. Lines of symmetry in common shapes

0 lines

Scalene triangle

All sides are unequal, so no fold makes matching halves.

1 line

Isosceles triangle

The line runs from the top vertex to the midpoint of the base.

3 lines

Equilateral triangle

Each vertex connects to the midpoint of the opposite side.

2 lines

Non-square rectangle

One vertical and one horizontal line through the centre.

4 lines

Square

Vertical, horizontal and both diagonal lines.

∞ lines

Circle

Every diameter is a line of symmetry.

3. Regular polygons

A regular polygon has all sides equal and all interior angles equal. A regular polygon with n sides has n lines of symmetry.

Regular pentagon

5 lines

Regular hexagon

6 lines

Regular octagon

8 lines

Regular decagon

10 lines

4. Symmetry in quadrilaterals

Rhombus (not a square)

2 lines

Both diagonals are lines of symmetry.

General parallelogram

0 lines

A typical slanted parallelogram has no reflection symmetry.

Kite (usual symmetric kite)

1 line

The symmetry line passes through the vertices where the equal pairs of sides meet.

Square

4 lines

Two centre lines and two diagonals.

5. How to test a line

1
Imagine folding the figure along the candidate line.
2
Check whether every point on one side matches a corresponding point on the other side.
3
If the two halves overlap exactly, the line is a line of symmetry.
Remember: A diagonal is not automatically a line of symmetry. It must divide the figure into matching mirror halves.
