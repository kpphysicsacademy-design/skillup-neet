Powers with Integer Exponents

A power is a compact way to write repeated multiplication. Learn the base, exponent, signs and reciprocal meaning step by step.

6 short lessonsWorked examplesClass 7 level

1Understand a power

In a power aⁿ, a is called the base and n is called the exponent or index. For a positive whole-number exponent, multiply the base by itself n times.

3⁴ = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81
3Base — the repeated factor
4Exponent — number of factors

2Positive integer exponents

aⁿ = a × a × … × a

There are n factors of a. For example, 5³ means three factors of 5.

5³ = 5 × 5 × 5 = 125

Do not confuse 5³ with 5 × 3. The exponent tells how many times the base is used as a factor.

3Zero exponent

a⁰ = 1   (a ≠ 0)

Any non-zero number raised to the power zero has a value of 1.

7⁰ = 1

The base must be non-zero for this rule.

4Negative integer exponents

a⁻ⁿ = 1 / aⁿ   (a ≠ 0)

A negative exponent means take the reciprocal of the corresponding positive power. It does not automatically make the value negative.

2⁻³ = 1 / 2³ = 1 / 8

First calculate the positive power, then take its reciprocal.

Remember: the base cannot be zero when the exponent is negative, because division by zero is undefined.

5Negative bases: odd and even powers

(−2)³ = −8Odd exponent: negative result for a negative base.
(−2)⁴ = 16Even exponent: positive result for a negative base.

Each negative factor pairs with another negative factor to make a positive product. An unpaired negative factor leaves the product negative.

6Why parentheses matter

(−3)² = (−3) × (−3) = 9

The negative sign is part of the base, so it is squared too.

−3² = −(3 × 3) = −9

Without parentheses, the exponent applies to 3 first; the negative sign remains outside.

Look for parentheses before deciding whether the negative sign is included in the base.
