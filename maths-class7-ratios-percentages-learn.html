Ratios and Percentages

Brief Learn · Compare quantities, scale ratios and calculate percentages.

1. Understanding a ratio

A ratio compares two or more quantities using the same unit. The order matters: a ratio of 2 red counters to 3 blue counters is 2:3.

a : b = a/b

A ratio can be written with a colon or as a fraction. Keep the quantities in the same order and convert them to the same unit before comparing.

2. Simplifying ratios

Divide every term by the same common factor until the terms have no common factor greater than 1.

Example: 24 : 36
Divide both terms by 12 → 2 : 3

Like fractions, equivalent ratios represent the same comparison.

3. Equivalent ratios and sharing

Multiply or divide every term by the same non-zero number to make an equivalent ratio.

3 : 5 = 6 : 10 = 9 : 15

If the ratio is 4:7 and the second quantity is 28, one part is 28 ÷ 7 = 4. The first quantity is 4 × 4 = 16.

For a total-sharing problem, add the ratio parts first, then divide the total by the sum of the parts.

4. Meaning of percentage

Percent means “per hundred”. A percentage is a fraction with denominator 100.

p% = p/100
25% = 25/100 = 1/4 = 0.25
3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%

Fraction to percentage: multiply by 100%. Decimal to percentage: multiply by 100.

5. Finding a percentage of a quantity

Convert the percentage to a fraction or decimal and multiply by the quantity.

Example: 25% of 40 = 1/4 × 40 = 10
Example: 12.5% of 240 = 1/8 × 240 = 30

6. Percentage increase and decrease

Percentage change = (change ÷ original value) × 100%

Increase: add the calculated increase to the original. Decrease or discount: subtract the decrease from the original.

Increase 200 by 15%: 15% of 200 = 30; new value = 230.
Discount ₹500 by 10%: discount = ₹50; sale price = ₹450.

For reverse percentage, identify what percentage the final value represents. If ₹640 is after a 20% discount, it is 80% of the original: 640 ÷ 0.8 = ₹800.

7. Common mistakes

  • Changing the order of the quantities in a ratio.
  • Adding or subtracting terms instead of dividing by a common factor when simplifying.
  • Comparing quantities measured in different units without converting first.
  • Confusing 0.25 with 0.25%: 0.25 equals 25%.
  • Calculating percentage change using the new value instead of the original value as the base.
  • Subtracting a discount percentage from the price without first calculating its amount.
