CLASS 7 MATHEMATICS · SYMMETRY

Rotational Symmetry

Explore how a shape can turn around a fixed point and still look exactly the same. Learn the centre, angle and order of rotation.

1. What is rotational symmetry?

A shape has rotational symmetry when it fits exactly over its original outline after a rotation through an angle greater than 0° and less than 360°. The turn happens around a fixed point.

Centre of rotation: the fixed point about which the shape turns.

2. Angle of rotation

The smallest positive angle through which a shape turns and first matches its original position is its smallest angle of rotation.

Measure the turn in degrees, in either clockwise or anticlockwise direction.

3. Order and angle are connected

The order tells how many matching positions occur in one complete 360° turn. For a shape with finite rotational order:

Smallest angle = 360° ÷ Order

Equivalently, Order = 360° ÷ smallest angle.

4. Common shapes

  • Square: order 4; smallest angle 90°
  • Rectangle (not square): order 2; 180°
  • Equilateral triangle: order 3; 120°
  • Regular pentagon: order 5; 72°
  • Regular hexagon: order 6; 60°
  • General parallelogram: order 2; 180°

5. Special cases

  • Circle: it matches after every angle of rotation, so it has infinitely many rotational symmetries.
  • A non-square isosceles triangle has order 1: it matches only after a complete 360° turn.
  • Every shape returns to its starting position after 360°.

6. Worked examples

Example A: A regular octagon has 8 equal rotational positions. Smallest angle = 360° ÷ 8 = 45°.
Example B: A figure first matches after 60°. Order = 360° ÷ 60° = 6.
Example C: A design matches at 90°, 180°, 270° and 360°. There are 4 matching positions, so its order is 4.

7. How to find the order

  1. Mark the centre of rotation.
  2. Imagine or perform a steady turn from 0° to 360°.
  3. Count every position where the outline matches exactly, including the final 360° position.
  4. Use 360° divided by the order to find the smallest angle.
