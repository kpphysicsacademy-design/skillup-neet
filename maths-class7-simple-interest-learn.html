Simple Interest

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal. The interest earned each equal time period remains constant when the principal and rate do not change.

1. The four important terms

  • Principal (P): the original money borrowed or invested
  • Rate (R): interest rate per year, written as a percentage
  • Time (T): duration for which money is borrowed or invested
  • Simple Interest (SI): extra money paid or earned on the principal

2. Simple interest formula

SI = (P × R × T) ÷ 100

Use an annual rate with time expressed in years. If time is in months, convert it into years by dividing by 12.

Example: Find SI on ₹2,000 at 5% per year for 3 years.
SI = (2,000 × 5 × 3) ÷ 100 = ₹300.

3. Total amount

Amount = P + SI

If P = ₹2,000 and SI = ₹300, amount = ₹2,300.

4. Convert months

Time in years = months ÷ 12

6 months = ½ year; 9 months = ¾ year; 15 months = 1¼ years.

5. Find the missing value

P = (SI × 100) ÷ (R × T)
R = (SI × 100) ÷ (P × T)
T = (SI × 100) ÷ (P × R)
If ₹1,500 earns ₹225 in 3 years, R = (225 × 100) ÷ (1,500 × 3) = 5% per year.

6. How simple interest changes

  • Double the principal while keeping rate and time fixed: SI doubles.
  • Double the annual rate while keeping principal and time fixed: SI doubles.
  • Double the time while keeping principal and rate fixed: SI doubles.
  • For a fixed principal and rate, interest is the same for every equal year.

7. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Do not use months as years without conversion.
  • Do not add principal and rate; multiply P, R and T.
  • Amount includes the principal as well as the interest.
  • Keep the rate and time units consistent; an annual rate requires time in years.
