Standard Form of Large Numbers

Make very large and very small numbers easier to read using powers of ten. Learn the pattern, follow worked examples, then test your understanding.

1. What is standard form?

a × 10ⁿ   where   1 ≤ a < 10

The first number, a, must be at least 1 but smaller than 10. The exponent n is an integer.

Example: 45,000 = 4.5 × 10⁴. The coefficient is 4.5 and the exponent is 4.

2. Large numbers

Move the decimal point left until one non-zero digit remains before it. Count the places moved; use a positive exponent.

6,300,000 = 6.3 × 10⁶
82,000 = 8.2 × 10⁴

3. Small numbers

Move the decimal point right until the number is between 1 and 10. Count the places moved; use a negative exponent.

0.0047 = 4.7 × 10⁻³
0.00006 = 6 × 10⁻⁵

4. Convert standard form to an ordinary number

1
Look at the exponent of 10.
2
Positive exponent: move the decimal point right. Negative exponent: move it left.
3
Add zeros where needed to keep the place value correct.
3.25 × 10⁴ = 32,500
7.1 × 10⁻³ = 0.0071

5. Compare numbers

When two numbers are in standard form, compare their powers of ten first. The larger exponent usually gives the larger number when both coefficients are between 1 and 10. If exponents match, compare the coefficients.

4.2 × 10⁴ = 42,000
3.9 × 10⁵ = 390,000
So 3.9 × 10⁵ is greater.

6. Common mistakes

Check these before submitting:
  • The coefficient must be at least 1 and less than 10.
  • Large numbers use a positive exponent.
  • Numbers between 0 and 1 use a negative exponent.
  • Count every decimal-place movement carefully.
  • Do not confuse 10⁴ = 10,000 with 10 × 4 = 40.
