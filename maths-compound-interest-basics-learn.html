Compound Interest

Compound interest means you earn interest on your original money and also on the interest added earlier. That is why money can grow faster over time.

1. Know the four important terms

P
Principal: The starting money invested or borrowed. Example: ₹1,000.
R
Rate: Interest percentage per year. Example: 10% per year.
T
Time: How long the money stays invested. Example: 2 years.
A
Amount: Principal plus all interest at the end.

2. Watch the money grow

Suppose ₹1,000 earns 10% compound interest every year.

Year 1
Interest = 10% of ₹1,000 = ₹100
New amount = ₹1,000 + ₹100 = ₹1,100
Year 2
Interest = 10% of ₹1,100 = ₹110
New amount = ₹1,100 + ₹110 = ₹1,210
₹1,000 → ₹1,100 → ₹1,210

Notice: in year 2, interest is calculated on ₹1,100, not just the original ₹1,000.

3. Formula for annual compounding

A = P × (1 + R/100)T

Here, P = principal, R = annual rate in percent, and T = time in years.

Example: P = ₹1,000, R = 10%, T = 2 years
A = 1,000 × (1 + 10/100)²
A = 1,000 × 1.1 × 1.1 = ₹1,210
Compound interest = A − P = 1,210 − 1,000 = ₹210

4. Simple interest vs compound interest

For ₹1,000 at 10% per year over 2 years:

MethodInterestFinal amount
Simple interest₹200₹1,200
Compound interest₹210₹1,210

Remember: Simple interest is calculated on the original principal. Compound interest is calculated on the updated amount after each compounding period.

5. What does compounding frequency mean?

It tells how often interest is added to the balance.

  • Annually — once each year
  • Half-yearly — twice each year
  • Quarterly — four times each year
  • Monthly — twelve times each year
A = P × (1 + R/(100m))mT

In this formula, m is the number of compounding periods per year.

6. Quick self-check

₹500 is invested at 10% compound interest for one year. What is the final amount?

10% of ₹500 = ₹50. Add it to the principal: ₹500 + ₹50 = ₹550.
