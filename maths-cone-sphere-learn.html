CLASS 9 · SURFACE AREAS AND VOLUMES

Cone and Sphere

A quick concept guide with formulas, diagrams and worked examples.

1. Right circular cone

A cone has a circular base, a curved surface, a vertical height (h), radius (r) and slant height (l). For a right circular cone, these form a right triangle.

Slant height \(l=\sqrt{r^2+h^2}\)
Curved surface area (CSA) \(πrl\)
Total surface area (TSA) \(πrl+πr^2=πr(l+r)\)
Volume \(\frac{1}{3}πr^2h\)

2. Sphere

A sphere is a perfectly round three-dimensional solid. Every point on its surface is at the same distance r from its centre.

Surface area \(4πr^2\)
Volume \(\frac{4}{3}πr^3\)
Diameter \(d=2r\)

3. Worked examples

Example 1: A cone has radius 3 cm and height 4 cm. Its slant height is \(\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5\) cm. CSA = \(π×3×5=15π\) cm².

Example 2: A sphere has radius 7 cm. Surface area = \(4π×7^2=196π\) cm². Using \(π=22/7\), this is 616 cm².

Example 3: A cone with r = 3 cm and h = 6 cm has volume \(\frac13π×9×6=18π\) cm³.

4. Remember

  • Use slant height for cone curved surface area, not vertical height.
  • Use vertical height for cone volume.
  • Sphere surface area depends on r²; sphere volume depends on r³.
  • Write square units for area and cubic units for volume.
  • Keep units consistent before substituting values.
