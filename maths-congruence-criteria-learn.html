Congruence Criteria

Brief Learn · Class 9 · Understand when two triangles have exactly the same shape and size.

✏️ Practice Quiz📐 Open Simulator
△ABC ≅ △PQR
A ↔ P   B ↔ Q   C ↔ R

The order of the letters tells you the corresponding vertices. Matching sides and angles must be compared in that same order.

1. SSS — Side, Side, Side

If the three sides of one triangle are respectively equal to the three sides of another triangle, the triangles are congruent.

AB = PQ, BC = QR, AC = PR
∴ △ABC ≅ △PQR (SSS)

2. SAS — Side, Angle, Side

If two sides and the angle included between those sides in one triangle are respectively equal to the corresponding parts of another triangle, the triangles are congruent.

AB = PQ, AC = PR, ∠A = ∠P
∴ △ABC ≅ △PQR (SAS)
Important: the angle must be between the two given sides. Two sides plus a non-included angle is SSA, which is not a general congruence criterion.

3. ASA — Angle, Side, Angle

If two angles and the included side of one triangle are respectively equal to the corresponding two angles and included side of another triangle, the triangles are congruent.

∠A = ∠P, AB = PQ, ∠B = ∠Q
∴ △ABC ≅ △PQR (ASA)

The known side must lie between the two known angles.

4. RHS — Right angle, Hypotenuse, Side

For two right-angled triangles, if the hypotenuse and one corresponding side (leg) are equal, the triangles are congruent.

∠B = ∠Q = 90°
AC = PR (hypotenuse), AB = PQ (side)
∴ △ABC ≅ △PQR (RHS)
RHS applies only when both triangles are right-angled. The hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle.

5. AAS — Angle, Angle, Side

If two corresponding angles and a corresponding non-included side are equal, the triangles are congruent. The third angles are automatically equal because the angles of a triangle add to 180°.

∠A = ∠P, ∠B = ∠Q, AC = PR
∴ △ABC ≅ △PQR (AAS)

AAS is a valid deduction from the angle-sum property and ASA. Learn the four commonly stated Class 9 criteria first: SSS, SAS, ASA and RHS.

What does NOT prove congruence?

AAA

Three equal angles

AAA establishes similarity, not necessarily congruence. The triangles may have different sizes.

SSA

Two sides and a non-included angle

SSA can produce more than one possible triangle, so it is not a general congruence rule.

PERIMETER

Equal perimeter

Equal perimeter alone does not mean that corresponding sides and angles are equal.

CPCT — Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles

Once you have proved that two triangles are congruent, you can conclude that their corresponding sides and angles are equal.

If △ABC ≅ △PQR, then
AB = PQ, BC = QR, AC = PR
∠A = ∠P, ∠B = ∠Q, ∠C = ∠R

How to write a congruence proof

  1. Identify the two triangles to compare.
  2. Write the given equal sides and angles.
  3. Check which valid criterion is satisfied.
  4. Write the congruence statement in correct vertex order.
  5. Use CPCT to prove any remaining equal sides or angles.
