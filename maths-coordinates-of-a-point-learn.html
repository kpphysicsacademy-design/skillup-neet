Coordinates of a Point

Understand how a pair of numbers identifies the exact position of a point on a plane.

P(x, y) = (abscissa, ordinate)
📘 Brief Learn🧪 Simulator

1. Cartesian coordinate system

Two perpendicular number lines form the Cartesian plane. The horizontal number line is the x-axis; the vertical number line is the y-axis. Their intersection is the origin O(0,0).

2. Meaning of coordinates

x-coordinate (abscissa)First number; signed horizontal position measured from the y-axis.
y-coordinate (ordinate)Second number; signed vertical position measured from the x-axis.
Example: P(3,2) is 3 units to the right of the y-axis and 2 units above the x-axis.

3. Read a point from a graph

  1. From the point, trace a perpendicular to the x-axis to read its horizontal coordinate.
  2. Trace a perpendicular to the y-axis to read its vertical coordinate.
  3. Write the result as (x,y), never swapping the order.

The x-coordinate is also called the abscissa; the y-coordinate is called the ordinate.

4. Signs and quadrants

Quadrant I (+,+)Right and above
Quadrant II (−,+)Left and above
Quadrant III (−,−)Left and below
Quadrant IV (+,−)Right and below

A point on an axis is not in any quadrant.

5. Points on axes and origin

x-axis: (a,0), because y=0.
y-axis: (0,b), because x=0.
Origin: (0,0), where both axes meet.

7. Reflections and distance from the axes

Across the x-axis(x, y) becomes (x, −y). The horizontal coordinate stays the same.
Across the y-axis(x, y) becomes (−x, y). The vertical coordinate stays the same.
Across the origin(x, y) becomes (−x, −y), the point directly opposite through O.
Distance from axesDistance from x-axis = |y|; distance from y-axis = |x|.
Example: The reflection of A(−3, 4) across the x-axis is (−3, −4). Its distance from the y-axis is 3 units and from the x-axis is 4 units.

8. Check your understanding

Try 1: A point is 5 units left and 2 units below the origin. Write its ordered pair.
Try 2: Find the reflection of (4, −3) in the y-axis.
Try 3: Find the distances of (−6, 2) from the x-axis and y-axis.

Answers: (−5, −2); (−4, −3); 2 units and 6 units respectively.

9. Why order matters

(4,2) and (2,4) are different points: the first has horizontal position 4 and vertical position 2; the second has horizontal position 2 and vertical position 4. They are equal only when the two coordinates are equal.

Quick revision

  • Always write x first and y second.
  • Abscissa = x-coordinate; ordinate = y-coordinate.
  • Positive x is right; negative x is left.
  • Positive y is up; negative y is down.
  • Zero in one coordinate places the point on an axis.
