Cyclic Quadrilaterals · Brief Learn

Quick theorem revision with a labelled diagram and worked angle examples.

Core property and diagram

A quadrilateral is cyclic when all four vertices lie on the circumference of the same circle. Each side is a chord of that circle.

In the diagram, A, B, C and D lie on one circle; therefore ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral.

Opposite-angle theorem

Either pair of opposite interior angles of a cyclic quadrilateral is supplementary.

∠A + ∠C = 180°
∠B + ∠D = 180°

Worked example: If ∠A = 72°, then ∠C = 180° − 72° = 108°.

Exterior-angle theorem

An exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral is equal to its interior opposite angle.

Exterior angle at A = ∠C

Why? The exterior angle and interior angle A form a linear pair. Since ∠A + ∠C = 180°, the exterior angle (180° − ∠A) equals ∠C.

Converse test

If a pair of opposite angles of a quadrilateral adds up to 180°, then the quadrilateral is cyclic.

∠A + ∠C = 180° ⇒ ABCD is cyclic

Use this test when the question gives angle values but does not state that all four vertices lie on a circle.

Quick worked example

Opposite angles are (2x + 10)° and (3x + 20)°.

2x + 10 + 3x + 20 = 180
5x = 150 ⇒ x = 30

The two angles are 70° and 110°.

Remember

  • All four vertices lie on one circle.
  • Opposite angles add to 180°.
  • An exterior angle equals the opposite interior angle.
  • A supplementary opposite-angle pair proves cyclicity.
