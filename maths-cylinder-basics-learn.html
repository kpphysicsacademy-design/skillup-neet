🥫 Cylinder Basics

Understand radius, diameter, height, curved surface area, total surface area and volume with a simple picture and worked examples.

1. Meet the cylinder

Radius (r): centre to edge of the circular base. Diameter (d): across the circle through its centre. Height (h): distance between the two circular bases.

Diameter = 2 × radius  (d = 2r)
Remember: radius is half the diameter. If diameter = 14 cm, radius = 7 cm.

2. Curved Surface Area (CSA)

Imagine removing the top and bottom of a tin can and unrolling its curved side. It becomes a rectangle. Its width is the circle's circumference and its height is h.

CSA = 2πrh

Use this when you cover only the curved side, like putting a label around a can.

3. Total Surface Area (TSA)

A closed cylinder has one curved surface and two circular ends.

TSA = 2πrh + 2πr²
TSA = 2πr(r + h)

Use TSA when you need the area of the entire outside of a closed cylinder.

4. Volume — space inside

Volume is the space the cylinder can hold. Think about water inside a cylindrical bottle.

Volume = πr²h

Area of circular base (πr²) × height (h). Volume is measured in cubic units such as cm³.

5. Solved examples

Example 1 — Find volume
Radius = 7 cm, height = 10 cm, π = 22/7
V = πr²h
= (22/7) × 7 × 7 × 10
= 1,540 cm³
Example 2 — Find curved surface area
Radius = 7 cm, height = 8 cm
CSA = 2πrh
= 2 × (22/7) × 7 × 8
= 352 cm²

6. Quick formula recap

Circumference = 2πr
CSA = 2πrh
TSA = 2πr(r + h)
Volume = πr²h

Use π = 22/7 or 3.14 as instructed in the question. CSA and TSA use square units; volume uses cubic units.

🎯 Quick check

A cylinder has radius 3 cm and height 5 cm. What is its volume?

V = π × 3² × 5 = 45π cm³ (approximately 141.3 cm³).
