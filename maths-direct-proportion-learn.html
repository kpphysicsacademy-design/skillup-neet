📈 Direct Proportion

Understand how two quantities increase or decrease together in the same ratio.

1. What is direct proportion?

Two quantities are directly proportional when one quantity increases or decreases by a factor and the other changes by the same factor.

y ∝ x   →   y = kx

Here, k is the constant of proportionality.

Example: If one notebook costs ₹15, then 2 notebooks cost ₹30 and 4 notebooks cost ₹60. More notebooks mean proportionally greater cost.

2. The constant ratio

y / x = k

For direct proportion, the ratio between corresponding values remains constant.

If y = 20 when x = 4:
k = 20 ÷ 4 = 5
So the relationship is y = 5x.

3. Solve using the unitary method

Step 1Find the value of one unit.
Step 2Multiply by the required number of units.
4 kg rice costs ₹240.
1 kg costs = 240 ÷ 4 = ₹60
7 kg costs = 60 × 7 = ₹420
💡 First find the value of 1. Then multiply to find the required value.

4. More everyday examples

Distance and time: At a constant speed, more time means more distance.
Quantity and cost: At a fixed unit price, more items mean greater total cost.
Recipe quantities: If the number of people doubles, each ingredient quantity doubles.

5. Direct or not direct?

Check whether the ratio stays constant. Do not confuse direct proportion with inverse proportion.

Direct: More items at the same price per item → greater total cost.
Not direct: More workers doing the same task → less time needed (this is inverse proportion).

6. Quick check

5 pens cost ₹40. What do 8 pens cost at the same rate?
One pen = ₹40 ÷ 5 = ₹8. Eight pens = ₹8 × 8 = ₹64.
