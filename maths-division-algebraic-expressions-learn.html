➗ Division of Algebraic Expressions

Divide coefficients and variables carefully, then simplify the expression.

1. Divide a monomial by a monomial

12x² ÷ 4x = 3x

Divide the numerical coefficients. For the same variable, subtract the exponent in the divisor from the exponent in the dividend.

12x² ÷ 4x = (12 ÷ 4) × (x² ÷ x) = 3 × x = 3x.
💡 For nonzero x, x² ÷ x = x²⁻¹ = x.

2. Divide each term of a polynomial

(12x² + 8x) ÷ 4x = 3x + 2

When every term is divided by one monomial, divide each term separately.

Step 112x² ÷ 4x = 3x
Step 28x ÷ 4x = 2
Step 3Write the quotient: 3x + 2

3. Use the laws of exponents

xᵐ ÷ xⁿ = xᵐ⁻ⁿ   (x ≠ 0)

When dividing powers with the same nonzero base, subtract the exponents.

a⁵ ÷ a² = a⁵⁻² = a³.
18a³b² ÷ 6ab = 3a²b.

4. Factorise before dividing

Sometimes the numerator is a polynomial and the divisor is a bracket. Factorise the numerator, then cancel the common factor, while keeping the restriction that the divisor cannot be zero.

(x² − 9) ÷ (x − 3)
= [(x − 3)(x + 3)] ÷ (x − 3)
= x + 3, provided x ≠ 3.

5. Common mistakes to avoid

• Divide every term, not just the first term.
• Subtract exponents only when the bases are the same.
• Keep the sign of each term.
• State restrictions when cancelling a variable or bracket.

6. Quick check

Simplify (15x² − 10x) ÷ 5x, where x ≠ 0.
15x² ÷ 5x = 3x and −10x ÷ 5x = −2. Answer: 3x − 2.
