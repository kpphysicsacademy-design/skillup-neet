Equations Reducible to Linear Form

Some equations do not look linear at first. Expand brackets, combine like terms, or clear denominators to rewrite them as a simple equation in x.

ax + b = cx + d

1. Expand brackets first

Use the distributive property: multiply the term outside a bracket by every term inside it.

Example: 2(x + 3) = 14
2x + 6 = 14
2x = 8
x = 4

2. Combine like terms

Terms containing x can be combined with other x terms. Constants can be combined with constants. Do not combine an x term with a number-only term.

Example: 2x + 3x = 25
5x = 25
x = 5

3. Clear fractions

When an equation contains fractions, multiply every term on both sides by the least common denominator (LCD). This removes denominators while keeping the equation balanced.

Example: (x + 4)/3 = 5
Multiply both sides by 3: x + 4 = 15
x = 11
Two denominators: (x − 1)/2 = (x + 5)/4
Multiply every term by 4: 2(x − 1) = x + 5
2x − 2 = x + 5
x = 7

4. Handle decimals

You can multiply every term by 10, 100, or another suitable power of 10 to remove terminating decimals, or solve by collecting terms directly.

Example: 0.5x + 2 = 0.25x + 5
0.25x = 3
x = 12

5. A step-by-step strategy

  1. Expand all brackets carefully.
  2. Clear fractions using the least common denominator when helpful.
  3. Remove decimals by scaling all terms if convenient.
  4. Combine like terms on each side.
  5. Collect variable terms on one side and constants on the other.
  6. Divide by the coefficient of x.
  7. Substitute the answer into the original equation to check it.

6. Common mistakes

Incorrect distribution
3(x + 2) = 3x + 6, not 3x + 2.
Partial denominator clearing
Multiply every term on both sides by the LCD.
Sign errors
Keep track of negative signs when expanding or moving terms.
Skipping verification
Check both sides in the original equation.

Quick revision

Expand → clear denominators → simplify → collect like terms → isolate x → verify. The aim is to transform the original equation into a linear equation without changing its solution.
