Equivalent Rational Numbers

Equivalent rational numbers may look different, but they represent exactly the same value. Their simplest forms are equal.

1. Meaning of equivalent rational numbers

Two rational numbers are equivalent when they have the same value. For example, 1/2, 2/4 and 5/10 all represent one-half.

1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 = 4/8
Each fraction reduces to 1/2, so they are equivalent rational numbers.

2. Multiply numerator and denominator

Multiply both numerator and denominator by the same non-zero integer. The value does not change.

a/b = (a × m)/(b × m), where m ≠ 0 and b ≠ 0
Find three fractions equivalent to 4/7:
4/7 = 8/14 = 12/21 = 16/28.

3. Divide by a common factor

You can also divide both numerator and denominator by the same non-zero common divisor. This often produces a simpler equivalent fraction.

18/24 = (18 ÷ 6)/(24 ÷ 6) = 3/4.

4. Negative rational numbers

The sign must be preserved when making an equivalent fraction. Multiplying both terms by a negative integer changes both signs, not the value.

−2/3 = −4/6 = 4/−6 = 6/−9

Multiplying only one term by −1 changes the value to its opposite.

5. Find a missing numerator or denominator

Find the factor used on the known numerator or denominator, then apply the same factor to the other term.

4/7 = x/21. Since 7 × 3 = 21, x = 4 × 3 = 12.

3/4 = 12/x. Since 3 × 4 = 12, x = 4 × 4 = 16.

6. Check equivalence by cross multiplication

For b and d non-zero, a/b = c/d exactly when a × d = b × c.

3/4 and 9/12: 3 × 12 = 36 and 4 × 9 = 36
Therefore, 3/4 = 9/12.

7. Common mistakes

  • Adding the same number to numerator and denominator does not generally preserve value.
  • Multiplying only one term changes the value.
  • Use the same non-zero multiplier on both terms.
  • Keep negative signs consistent.
  • Never use zero as a denominator.
