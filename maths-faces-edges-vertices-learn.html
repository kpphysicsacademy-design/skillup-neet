CLASS 7 MATHEMATICS · 3D SHAPES

Faces, Edges and Vertices

Understand the parts of solid shapes, compare common prisms and pyramids, and use a relationship to check your counts.

1. Faces

A face is a flat surface of a solid. A cube has 6 square faces. A cylinder has 2 flat circular faces and one curved surface; the curved surface is not a flat face.

2. Edges

An edge is a line segment where two flat faces meet. A cube has 12 edges. Curved boundaries are not counted as straight edges in the usual elementary description of polyhedra.

3. Vertices

A vertex (plural: vertices) is a corner where edges meet. A cube has 8 vertices. A cone has one vertex, called its apex.

4. Common solids

  • Cube: 6 faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices
  • Cuboid: 6 faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices
  • Triangular prism: 5 faces, 9 edges, 6 vertices
  • Square-based pyramid: 5 faces, 8 edges, 5 vertices
  • Tetrahedron: 4 faces, 6 edges, 4 vertices

5. Prisms and pyramids

A prism has two identical, parallel polygonal bases. If each base has n sides, it has 2n vertices, 3n edges and n + 2 faces.

A pyramid has one polygonal base and one apex. If its base has n sides, it has n + 1 vertices, 2n edges and n + 1 faces.

Example: A hexagonal prism has 12 vertices, 18 edges and 8 faces. A hexagonal pyramid has 7 vertices, 12 edges and 7 faces.

6. Euler’s formula

For a convex polyhedron, the number of vertices (V), edges (E) and faces (F) are related by:

V − E + F = 2
Check a cube: 8 − 12 + 6 = 2.
Find edges: If V = 10 and F = 7, then 10 − E + 7 = 2, so E = 15.

7. Counting strategy

  1. Look at the solid from different directions.
  2. Count every flat face, including hidden faces.
  3. Trace each distinct edge once.
  4. Count each corner once, even if several edges meet there.
  5. For a convex polyhedron, verify your result with Euler’s formula.
