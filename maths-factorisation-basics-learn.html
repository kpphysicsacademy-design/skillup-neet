Factorisation made simple

Factorisation means breaking an expression into a product of simpler factors. Learn the patterns one by one.

THE BIG IDEA
6x + 12 = 6(x + 2)

Multiplication builds an expression. Factorisation reverses the process and writes it as a product.

1. Take out the common factor

Find the biggest factor shared by every term. Place it outside the bracket.

Example: 8x + 12
1
Common factor of 8 and 12 is 4.
2
Divide each term by 4: 8x ÷ 4 = 2x and 12 ÷ 4 = 3.
3
4(2x + 3)
Check by multiplying back: 4 × 2x + 4 × 3 = 8x + 12.

2. Difference of two squares

When two square terms are separated by a minus sign, use this pattern:

a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b)
Example: x² − 25
x² − 5²
(x − 5)(x + 5)
This pattern is for a difference (minus), not a sum: a² + b² does not factor this way over the real numbers.

3. Perfect-square trinomials

Square of a sum
a² + 2ab + b² = (a + b)²
Square of a difference
a² − 2ab + b² = (a − b)²
Example: x² + 6x + 9
x² + 2(x)(3) + 3²
(x + 3)²

4. Factorise a quadratic

For x² + bx + c, find two numbers that add to b and multiply to c.

Example: x² + 5x + 6
1
Find two numbers with sum 5 and product 6.
2
The numbers are 2 and 3.
3
(x + 2)(x + 3)
Check: (x + 2)(x + 3) = x² + 3x + 2x + 6 = x² + 5x + 6.

5. Quick pattern guide

Common factor
6x + 9 = 3(2x + 3)
Difference of squares
x² − 16 = (x − 4)(x + 4)
Perfect square
x² + 4x + 4 = (x + 2)²
Quadratic
x² + 7x + 12 = (x + 3)(x + 4)
