Applications of Heron's Formula to Quadrilaterals

Class 9 · Brief Learn · Divide, calculate and add.

1. Split the quadrilateral

Draw a diagonal. It divides a quadrilateral into two triangles. Apply Heron's formula to each triangle separately. The diagonal is a shared side and has the same length in both triangles.

2. Heron's formula

For each triangle, calculate its semi-perimeter and then its area.

\(s=\frac{a+b+c}{2}\)
\(A=\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}\)

Use a separate semi-perimeter for each triangle.

3. Quadrilateral area

\(A_{ABCD}=A_{ABC}+A_{ACD}\)

Add the two triangle areas. Do not add the perimeters. The final unit is square units (cm², m², etc.).

4. Worked example

The two triangles have sides (3, 4, 5) cm and (5, 12, 13) cm.

Triangle 1: \(s_1=6\), so \(A_1=\sqrt{6\times3\times2\times1}=6\text{ cm}^2\).

Triangle 2: \(s_2=15\), so \(A_2=\sqrt{15\times10\times3\times2}=30\text{ cm}^2\).

\(A_{ABCD}=6+30=36\text{ cm}^2\)

5. Exam checklist

  • Identify a diagonal and the two triangles.
  • Check the triangle inequality for both sets of sides.
  • Calculate both semi-perimeters separately.
  • Apply Heron's formula twice.
  • Add areas and use square units.

This is the diagonal-splitting method, not a universal formula for every quadrilateral.
