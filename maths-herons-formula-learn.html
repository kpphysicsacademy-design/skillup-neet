Heron's Formula · Brief Learn

Quick Class 9 revision: find a triangle's area using only its three side lengths.

1. Semi-perimeter

For a triangle with side lengths a, b and c, first calculate the perimeter and then take half of it. This half-perimeter is called the semi-perimeter, represented by s.

\(s=\frac{a+b+c}{2}\)

Example: if the sides are 13 cm, 14 cm and 15 cm, then the perimeter is 42 cm and the semi-perimeter is 21 cm.

2. Heron's formula

Once the semi-perimeter is known, substitute s and the three side lengths into Heron's formula to calculate the area.

\(A=\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}\)

Here, A is the area and s is the semi-perimeter. Keep all lengths in the same unit; the resulting area is in square units.

3. Check triangle validity

Three positive lengths form a triangle only when the sum of any two sides is greater than the third side.

\(a+b>c,\quad b+c>a,\quad c+a>b\)

For example, 4 cm, 5 cm and 6 cm can form a triangle, but 2 cm, 3 cm and 6 cm cannot.

4. Worked example

Find the area of a triangle with sides 13 cm, 14 cm and 15 cm.

\(s=\frac{13+14+15}{2}=21\)
\(A=\sqrt{21(21-13)(21-14)(21-15)}\)
\(A=\sqrt{21\times8\times7\times6}=\sqrt{7056}=84\text{ cm}^2\)

So, the triangle's area is 84 cm².

5. Remember

  • Heron's formula is useful when all three side lengths are known.
  • Use the semi-perimeter, not the full perimeter, in the formula.
  • Use consistent length units before substituting.
  • Area is always expressed in square units.
