Intersecting Lines

Understand the four angles formed when two straight lines cross. Use vertically opposite angles and linear pairs to find unknown angles.

✏️ Quiz📐 Simulator
01 • INTERSECTING LINES

Four angles at one point

When two straight lines cross, they form four angles around their common point of intersection, called the vertex.

Angles opposite each other are vertically opposite. Angles next to each other are adjacent.
02 • VERTICALLY OPPOSITE

Opposite angles are equal

∠1 = ∠3   and   ∠2 = ∠4
Example: if ∠1 = 65°, then its vertically opposite angle ∠3 = 65°.

This property follows because each neighbouring pair forms a linear pair and has a sum of 180°.

03 • LINEAR PAIR

Adjacent angles on a straight line

a + b = 180°
Example: if one angle is 65°, the adjacent angle is 180° − 65° = 115°.

A linear pair consists of two adjacent angles whose non-common arms form a straight line.

04 • ANGLES AROUND A POINT

Complete turn

a + b + c + d = 360°
All angles around one point add up to 360°. For two intersecting lines, the four angles therefore total 360°.
05 • WORKED EXAMPLE

Find all four angles

Two lines intersect. One angle is 72°. Find the other three.

Vertically opposite angle = 72°.
Each adjacent angle = 180° − 72° = 108°.
All four: 72°, 108°, 72°, 108°.

Quick revision checklist

• Intersecting lines form four angles.
• Vertically opposite angles are equal.
• A linear pair is adjacent and sums to 180°.
• Adjacent angles share a vertex and one arm.
• Angles around a point sum to 360°.
