⚡ Laws of Exponents

Learn the power rules with short examples. Understand positive, zero and negative exponents before you practise.

1. What is an exponent?

An exponent tells us how many times a base is multiplied by itself. In 2³, the base is 2 and the exponent is 3.

2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8

2. Five important laws

Multiply same basesaᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
2³ × 2² = 2⁵ = 32
Divide same basesaᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ
5⁴ ÷ 5² = 5² = 25
Power of a power(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
(3²)³ = 3⁶ = 729
Zero exponenta⁰ = 1
7⁰ = 1 (when a ≠ 0)
Negative exponenta⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ
2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8

3. Negative exponent made easy

A negative exponent does not make the answer negative. It tells you to take the reciprocal.

2⁻¹Reciprocal of 2 = 1/2
5⁻²1/(5 × 5) = 1/25
(1/3)⁻²Invert first: 3² = 9
1/7³Write as 7⁻³
💡 Remember: negative power = reciprocal. Keep the base non-zero.

4. Quick worked examples

Example A: 3⁻² × 3⁵
Same base: add powers → −2 + 5 = 3
Answer: 3³ = 27
Example B: 10⁻³ ÷ 10⁻⁵
Same base: subtract powers → −3 − (−5) = 2
Answer: 10² = 100
Example C: (a⁻²)³
Multiply powers → −2 × 3 = −6
Answer: a⁻⁶ = 1/a⁶

5. Check your understanding

What is the value of 4⁻²?
4⁻² = 1/4² = 1/16
