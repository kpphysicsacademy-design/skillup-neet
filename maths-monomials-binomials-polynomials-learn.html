Monomials, Binomials and Polynomials

Learn how algebraic expressions are built, classified and simplified.

1. What is an algebraic expression?

An algebraic expression combines numbers, variables and mathematical operations. For example:

4x² − 3x + 7

The parts separated by + or − signs are called terms.

Terms: 4x², −3x and 7
Variable: x
Coefficients: 4 and −3
Constant term: 7

2. Classify by number of terms

1
Monomial — one term
7x²
Examples: 5, −3a, 6xy
2
Binomial — two terms
x² + 4
Examples: a + b, 3x − 2
3
Trinomial — three terms
x² + 3x + 2
A polynomial can have one, two, three or more terms.

3. Coefficient and constant

The coefficient is the numerical factor multiplying a variable term. The constant has no variable.

In −8x² + 3x − 5:
Coefficient of x² = −8
Coefficient of x = 3
Constant term = −5

4. Understand degree

For a one-variable polynomial, its degree is the greatest exponent of the variable with a non-zero coefficient.

5x⁴ + 2x² − x + 9

The degree is 4 because the highest power of x is 4.

For a term with more than one variable, add the exponents to get its total degree. Example: 6a²b³ has total degree 2 + 3 = 5.

5. Like terms

Like terms have exactly the same variables raised to the same powers. Their coefficients may be different.

3x² + 5x² = 8x²
Like: 3ab and −7ab
Unlike: 2x and 2x²

6. Add and subtract like terms

Combine the coefficients and keep the variable part unchanged.

3x + 5x − 2x = 6x
For subtraction, distribute the minus sign across the second expression:
(5x + 3) − (2x + 1) = 5x + 3 − 2x − 1 = 3x + 2

7. Evaluate a polynomial

Replace each variable with the given number, then calculate using the order of operations.

P(x) = 2x² − 3x + 1
When x = 2:
P(2) = 2(2²) − 3(2) + 1
= 8 − 6 + 1 = 3

Remember these points

  1. Count terms to classify an expression.
  2. Keep the sign with each term.
  3. Combine only like terms.
  4. The degree is the highest relevant exponent (or total exponent sum for a multivariable term).
  5. Substitute values carefully when evaluating.
