Polynomials made simple

Understand the idea in four small steps. Read the examples, then try the quick questions.

ONE EXAMPLE TELLS THE STORY
4x² − 3x + 7

This expression has 3 terms: 4x², −3x and +7. The highest power of x is 2, so its degree is 2.

1. Count the terms

1 TERM
5x
MonomialMono = one
2 TERMS
x + 4
BinomialBi = two
3 TERMS
x² + 2x + 1
TrinomialTri = three
ONE OR MORE
x³ + x + 6
PolynomialGeneral name
Tip: Count terms after simplifying. For example, 2x + 3x = 5x, which is one term.

2. Know the parts

6x² − 4x + 9
1
Variable: x — the letter that can change.
2
Coefficient: 6 and −4 — the numbers multiplying the variable terms.
3
Constant: 9 — the number without a variable.
4
Degree: 2 — the highest exponent of x.
Remember: The coefficient of x, when no number is written, is 1. The coefficient of −x is −1.

3. Combine like terms

Like terms have the same variables with the same powers. Add or subtract only their coefficients.

LIKE TERMS
3x² + 5x² = 8x²
Same power → combine.
SIMPLIFY STEP BY STEP
4x + 3 − 2x + 5
=(4x − 2x) + (3 + 5)
= 2x + 8
Do not combine unlike terms: 2x + 3 is not 5x. Also, x and x² are unlike terms.

4. Substitute and find the value

Replace the variable with the given number. Then calculate powers first.

Example: Find P(2)
P(x) = 2x² + 3
Put x = 2:
P(2) = 2 × 2² + 3
= 2 × 4 + 3
= 11
