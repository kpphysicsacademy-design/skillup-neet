🔢 Number Systems

Explore natural numbers, whole numbers, integers, rational numbers, irrational numbers and the real-number system. Learn how the sets fit together and how to classify examples.

1. Natural numbers (N)

Natural numbers are counting numbers. In this lesson, we start from 1.

N = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, …}
Examples: 6 students, 12 books, 100 steps. Zero, negative numbers and fractions are not natural numbers under this convention.

2. Whole numbers (W)

Whole numbers are natural numbers together with zero.

W = {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, …}
Every natural number is whole, but zero is whole and not natural in this convention.

3. Integers (Z)

Integers include negative whole numbers, zero and positive whole numbers.

Z = {…, −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, …}
Examples: −8, 0, 14. Numbers such as 2.5 and 3/4 are not integers.

4. Rational numbers (Q)

A rational number can be written as p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.

Q = {p/q : p, q ∈ Z; q ≠ 0}
Examples: 3/5, −7/4, 0 (= 0/1), 6 (= 6/1), 0.25 (= 1/4) and 0.333… (= 1/3).

Decimal clue: A rational decimal either terminates or eventually repeats.

5. Irrational numbers

An irrational number cannot be written as a ratio of two integers. Its decimal expansion is non-terminating and non-repeating.

Examples: √2, √3, √5, π
√9 = 3 is rational because 9 is a perfect square. But √7 is irrational because 7 is not a perfect square.

6. Real numbers (R)

All rational and irrational numbers together form the real numbers.

R = Q ∪ (irrational numbers)

Natural ⊂ Whole ⊂ Integers ⊂ Rational ⊂ Real. Irrational numbers are also real, but they are not rational.

🧠 Remember these exam points

  • Every natural number is an integer, and every integer is rational.
  • Every terminating or repeating decimal is rational.
  • A non-terminating, non-repeating decimal is irrational.
  • The square root of a positive integer is rational when that integer is a perfect square.
  • Rational and irrational numbers are both real numbers.
