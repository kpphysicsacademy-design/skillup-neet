Parallel Lines and Transversal

Learn how a transversal creates angle pairs, identify equal and supplementary angles, and test whether two lines are parallel.

✏️ Quiz📐 Simulator
01 • KEY IDEA

Parallel lines and a transversal

A transversal is a line that intersects two or more lines at distinct points. When it cuts two parallel lines, special angle relationships appear.

The symbol l ∥ m means line l is parallel to line m.
02 • EQUAL ANGLE PAIRS

Corresponding angles

They occupy the same relative position at the two intersections.

Corresponding angles are equal
Example: if one corresponding angle is 68°, the other is 68°.

Alternate interior angles

They lie between the parallel lines and on opposite sides of the transversal.

Alternate interior angles are equal
Example: 112° and 112°.

Alternate exterior angles

They lie outside the parallel lines and on opposite sides of the transversal. They are equal.

03 • SUPPLEMENTARY PAIRS

Co-interior (same-side interior) angles

They lie between the parallel lines and on the same side of the transversal.

Co-interior angles add to 180°
Example: if one is 125°, the other is 180° − 125° = 55°.

Linear pair and vertically opposite angles

A linear pair adds to 180°. Vertically opposite angles are equal.
04 • CONVERSE TESTS

How to prove lines are parallel

• If a pair of corresponding angles is equal, the lines are parallel.
• If a pair of alternate interior angles is equal, the lines are parallel.
• If a pair of co-interior angles adds to 180°, the lines are parallel.
05 • WORKED EXAMPLE

Find x using corresponding angles

Suppose corresponding angles are (3x + 15)° and (5x − 25)°. For parallel lines they are equal:

3x + 15 = 5x − 25
40 = 2x
x = 20
Each angle = 3(20) + 15 = 75°.

Quick revision checklist

• Corresponding → equal
• Alternate interior → equal
• Alternate exterior → equal
• Co-interior → sum 180°
• Linear pair → sum 180°
• Vertically opposite → equal
• Converse tests let you prove lines are parallel.
