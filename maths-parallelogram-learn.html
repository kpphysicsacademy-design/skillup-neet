Parallelogram Theorems · Brief Learn

Quick theorem revision with mathematical notation.

Core properties

For parallelogram (ABCD), opposite sides are parallel and equal, opposite angles are equal, and consecutive angles are supplementary.

\(AB\parallel CD,\quad AD\parallel BC\)
\(AB=CD,\quad AD=BC\)
\(\angle A=\angle C,\quad \angle B=\angle D\)
\(\angle A+\angle B=180^\circ\)

Diagonal theorem

The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other. If (AC\cap BD=O), then:

\(AO=OC,\quad BO=OD\)

Each diagonal divides the parallelogram into two congruent triangles.

Converse tests

A quadrilateral is a parallelogram if any one of these conditions is established:

  • Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
  • Both pairs of opposite sides are equal.
  • One pair of opposite sides is both equal and parallel.
  • The diagonals bisect each other.
  • Both pairs of opposite angles are equal.

Quick worked example

If consecutive angles are ( (3x+10)^\circ ) and ( (2x+20)^\circ ), use their supplementary relationship:

\(3x+10+2x+20=180\)
\(5x=150\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=30\)

The angles are (100^\circ) and (80^\circ).
