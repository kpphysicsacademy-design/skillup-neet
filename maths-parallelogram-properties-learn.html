Parallelogram Properties

A parallelogram is a quadrilateral in which both pairs of opposite sides are parallel. Explore its side, angle and diagonal relationships, then apply them to numerical problems.

Sum of interior angles = 360°

1. Opposite sides

Parallel
Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
Equal
Opposite sides have equal lengths.
If AB = 3x + 1 and CD = 5x − 9, then AB = CD. So 3x + 1 = 5x − 9, giving x = 5.

2. Opposite and adjacent angles

Opposite angles are equal
Adjacent angles add to 180°
If angle A = 110°, then angle C = 110° and angles B and D are 70° each.

3. Diagonals

The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other. Their intersection is the midpoint of each diagonal. They are not necessarily equal or perpendicular.

If diagonals AC and BD meet at O and AO = 7.5 cm, then OC = 7.5 cm and AC = 15 cm.

4. Perimeter

P = 2(a + b)

Here a and b are the lengths of adjacent sides. Opposite sides are equal, so the perimeter is twice the sum of two adjacent sides.

If adjacent sides are 8 cm and 15 cm, P = 2(8 + 15) = 46 cm.

5. Area

Area = base × perpendicular height

Use the perpendicular height, not the slant side, when calculating area. The height is the shortest perpendicular distance between the pair of parallel bases.

For base 12 cm and perpendicular height 7 cm, area = 12 × 7 = 84 cm².

6. Conditions for special parallelograms

Rectangle
A parallelogram with one right angle has four right angles. Its diagonals are equal.
Rhombus
A parallelogram with equal adjacent sides has all four sides equal. Its diagonals are perpendicular.
Square
A square is both a rectangle and a rhombus.
General case
Diagonals bisect each other, but need not be equal or perpendicular.

7. Common mistakes

  • Do not assume adjacent sides are equal.
  • Do not assume every angle is 90°.
  • Do not assume diagonals are equal or perpendicular.
  • Use perpendicular height, not slant side, for area.
  • Opposite angles are equal; adjacent angles are supplementary.
