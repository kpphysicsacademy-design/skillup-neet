Binomial Coefficients

\((x+y)^n=\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{n}\binom nr x^{n-r}y^r\)

1. The core idea

A binomial coefficient counts how many ways we can choose r objects from n distinct objects. It is written as \(\binom nr\) or \({}^nC_r\).

\(\displaystyle\binom nr=\frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!},\quad 0\le r\le n\)

Here, \(n! = n(n-1)(n-2)\cdots 2\cdot1\), and \(0!=1\).

Remember: In the expansion of \((x+y)^n\), the coefficient of \(x^{n-r}y^r\) is \(\binom nr\).

Important properties

  • End terms: \(\binom n0=\binom nn=1\).
  • Choose one: \(\binom n1=\binom n{n-1}=n\).
  • Symmetry: \(\binom nr=\binom n{n-r}\).
  • Pascal identity: \(\binom nr+\binom n{r+1}=\binom{n+1}{r+1}\).
  • Sum of a row: \(\sum_{r=0}^n\binom nr=2^n\).

Pascal’s triangle

Each interior number is the sum of the two numbers immediately above it.

2. Worked examples

Example A · Calculate \(\binom 62\)
\(\binom 62=\frac{6!}{2!4!}=\frac{6\times5}{2\times1}=15\)

There are 15 ways to choose two objects from six.

Example B · Find a coefficient

Find the coefficient of \(x^3y^2\) in \((x+y)^5\).

\(r=2\Rightarrow \binom52=\frac{5!}{2!3!}=10\)

The coefficient is 10.

Example C · Use symmetry

Find \(\binom 98\) without expanding large factorials.

\(\binom98=\binom91=9\)

Choosing eight objects to keep is equivalent to choosing one object to leave out.

Example D · Sum of coefficients

The sum of the coefficients in \((x+1)^6\) is obtained by setting \(x=1\):

\((1+1)^6=2^6=64\)

QUICK RECAP

Three things to know

  1. Use factorials to calculate \(\binom nr\).
  2. Use symmetry to simplify \(\binom nr\).
  3. Match the exponent of y to r in a binomial expansion.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing \(r\) with \(n-r\) in the term.
  • Forgetting that \(0!=1\).
  • Assuming coefficients are always increasing; the row is symmetric.
  • Mixing up a coefficient with the full term containing variables.
