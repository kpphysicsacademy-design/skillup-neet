Perfect Cubes

A perfect cube is a number formed by multiplying an integer by itself three times.

n³ = n × n × n

1. Cube numbers

Multiply a number by itself three times to find its cube.

1³=1 · 2³=8 · 3³=27 · 4³=64 · 5³=125

The first positive perfect cubes are 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343, 512, 729 and 1000.

2. Cube roots

The cube root reverses cubing. It is the number which, when cubed, gives the original value.

∛125 = 5 because 5³ = 125

Unlike square roots, cube roots of negative numbers are real: ∛(−8) = −2.

3. Prime factorisation

For a perfect cube, each prime factor appears in a group of three (its exponent is a multiple of 3).

216 = 2³ × 3³, so ∛216 = 2 × 3 = 6

4. Recognising perfect cubes

  • Compare the number with nearby cube numbers.
  • In prime factorisation, every exponent must be divisible by 3.
  • A negative integer can also be a perfect cube, such as −27 = (−3)³.
  • The last digit alone cannot prove that a number is a perfect cube.

5. Cube patterns and properties

  • The cube of an even integer is even.
  • The cube of an odd integer is odd.
  • The cube of a negative integer is negative.
  • The cube of a product is the product of the cubes: (ab)³ = a³b³.
  • Consecutive cube differences are (n+1)³ − n³ = 3n² + 3n + 1.

6. Worked examples

  1. ∛343 = 7 because 7³ = 343.
  2. Is 100 a perfect cube? No; it lies between 4³ = 64 and 5³ = 125.
  3. ∛(−64) = −4.
  4. ∛1728: 1728 = 2⁶ × 3³, so the cube root is 2² × 3 = 12.
