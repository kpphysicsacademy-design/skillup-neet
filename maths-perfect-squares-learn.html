Perfect Squares

A perfect square is a number that can be written as an integer multiplied by itself.

n² = n × n

1. Square numbers

Multiply a whole number by itself to get its square.

1² = 1 · 2² = 4 · 3² = 9 · 4² = 16 · 5² = 25

The sequence of square numbers begins 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, …

2. Square roots

The square root reverses squaring. The principal square root is the non-negative value.

√49 = 7 because 7 × 7 = 49

For example, both 7² and (−7)² equal 49, but the principal square root √49 is 7.

3. How to recognise perfect squares

  • Check whether the number is the product of an integer with itself.
  • Compare it with nearby square numbers.
  • Use the unit digit as a quick filter: a perfect square in base ten can end only in 0, 1, 4, 5, 6 or 9.
  • A square number has an odd number of positive factors.

A last digit test can rule out some numbers, but it cannot prove that a number is a perfect square.

4. Patterns in square numbers

The difference between consecutive squares increases by 2 each time.

(n + 1)² − n² = 2n + 1

Example: 25 − 16 = 9, and 36 − 25 = 11. These differences are consecutive odd numbers.

5. Useful properties

  • The square of an even integer is even.
  • The square of an odd integer is odd.
  • The square of a prime number has exactly three positive factors: 1, the prime and its square.
  • Multiplying a perfect square by another perfect square gives a perfect square.
  • The square of a number ending in 5 ends in 25.

6. Worked examples

  1. Is 81 a perfect square? Yes, because 9 × 9 = 81.
  2. Find √144: since 12 × 12 = 144, √144 = 12.
  3. Find the next square after 64: 9² = 81.
  4. Is 50 a perfect square? No; it lies between 7² = 49 and 8² = 64.
