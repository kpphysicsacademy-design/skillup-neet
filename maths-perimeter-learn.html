Perimeter

Perimeter is the total distance around the outside boundary of a closed 2D shape. Imagine walking once around its edge: the distance travelled is its perimeter.

🧮 Explore Simulator📝 Practise 30 questions

1. Square

A square has four equal sides. Add all four side lengths.

P = 4 × side
Example: side = 5 cm
P = 4 × 5 = 20 cm

2. Rectangle

A rectangle has two equal lengths and two equal widths.

P = 2 × (length + width)
Example: length = 8 cm, width = 3 cm
P = 2 × (8 + 3) = 22 cm

3. Triangle

The perimeter of any triangle is the sum of its three side lengths.

P = a + b + c
Example: sides = 6 cm, 7 cm and 9 cm
P = 6 + 7 + 9 = 22 cm

4. Regular polygon

A regular polygon has equal sides. Multiply one side length by the number of sides.

P = number of sides × side
Example: regular pentagon, side = 4 cm
P = 5 × 4 = 20 cm

5. Find a missing side

If the perimeter and all other boundary sides are known, subtract their total from the perimeter.

Missing side = P − sum of known sides
Example: P = 34 cm; known sides = 10 cm, 10 cm and 7 cm
Missing side = 34 − 27 = 7 cm

6. Perimeter in real life

Perimeter helps calculate fencing, borders, frame lengths and walking distances around a field.

Fencing needed = boundary perimeter − gate opening
Example: boundary = 40 m, gate = 2 m
Fencing needed = 40 − 2 = 38 m

Remember the units

Perimeter measures length, so use units such as mm, cm, m or km. Convert all measurements to the same unit before adding them. Do not use square units (cm²) for perimeter.

Quick check: Trace only the outside boundary of the shape. Add each outside side once, and do not include interior lines.
