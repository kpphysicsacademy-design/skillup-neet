Pie Charts

A pie chart (circle graph) shows how a whole is divided among categories. Each category is represented by a sector whose central angle is proportional to its share of the total.

Full circle = 360° = 100%

1. Sector angle and percentage

The sector angle is proportional to the category's frequency or percentage.

Sector angle = (category value ÷ total) × 360°
Percentage = (sector angle ÷ 360°) × 100

Example: 25% of a dataset occupies 0.25 × 360° = 90°.

2. Convert an angle to a fraction

Divide the sector angle by 360° and simplify the fraction.

Fraction = sector angle ÷ 360°

Example: 72° ÷ 360° = 1/5 of the whole.

3. Find the number of observations

When the total number of observations is known, multiply the sector's fraction by the total.

Category count = (sector angle ÷ 360°) × total

Example: In a survey of 60 people, a 120° sector represents (120 ÷ 360) × 60 = 20 people.

4. Find a missing sector angle

All sectors together form one complete circle, so their angles add to 360°.

Missing angle = 360° − sum of known angles

Example: If three sectors are 80°, 110° and 95°, the fourth is 360° − 285° = 75°.

5. Worked examples

  1. 10% of a circle: 0.10 × 360° = 36°.
  2. 45° as a percentage: 45 ÷ 360 × 100 = 12.5%.
  3. 30 of 80 responses: 30 ÷ 80 × 360° = 135°.
  4. A 54° sector in a chart of 200 responses: 54 ÷ 360 × 200 = 30 responses.

6. Remember

  • A full circle is 360° and represents 100%.
  • All sector angles add to 360°.
  • A larger sector represents a larger share of the total.
  • Use the total count when converting a sector angle into observations.
  • Check that calculated sector angles sum to 360°.
