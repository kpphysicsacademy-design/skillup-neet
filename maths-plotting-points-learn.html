Plotting Points on the Cartesian Plane

Use an ordered pair to locate a point accurately. Always read the horizontal coordinate first and the vertical coordinate second.

(x, y) = (horizontal, vertical)

1. Axes and origin

The horizontal number line is the x-axis. The vertical number line is the y-axis. They meet at the origin O(0, 0), dividing the plane into four quadrants.

2. How to plot an ordered pair

  1. Start at the origin (0, 0).
  2. Move horizontally by the x-coordinate: right if positive, left if negative.
  3. From there, move vertically by the y-coordinate: up if positive, down if negative.
  4. Mark and label the point.
Example: To plot (−3, 2), move 3 units left and then 2 units up. The point lies in Quadrant II.

3. The four quadrants

Quadrant I
(+, +) right and up
Quadrant II
(−, +) left and up
Quadrant III
(−, −) left and down
Quadrant IV
(+, −) right and down

Quadrants are numbered anticlockwise, starting at the top-right. A point on either axis is not in any quadrant.

4. Points on the axes

On the x-axis: y = 0, such as (4, 0) or (−2, 0).
On the y-axis: x = 0, such as (0, 3) or (0, −5).
Origin: (0, 0), where both axes meet.

5. Order matters

The first coordinate gives horizontal position; the second gives vertical position. Therefore (4, 2) and (2, 4) are different points.

For (4, 2), move 4 right and 2 up. For (2, 4), move 2 right and 4 up.

Quick revision

  • Always write coordinates as (x, y).
  • Positive x: right; negative x: left.
  • Positive y: up; negative y: down.
  • Points on axes have at least one coordinate equal to zero.
  • Check the signs to identify the quadrant.
