Plotting Points on the Cartesian Plane

Locate any point from its ordered pair. Remember: x first, then y.

P(x, y) = horizontal, then vertical
📘 Brief Learn🧪 Simulator

1. Axes, origin and ordered pairs

The horizontal line is the x-axis; the vertical line is the y-axis. They meet at the origin O(0,0). In (x,y), x is the abscissa (horizontal coordinate) and y is the ordinate (vertical coordinate).

2. Four steps to plot a point

  1. Draw the axes and choose an equal scale on both axes.
  2. Start at the origin (0,0).
  3. Move horizontally: right for positive x, left for negative x.
  4. Move vertically: up for positive y, down for negative y. Mark and label the point.
Example: P(−3, 2)
Move 3 units left, then 2 units up. The point is in Quadrant II.

3. Direction and quadrant guide

Quadrant I (+,+)Right and up
Quadrant II (−,+)Left and up
Quadrant III (−,−)Left and down
Quadrant IV (+,−)Right and down

Quadrants are numbered anticlockwise from the top-right. Points on axes are not in any quadrant.

4. Points on the axes

x-axis: y = 0, e.g. (−4,0).
y-axis: x = 0, e.g. (0,5).
Origin: (0,0), where both axes meet.

5. Order matters

(4,2) and (2,4) are different points. The first number always controls horizontal movement; the second controls vertical movement.

Quick revision

  • Write coordinates in the order (x,y).
  • Negative x means left; negative y means down.
  • Use the same unit scale on both axes unless a different scale is specified.
  • If either coordinate is zero, the point is on an axis.
