Degree and Types of Polynomials

Brief Learn • Class 9 • Polynomials

📝 Practice Quiz🧪 Interactive Simulator

1. Degree of a polynomial

For a non-zero polynomial in one variable, the degree is the greatest exponent with a non-zero coefficient, after simplifying and arranging like terms.

p(x) = 4x⁵ − 2x³ + 7x − 9
Degree = 5; leading coefficient = 4

2. Types based on degree

  • Constant: degree 0; example 7
  • Linear: degree 1; ax + b, a ≠ 0
  • Quadratic: degree 2; ax² + bx + c, a ≠ 0
  • Cubic: degree 3; ax³ + bx² + cx + d, a ≠ 0

Higher-degree polynomials include quartic (degree 4), quintic (degree 5), and so on.

3. Types based on number of terms

  • Monomial: one non-zero term, e.g. 5x²
  • Binomial: two unlike terms, e.g. x² − 3
  • Trinomial: three unlike terms, e.g. x² + 2x + 1

These two classifications are independent. For example, 7x³ is a cubic monomial.

4. Standard form and leading coefficient

Write terms in descending powers. The coefficient of the highest-power term is the leading coefficient.

3 − 2x + 5x⁴ − x²
= 5x⁴ − x² − 2x + 3
Degree 4; leading coefficient 5

5. Important special cases

  • Non-zero constant polynomial has degree 0.
  • The zero polynomial (all coefficients zero) has no defined degree in the usual school convention.
  • A missing power has coefficient zero; it does not affect the degree.
  • Expressions with negative or fractional variable exponents are not polynomials in x.

6. Quick practice examples

6x³ − 4x + 1 → cubic, degree 3, trinomial
−9x² → quadratic, degree 2, monomial
5x + 8 → linear, degree 1, binomial
−4 → constant, degree 0, monomial

Quick revision

  • Degree = highest exponent with non-zero coefficient.
  • Classify by degree and by number of terms separately.
  • Simplify like terms before finding degree.
  • Zero polynomial is a special case; its degree is undefined.
