Brief concept: What is a polynomial?

A polynomial is an algebraic expression made by adding or subtracting terms. In one variable, its powers must be non-negative whole numbers, and its coefficients are constants.

\\[P(x)=a_nx^n+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\\cdots+a_1x+a_0\\]

Here, each \(a_i\) is a coefficient, \(x\) is the variable, and the exponents are whole numbers such as 0, 1, 2, 3, …

Term
A part separated by + or −\(5x^2\)
Coefficient
Number multiplying the variable part\(5\) in \(5x^2\)
Constant
A term without a variable\(-3\)
Degree
Highest exponent with a non-zero coefficient\(3\) in \(2x^3+x+1\)

By number of non-zero terms: one term = monomial; two = binomial; three = trinomial. A non-zero constant has degree 0. The zero polynomial has no defined degree.

\\[4x^3-2x^2+3x-5\\]

This example has four terms, degree 3, and leading coefficient 4.

5. Quick revision

Coefficient
Number multiplying the variable part\(7\) in \(7x^2\)
Constant term
Term without a variable\(-5\) in \(x+(-5)\)
Leading coefficient
Coefficient of the highest-degree term\(4\) in \(4x^3+2\)
Standard form
Terms arranged in descending powers\(x^3+x^2+x+1\)
