Positive and Negative Rational Numbers

A rational number can be written as p/q, where p and q are integers and q is not zero. Its value may be positive, negative or zero.

1. Positive, negative and zero

  • Positive rational numbers are greater than zero, such as 3/5 and 2.
  • Negative rational numbers are less than zero, such as −3/5 and −2.
  • Zero is neither positive nor negative.
Negative0Positive

2. Equivalent rational numbers

Multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero integer gives an equivalent rational number.

3/4 = 6/8 = 9/12
−2/3 = −4/6 = −6/9
To simplify −18/24, divide numerator and denominator by 6: −3/4.

3. Standard form

A rational number is in standard form when its denominator is positive and the numerator and denominator have no common factor other than 1.

−10/15 is not in standard form. Divide both terms by 5 to get −2/3.

4. Compare on a number line

  • Numbers farther right are greater.
  • Every positive rational number is greater than zero and every negative rational number.
  • Among negative numbers, the value closer to zero is greater: −1/5 is greater than −2/5.
−3/4 < −1/2 < 0 < 1/3 < 2/3

5. Addition and subtraction

Use a common denominator before adding or subtracting fractions.

2/7 + 3/7 = 5/7
−5/9 + 2/9 = −3/9 = −1/3
3/4 − 1/2 = 3/4 − 2/4 = 1/4
Subtracting a negative is the same as adding its opposite: −4/7 − (−2/7) = −4/7 + 2/7 = −2/7.

6. Multiplication and division

Multiply: numerator × numerator; denominator × denominator.
Divide: multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor.
  • Positive × positive = positive.
  • Negative × negative = positive.
  • Positive × negative = negative.
  • Division follows the same sign rules, provided the divisor is not zero.
(−2/3) × (3/5) = −2/5
(−3/8) ÷ (−2/5) = (−3/8) × (−5/2) = 15/16.

7. Important properties and common errors

  • Additive inverse: a + (−a) = 0.
  • Multiplicative identity: a × 1 = a.
  • Zero property: a × 0 = 0.
  • Division by zero is undefined.
  • Do not assume a negative number with a larger denominator is always smaller; compare values carefully.
  • Reduce the final fraction when possible.
