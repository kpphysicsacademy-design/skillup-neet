Profit, Loss and Discount

Understand cost price, selling price and marked price. The most important rule: profit and loss percentages use cost price as the base, while discount percentage uses marked price.

Profit/Loss → CP base • Discount → MP base

1. Important terms

  • Cost Price (CP): the price paid to buy an article.
  • Selling Price (SP): the price received when the article is sold.
  • Marked Price (MP): the listed or tagged price before discount.
  • Profit: when SP is greater than CP.
  • Loss: when SP is less than CP.
  • Discount: reduction from the marked price.

2. Profit and loss

Profit = SP − CP
Loss = CP − SP

Profit % = (Profit ÷ CP) × 100. Loss % = (Loss ÷ CP) × 100.

Example: CP = ₹500 and SP = ₹600. Profit = ₹100 and profit percentage = 100/500 × 100 = 20%.

3. Find selling price

SP = CP × (100 + Profit %) / 100
SP = CP × (100 − Loss %) / 100

Example: CP = ₹800 and profit = 15%. SP = 800 × 115/100 = ₹920.

4. Find cost price

CP = SP × 100 / (100 + Profit %)
CP = SP × 100 / (100 − Loss %)

Example: SP = ₹920 at 15% profit. CP = 920 × 100/115 = ₹800.

5. Discount and marked price

Discount = MP − SP
Discount % = (Discount ÷ MP) × 100
SP = MP × (100 − Discount %) / 100

Example: MP = ₹1,200 with a 10% discount. Discount = ₹120 and SP = ₹1,080.

6. Markup followed by discount

Suppose CP = ₹1,000. A seller marks it 25% above CP, so MP = ₹1,250. A 10% discount gives SP = ₹1,125.

Profit = ₹125 → Profit % = 12.5%

Do not simply subtract 10% from 25%. The discount is applied to the marked price, not directly to the cost price.

7. Successive discounts

Two discounts are applied one after another, each to the reduced price.

Equivalent discount = a + b − (a × b / 100)

Example: 20% followed by 10% gives 20 + 10 − 2 = 28% equivalent discount.

8. Quick checks

  • Profit or loss is always compared with CP.
  • Discount is always compared with MP.
  • Write down the base before calculating a percentage.
  • Check your result by calculating SP from CP or MP.
