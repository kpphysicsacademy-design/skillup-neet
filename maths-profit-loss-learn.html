BRIEF LEARN · PROFIT AND LOSS

Understand every transaction

Compare the amount paid to acquire an item with the amount received when it is sold. Always calculate percentages using the cost price unless the question specifies another base.

1. Cost price and selling price

Cost Price (CP) is the total amount spent to obtain an item. Selling Price (SP) is the amount received when it is sold. Include extra expenses such as transport, repairs or packaging in the total cost when they are part of the transaction.

Profit = SP − CP (when SP > CP)
Loss = CP − SP (when CP > SP)
Example: A bag costs ₹450 and sells for ₹510. Profit = ₹510 − ₹450 = ₹60.

2. Profit percentage

Profit % = (Profit ÷ CP) × 100

If CP = ₹400 and profit = ₹80, profit percentage = 80 ÷ 400 × 100 = 20%.

3. Loss percentage

Loss % = (Loss ÷ CP) × 100

If CP = ₹600 and loss = ₹90, loss percentage = 90 ÷ 600 × 100 = 15%.

4. Find the selling price or cost price

SP = CP + Profit
SP = CP − Loss
CP = SP − Profit
CP = SP + Loss

For percentage questions, convert the percentage to a multiplier.

At 20% profit: SP = 120% of CP = 1.20 × CP.
At 15% loss: SP = 85% of CP = 0.85 × CP.
So if SP is ₹850 at 15% loss, CP = 850 ÷ 0.85 = ₹1,000.

5. Marked price and discount

Marked Price (MP) is the displayed/list price. Discount is reduced from MP to obtain SP.

Discount = MP − SP
Discount % = (Discount ÷ MP) × 100
SP = MP × (1 − discount rate)
MP = ₹1,200 with 10% discount. Discount = ₹120 and SP = ₹1,080. Compare ₹1,080 with CP to decide profit or loss.

6. Successive steps and common traps

  • Add all relevant expenses to CP before finding profit or loss.
  • Apply a discount to marked price, not directly to cost price.
  • Profit and loss percentages are normally based on CP; discount percentage is based on MP.
  • For two different items, calculate each cost price before combining totals.
  • Equal selling prices with equal profit and loss percentages do not necessarily cancel.
