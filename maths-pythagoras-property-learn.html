Pythagoras Property

Brief Learn · Understand the relationship before practising.

1. The key idea

In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides. The hypotenuse is opposite the 90° angle and is the longest side.

a² + b² = c²

The two shorter sides a and b meet at the right angle. Side c is opposite it.

2. Finding the hypotenuse

When both legs are known, add their squares and take the square root.

Example: a = 3 cm, b = 4 cm
c² = 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25
c = √25 = 5 cm

3. Finding a missing leg

When the hypotenuse and one leg are known, subtract the square of the known leg from the square of the hypotenuse, then take the square root.

Example: c = 13 cm, a = 5 cm
b² = 13² − 5² = 169 − 25 = 144
b = √144 = 12 cm

4. Pythagorean triples

Whole-number side lengths that satisfy the theorem are called Pythagorean triples.

  • 3, 4, 5
  • 5, 12, 13
  • 8, 15, 17
  • 7, 24, 25

Multiplying every number in a triple by the same positive number gives another triple.

5. Converse: test for a right triangle

Arrange the three side lengths so c is the longest. If a² + b² = c², the triangle is right-angled.

For 6, 8, 10: 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100 = 10².

If the sum is greater than c², the triangle is acute; if it is less than c², the triangle is obtuse, provided the lengths form a triangle.

6. Common mistakes

  • Using the formula for a triangle that is not right-angled.
  • Choosing a side other than the hypotenuse as c.
  • Adding side lengths instead of their squares.
  • For a missing leg, forgetting to subtract before taking the square root.
  • Forgetting units in the final length.
