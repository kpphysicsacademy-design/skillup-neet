Quadrants — Brief Learn

Read coordinate signs to identify where a point lies on the Cartesian plane.

1. The coordinate plane

Two perpendicular number lines form the Cartesian plane. The horizontal line is the x-axis; the vertical line is the y-axis. They meet at the origin O(0,0).

Quadrants are numbered anticlockwise, starting at the upper-right region.

2. Sign pattern of the four quadrants

Quadrant I · (+,+)x positive, y positive
Quadrant II · (−,+)x negative, y positive
Quadrant III · (−,−)x negative, y negative
Quadrant IV · (+,−)x positive, y negative

3. Points on the axes

x-axis: (x, 0)　·　y-axis: (0, y)　·　Origin: (0, 0)

A point on either axis is not in any quadrant. On the x-axis, y is zero. On the y-axis, x is zero.

4. Worked examples

  • (−3, 5): x negative, y positive → Quadrant II.
  • (4, −2): x positive, y negative → Quadrant IV.
  • (−6, −1): both negative → Quadrant III.
  • (0, 7): lies on the positive y-axis, not in a quadrant.

5. Quick revision

  • Always read the ordered pair as (x, y).
  • Check the sign of x first, then y.
  • Quadrants go anticlockwise: I, II, III, IV.
  • Zero in either coordinate means the point lies on an axis.
