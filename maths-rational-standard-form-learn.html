Standard Form of a Rational Number

A rational number is in standard form when its denominator is positive and its numerator and denominator have no common factor other than 1.

1. The two conditions

  • The denominator must be a positive integer.
  • The numerator and denominator must be coprime: their HCF is 1.
  • The fraction must represent the same rational number after simplification.
Standard form: p/q, where q > 0 and HCF(|p|, q) = 1

2. Reduce using HCF

Find the highest common factor (HCF) of the absolute values of numerator and denominator. Divide both by this HCF.

Example: Reduce 18/24.
HCF(18, 24) = 6.
18 ÷ 6 / 24 ÷ 6 = 3/4.
a/b = (a ÷ HCF) / (b ÷ HCF)

3. Keep the denominator positive

If the denominator is negative, move the negative sign to the numerator before or after simplifying.

6/(−15) = −6/15 = −2/5
(−14)/(−21) = 14/21 = 2/3
(−21)/28 = −3/4

4. Special case: zero

Zero divided by any non-zero integer is zero. Its standard form is always 0/1.

0/9 = 0/1    and    0/(−13) = 0/1

A denominator of zero is never allowed in a rational number.

5. Check whether a fraction is already standard

7/10: denominator positive and HCF(7,10) = 1, so it is standard.
−6/9: HCF(6,9) = 3, so reduce to −2/3.
4/(−7): denominator negative, so write −4/7.

6. Common mistakes

  • Dividing only the numerator or only the denominator.
  • Changing the sign of just one term incorrectly.
  • Stopping before the HCF becomes 1.
  • Writing zero over a denominator other than 1 as its standard form.
  • Confusing standard form of rational numbers with scientific notation.
