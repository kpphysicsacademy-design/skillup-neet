Remainder Theorem

Brief Learn • Class 9 • Polynomials

📝 Practice Quiz🧪 Interactive Simulator

1. The theorem

When a polynomial p(x) is divided by a linear polynomial x − a, the remainder is the value of p(x) at x = a.

p(x) = (x − a)q(x) + r
Remainder r = p(a)

The remainder is a constant because the divisor has degree one.

2. Why it works

By the polynomial division algorithm, p(x) = (x − a)q(x) + r. Substitute x = a:

p(a) = (a − a)q(a) + r = r

So evaluating the polynomial at a gives the remainder directly, without long division.

3. Worked example

Find the remainder when p(x) = x³ − 3x² + 4x + 5 is divided by x − 2.

a = 2
p(2) = 8 − 12 + 8 + 5 = 9
Remainder = 9

4. Any linear divisor

For a divisor mx + n (m ≠ 0), its zero is x = −n/m. Therefore:

Remainder on division by (mx + n) = p(−n/m)

Example: divisor 2x − 1 gives test value x = 1/2. The remainder is p(1/2), not p(2).

5. Find an unknown coefficient

If p(x) = x² + kx + 1 leaves remainder 4 when divided by x − 2:

p(2) = 4
4 + 2k + 1 = 4
2k = −1 → k = −1/2

6. Compare two remainders

If two polynomials leave the same remainder when divided by x − a, equate their values at a. This often creates an equation in an unknown coefficient.

Common mistakes

  • For x + 3, substitute −3, not +3.
  • For 2x − 1, solve 2x − 1 = 0 first.
  • The remainder is p(a), not the quotient.
  • Factor Theorem is the special case where p(a) = 0.

Quick revision

Divisor x − a → remainder p(a)
Divisor mx + n → remainder p(−n/m)
Remainder 0 → divisor is a factor
