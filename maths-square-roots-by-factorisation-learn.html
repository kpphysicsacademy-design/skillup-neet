Square Roots by Factorisation

Find the prime factors, group identical factors into pairs, take one factor from each pair and multiply them to obtain the square root.

√(p² × q²) = p × q

1. Prime factorisation

Write the number as a product of prime numbers. A factor tree can help you break a composite number into prime factors.

36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 = 2² × 3²

2. Make pairs

Group identical prime factors in pairs. For a perfect square, every prime factor has an even exponent.

36 = (2 × 2) × (3 × 3)

Each pair contributes one factor to the square root.

3. Take one factor from each pair

√36 = 2 × 3 = 6

The product of the selected factors is the principal square root.

4. Worked examples

  1. √144: 144 = 2⁴ × 3². Take 2² × 3 = 4 × 3 = 12.
  2. √324: 324 = 2² × 3⁴. Take 2 × 3² = 18.
  3. √900: 900 = 2² × 3² × 5². Take 2 × 3 × 5 = 30.

5. When a factor is unpaired

If a prime factor has an odd exponent, one factor remains outside a pair. The number is not a perfect square, but its radical can be simplified.

√72 = √(2³ × 3²) = 2 × 3 × √2 = 6√2

For example, √72 is not an integer because one factor of 2 remains unpaired.

6. Check your answer

  • Multiply the factors you selected.
  • Square the proposed root to check that it returns the original number.
  • For a perfect square, no prime factor should be left unpaired.

A quick check: 18² = 324, so √324 = 18.
