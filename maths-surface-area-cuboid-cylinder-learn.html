Surface Area of Cuboid and Cylinder

Brief Class 9 revision: identify faces, choose the correct formula and calculate the area covered.

1. Cuboid: total surface area (TSA)

A cuboid has six rectangular faces. Opposite faces are equal: two lb faces, two bh faces and two hl faces.

\(\text{TSA}=2(lb+bh+hl)\)

Example: l = 5 cm, b = 3 cm, h = 2 cm.

\(2(5×3+3×2+5×2)=2(31)=62\text{ cm}^2\)

2. Cuboid: lateral surface area (LSA)

Lateral area includes the four side walls, excluding the top and bottom. If the base is l × b and height is h:

\(\text{LSA}=2h(l+b)\)

For l = 5 cm, b = 3 cm and h = 2 cm: LSA = 2 × 2 × (5 + 3) = 32 cm².

3. Cylinder: curved surface area (CSA)

Unroll the curved surface of a cylinder. It forms a rectangle whose length is the circumference of the circular base (2πr) and whose width is h.

\(\text{CSA}=2πrh\)

For r = 7 cm and h = 10 cm, using π = 22/7:

\(2×\frac{22}{7}×7×10=440\text{ cm}^2\)

4. Cylinder: total surface area (TSA)

A closed cylinder has one curved surface and two circular ends. Add the areas of both circular bases to the CSA.

\(\text{TSA}=2πrh+2πr^2=2πr(r+h)\)

For an open-top cylinder, use CSA + one base = 2πrh + πr². Read the question carefully before choosing the formula.

5. Units and quick checks

  • Use the radius, not diameter: r = d/2.
  • Use the same length unit for every dimension.
  • Surface area is written in square units (cm², m²).
  • For a label around a can, calculate CSA only.
  • For a closed solid painted on every side, calculate TSA.

6. Formula recap

Cuboid TSA: \(2(lb+bh+hl)\)
Cuboid LSA: \(2h(l+b)\)
Cylinder CSA: \(2πrh\)
Cylinder TSA: \(2πr(r+h)\)
