Terms and Coefficients

Learn to identify terms, numerical coefficients, variables, constants and like terms in algebraic expressions.

📝 Practice quiz🧮 Open simulator

1. What is a term?

A term is a part of an algebraic expression separated by plus or minus signs. The sign immediately before a term belongs to that term.

4x−3y+8
Three terms: 4x, −3y and 8

2. Coefficient and variable

A coefficient is the numerical factor multiplying a variable part. The variable is the letter representing a number.

In −6m: coefficient = −6, variable = m
Remember: In x, the coefficient is 1. In −y, the coefficient is −1. The sign is part of the coefficient.

3. Constant term

A constant term contains no variable. It can be positive, negative or zero.

In 7x² − 3x + 12: constant = 12
In 5a − 9, the constant term is −9. The subtraction sign belongs to the constant.

4. Like and unlike terms

Like terms have exactly the same variable part with the same powers. Their numerical coefficients may be different.

3x and −8x are like terms
2x² and 5x² are like terms. But 2x and 2x² are unlike terms because the powers of x differ.

5. How to identify terms correctly

  • Split the expression at plus and minus signs, keeping each sign with its term.
  • Count each signed part as one term.
  • Find the numerical factor in each variable term.
  • A term with no variable is a constant.
  • Compare variable letters and their powers to identify like terms.
