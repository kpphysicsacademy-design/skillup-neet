Triangles and Their Properties

Brief Learn · Class 9 · Learn the key rules before practice

1. Angle-sum property

The three interior angles of every triangle add up to 180°.

∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°

Example: if two angles are 50° and 60°, the third angle is 180° − 50° − 60° = 70°.

2. Triangle types by sides

  • Equilateral: three equal sides; each angle is 60°.
  • Isosceles: exactly two equal sides; the base angles are equal.
  • Scalene: all three sides are different.

3. Triangle types by angles

  • Acute: all angles are less than 90°.
  • Right: one angle is exactly 90°.
  • Obtuse: one angle is greater than 90°.

A triangle cannot have two right angles because its angle sum is 180°.

4. Exterior-angle property

An exterior angle equals the sum of the two remote interior angles.

Exterior angle = remote angle 1 + remote angle 2

Example: if the exterior angle is 125° and one remote interior angle is 55°, the other is 70°.

5. Side-angle relationships

  • The longer side lies opposite the larger angle.
  • The shorter side lies opposite the smaller angle.
  • Equal sides have equal opposite angles.

6. Triangle inequality

The sum of any two side lengths must be greater than the third side.

a + b > c,   b + c > a,   c + a > b
If two sides are 5 cm and 8 cm, the third side x must satisfy 3 < x < 13. The endpoints are not allowed.

Quick exam method

  1. Use 180° for the interior-angle sum.
  2. Use equal base angles in an isosceles triangle.
  3. For an exterior angle, add the two remote interior angles.
  4. Compare a side with its opposite angle.
  5. For a missing side, check all three triangle inequalities.
