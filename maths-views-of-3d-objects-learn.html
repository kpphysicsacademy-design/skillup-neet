🧊 Views of 3D Objects

A 3D object looks different when viewed from above, from the front or from the side. These flat drawings are called views or orthographic views.

🧭 Open view simulator📝 Practise 30 questions

1. The three main views

Look straight down for the top view, straight at the front for the front view, and straight at one side for the side view. Each is a 2D outline or arrangement.

Remember: Top shows length and depth; front shows length and height; side shows depth and height.

2. Top view (plan)

Look vertically down. A plan shows the footprint: the ground positions occupied by the object. It does not show the height of each column.

3 cubes across and 2 deep create a 3 × 2 footprint: 6 squares in the top view.

3. Front view

Look straight at the front. It shows width across the front and height. Vertically stacked cubes appear one above another.

Column heights 3, 2 and 1 create a front profile 3 squares wide and 3 high.

4. Side view

Look straight at one side. It shows depth and height. It may differ from the front view because the depth arrangement can differ.

Side-view dimensions = depth × height
A cuboid 8 cm long, 5 cm deep and 3 cm high has a side view measuring 5 cm × 3 cm.

The view depends on how the solid is positioned.

6. Cube stacks: footprint versus height

The top view records which ground positions are occupied. Front and side views show the maximum column height at each position along that direction.

Total cubes = add the heights of all columns.
Four columns have heights 1, 2, 2 and 3. Total = 1 + 2 + 2 + 3 = 8 cubes. The top view still has only 4 occupied squares.

7. Areas of the views

For a cuboid with length L, depth D and height H:

Top area = L × D
Front area = L × H
Side area = D × H
L = 10 cm, D = 4 cm, H = 6 cm: top = 40 cm², front = 60 cm², side = 24 cm².

8. A reliable method

  1. Identify the viewing direction.
  2. Choose the two dimensions visible in that view.
  3. For top views, count occupied positions.
  4. For front and side views, record column heights.
  5. Check units; length uses cm, area uses cm².
