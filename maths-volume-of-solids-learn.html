CLASS 9 · SURFACE AREAS AND VOLUMES

Volume of Solids

Volume tells us how much three-dimensional space a solid occupies. It is measured in cubic units.

1. Cube and cuboid

A cube has all edges equal. A cuboid has length, breadth and height.

Cube: V = a³
Cuboid: V = l × b × h

Example: A cuboid of 8 cm × 5 cm × 3 cm has volume 8 × 5 × 3 = 120 cm³.

2. Cylinder

A cylinder has two equal circular bases. Its volume is the area of its circular base multiplied by its perpendicular height.

V = πr²h

Example: r = 3 cm and h = 5 cm gives V = π × 9 × 5 = 45π cm³.

3. Cone

A cone has a circular base and one vertex. For the same base radius and height, its volume is one-third of the matching cylinder.

V = ⅓πr²h

Example: r = 3 cm and h = 8 cm gives V = ⅓ × π × 9 × 8 = 24π cm³.

4. Sphere

A sphere is perfectly round; every point on its surface is at the same distance from its centre.

V = ⁴⁄₃πr³

Example: r = 3 cm gives V = ⁴⁄₃ × π × 27 = 36π cm³.

Remember

  • Convert all dimensions to the same unit before calculating.
  • Write the final volume in cubic units: cm³, m³, etc.
  • Diameter = 2 × radius.
  • If a solid is melted and reshaped without loss, total volume remains the same.
  • Use π = 22/7 or 3.14 as instructed.
