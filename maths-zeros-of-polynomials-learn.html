Zeros of Polynomials

Brief Learn • Class 9 • Polynomials

📝 Practice Quiz🧪 Interactive Simulator

1. Meaning of a zero

A zero of p(x) is a value a for which the polynomial evaluates to zero.

a is a zero of p(x) ⇔ p(a) = 0

Zeros are also called roots.

2. Find zeros by substitution

For p(x) = x² − 5x + 6:

p(2) = 4 − 10 + 6 = 0
p(3) = 9 − 15 + 6 = 0
Zeros: 2 and 3

3. Zeros and factors

If a is a zero, then (x − a) is a factor, and conversely.

p(a) = 0 ⇔ (x − a) divides p(x)

Example: x² − 9 = (x − 3)(x + 3), so its zeros are 3 and −3.

4. Graphical meaning

The graph y = p(x) meets or touches the x-axis wherever p(x) = 0. The x-coordinates of those intercepts are the real zeros.

5. Number of zeros

  • A non-zero constant polynomial has no zeros.
  • A linear polynomial has one zero.
  • A quadratic polynomial has at most two real zeros.
  • A cubic polynomial has at most three real zeros.

A polynomial of degree n can have at most n distinct zeros.

6. Useful patterns

x² − a² = (x − a)(x + a)
x² + (a+b)x + ab = (x+a)(x+b)

Use factorisation to identify zeros, then verify each by substitution.

Quick revision

  • Substitute a candidate into p(x).
  • Value zero means it is a zero.
  • Every real zero corresponds to an x-axis intercept.
  • Each zero a gives a factor x − a.
