📌 Quick Cheat Sheet

Absolute uncertainty (Δx) expresses the estimated uncertainty in a measurement and has the same unit as the measured quantity.

RuleExpression
Reported measurementx ± Δx
Estimated interval[x − Δx, x + Δx]
Range of readingsmaximum − minimum
Simple half-range estimateΔx ≈ (maximum − minimum) / 2

Worked example: 10.2, 10.4, 10.3 cm → mean 10.3 cm, range 0.2 cm, half-range 0.1 cm. Report approximately (10.3 ± 0.1) cm.

Another example: (12.0 ± 0.2) cm represents an estimated interval from 11.8 to 12.2 cm.

NEET tip: uncertainty is not the same as error. Half-range is a simple estimate for repeated readings, not a universal uncertainty formula; report uncertainty with appropriate significant figures.

1. Meaning of absolute uncertainty

Absolute uncertainty estimates the range of doubt in a measured quantity. It is written with the same unit as the measurement.

Measured value = x ± Δx

For example, (12.0 ± 0.2) cm means the estimated value is 12.0 cm with an uncertainty of 0.2 cm.

2. Upper and lower limits

The uncertainty gives a simple interval around the measured value.

Lower limit = x − Δx
Upper limit = x + Δx

For (20.0 ± 0.5) g, the interval is 19.5 g to 20.5 g.

3. Uncertainty from repeated readings

When repeated readings are available, calculate their mean and range. A common simple estimate of absolute uncertainty is half the range.

Range = maximum − minimum
Half-range uncertainty = (maximum − minimum) / 2

4. Instrument resolution

The smallest scale division or display increment gives information about instrument resolution. For many analogue instruments, a reading uncertainty is often estimated as about half the smallest division; follow the instrument's stated convention where available.

Display resolution alone does not guarantee accuracy.

5. Absolute vs relative uncertainty

Absolute uncertainty has the same unit as the measured quantity. Relative uncertainty is the ratio of absolute uncertainty to the measured value and has no unit.

Relative uncertainty = Δx / |x|
Percentage uncertainty = (Δx / |x|) × 100%

6. Reporting a measurement

Write the measured value, its unit and a suitable uncertainty together, for example (3.50 ± 0.05) m. The value and uncertainty should be rounded consistently and should reflect the measurement method.

7. What uncertainty does not mean

Uncertainty does not automatically remove systematic error or guarantee accuracy. Calibration, zero-error checks, suitable instruments and careful technique are still important.
