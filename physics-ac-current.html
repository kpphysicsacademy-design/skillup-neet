〰️ AC Current

Understand alternating current, instantaneous and peak values, RMS and average current, frequency, phase and current in basic AC circuits.

1. What is alternating current?

Alternating current (AC) is an electric current whose magnitude changes with time and whose direction reverses periodically. A sinusoidal current is commonly represented by:

\[i(t)=I_0\sin(\omega t+\phi)\]

Here, i is instantaneous current, I₀ is peak current, ω is angular frequency, t is time and φ is the initial phase.

AC repeatedly changes direction. Direct current (DC), in its usual steady form, flows in one direction.

2. Instantaneous and peak current

The instantaneous current is the current at a particular instant. Peak current (I₀) is the maximum magnitude attained by the AC current.

\[-I_0\leq i(t)\leq I_0\]

For a sine wave, current varies between +I₀ and −I₀. The peak-to-peak current is 2I₀.

3. RMS value of AC current

The root mean square (RMS) current is the effective current that produces the same average heating effect in a resistor as an equivalent DC current.

\[I_{\mathrm{rms}}=\frac{I_0}{\sqrt{2}}\approx0.707I_0\]

This relation is valid for a sinusoidal current. Conversely, \(I_0=\sqrt{2}I_{\mathrm{rms}}\).

Example: if peak current is 10 A, RMS current is \(10/\sqrt{2}\approx7.07\) A.

4. Average value of sinusoidal current

Over a complete cycle, the positive and negative halves cancel, so the average current is zero. Over one positive half-cycle, the average is non-zero.

\[I_{\mathrm{avg,full}}=0\]
\[I_{\mathrm{avg,half}}=\frac{2I_0}{\pi}\approx0.637I_0\]

RMS and average values are different: RMS describes heating effect, while average is the arithmetic mean over the chosen interval.

5. Frequency, time period and angular frequency

Frequency f is the number of complete cycles per second. Time period T is the time for one complete cycle.

\[f=\frac{1}{T},\qquad T=\frac{1}{f}\]
\[\omega=2\pi f=\frac{2\pi}{T}\]

Frequency is measured in hertz (Hz); angular frequency is measured in rad s⁻¹. A 50 Hz current has a period of 0.02 s.

6. AC current through a resistor

In a purely resistive AC circuit, current and voltage are in phase. They reach zero and maximum values at the same instants.

\[i(t)=\frac{V_0}{R}\sin\omega t\]
\[I_{\mathrm{rms}}=\frac{V_{\mathrm{rms}}}{R}\]

Ohm’s law applies instantaneously as \(v(t)=i(t)R\). The resistance does not introduce a phase difference.

7. Worked numerical examples

Example 1: Peak current is 14 A. Find RMS current.

\[I_{\mathrm{rms}}=\frac{14}{\sqrt2}\approx9.9\,\mathrm{A}\]

Example 2: RMS current is 5 A. Find peak current.

\[I_0=\sqrt2(5)=7.07\,\mathrm{A}\]

Example 3: A 50 Hz current flows. Find its time period.

\[T=\frac{1}{50}=0.02\,\mathrm{s}\]

Example 4: A 20 Ω resistor is connected to a 100 V RMS supply.

\[I_{\mathrm{rms}}=\frac{100}{20}=5\,\mathrm{A}\]

8. Quick revision and common mistakes

  • Sinusoidal current: \(i(t)=I_0\sin(\omega t+\phi)\).
  • Peak current: \(I_0\); peak-to-peak current: \(2I_0\).
  • RMS current: \(I_{\mathrm{rms}}=I_0/\sqrt2\).
  • Full-cycle average: \(I_{\mathrm{avg}}=0\).
  • Half-cycle average: \(I_{\mathrm{avg}}=2I_0/\pi\).
  • Frequency and period: \(f=1/T\).
  • Angular frequency: \(\omega=2\pi f\).
  • Pure resistor: current and voltage are in phase.
