〰️ AC Resonance

Understand resonance in an AC series LCR circuit, including resonant frequency, impedance, current, voltage magnification, quality factor and bandwidth.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations
Question 1 of 300%

1. What is AC resonance?

In a series LCR circuit, resonance occurs at the frequency for which inductive reactance equals capacitive reactance.

\[X_L=X_C\]

Their effects cancel, making the circuit purely resistive. The supply voltage and current are in phase.

2. Condition for resonance

Using \(X_L=\omega L\) and \(X_C=1/(\omega C)\), at resonance:

\[\omega_0L=\frac{1}{\omega_0C}\]
\[\omega_0^2=\frac{1}{LC}\]

Here \(\omega_0\) is the angular resonant frequency.

3. Resonant frequency

The angular and ordinary resonant frequencies are:

\[\omega_0=\frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}},\qquad f_0=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}\]

Resonant frequency increases if L or C decreases. In an ideal series circuit, it is independent of resistance.

4. Impedance and current at resonance

The impedance magnitude of a series LCR circuit is \(Z=\sqrt{R^2+(X_L-X_C)^2}\). At resonance:

\[Z_{min}=R,\qquad I_{max}=\frac{V}{R}\]

For a fixed RMS supply voltage, current is maximum because impedance is minimum.

Resonance does not mean impedance is zero unless the resistance is zero in the idealised model.

5. Phase and power factor

At resonance the phase angle is zero:

\[\phi=0,\qquad \cos\phi=1\]

The circuit behaves like a pure resistor and consumes maximum average power for a fixed RMS voltage and resistance.

\[P=VI\cos\phi=I^2R\]

6. Voltage magnification

At resonance, the voltages across the inductor and capacitor are equal in magnitude and opposite in phase. They cancel in the supply-voltage phasor sum.

\[V_L=IX_L,\qquad V_C=IX_C\]

Each can be much larger than the supply voltage in a high-Q circuit. The resistor voltage equals the supply voltage at resonance.

7. Quality factor and bandwidth

The quality factor of a series LCR circuit is:

\[Q=\frac{\omega_0L}{R}=\frac{1}{\omega_0CR}\]

Bandwidth is the difference between upper and lower half-power frequencies:

\[\Delta f=f_2-f_1=\frac{R}{2\pi L},\qquad Q=\frac{f_0}{\Delta f}\]

Higher Q means sharper resonance and smaller bandwidth.

8. Frequency response and quick revision

  • Below resonance: \(X_C>X_L\), circuit is capacitive.
  • At resonance: \(X_C=X_L\), circuit is resistive.
  • Above resonance: \(X_L>X_C\), circuit is inductive.
  • At resonance, impedance is minimum and current is maximum.
  • Power factor is unity and phase angle is zero.
  • \(f_0=1/(2\pi\sqrt{LC})\), \(Q=f_0/\Delta f\).
Remember: resonance is the balance point between inductive and capacitive reactance.
