〰️ AC Voltage

Understand sinusoidal alternating voltage, peak and RMS values, time period, frequency and phase through simple explanations and worked examples.

1. What is alternating voltage?

An alternating voltage (AC voltage) changes its magnitude and direction periodically with time. In a sinusoidal AC source, the voltage varies smoothly between positive and negative maximum values.

\\[v(t)=V_0\\sin(\\omega t+\\phi)\\]

Here v is instantaneous voltage, V₀ is peak voltage, ω is angular frequency, t is time and φ is initial phase.

Unlike steady DC voltage, sinusoidal AC reverses polarity every half-cycle.

2. Peak value and instantaneous value

The peak value (V₀) is the maximum magnitude reached by the alternating voltage. The instantaneous value is the voltage at a particular time.

\\[-V_0\\leq v(t)\\leq V_0\\]

For v = V₀ sin ωt, voltage is zero at ωt = 0, reaches +V₀ at π/2, returns to zero at π and reaches −V₀ at 3π/2.

3. Time period and frequency

The time period T is the time taken to complete one full cycle. Frequency f is the number of complete cycles per second.

\\[f=\\frac{1}{T},\\qquad T=\\frac{1}{f}\\]

Frequency is measured in hertz (Hz), while time period is measured in seconds (s).

For a 50 Hz AC supply, T = 1/50 = 0.02 s.

4. Angular frequency and phase

Angular frequency measures how quickly the phase angle changes. Phase describes the position of the waveform within its cycle.

\\[\\omega=2\\pi f=\\frac{2\\pi}{T}\\]

Angular frequency is measured in rad s⁻¹. In v = V₀ sin(ωt + φ), φ is the initial phase angle.

5. RMS value of AC voltage

The root mean square (RMS) value is the effective value of AC. It is the value of DC voltage that produces the same average heating effect in a resistor.

\\[V_{\\mathrm{rms}}=\\frac{V_0}{\\sqrt{2}}\\approx 0.707V_0\\]

This relation applies to a pure sinusoidal voltage. RMS is the value commonly specified for AC mains.

6. Average value of sinusoidal AC

The average of a pure sinusoidal voltage over one complete cycle is zero because the positive and negative half cycles cancel. Over a positive half-cycle, the average value is non-zero.

\\[V_{\\mathrm{avg,half}}=\\frac{2V_0}{\\pi}\\]
\\[V_{\\mathrm{avg,full}}=0\\]

Do not confuse the half-cycle average with the RMS value.

7. Worked numerical examples

Example 1: Peak voltage is 311 V. Find RMS voltage.

\\[V_{\\mathrm{rms}}=\\frac{311}{\\sqrt{2}}\\approx 220\\,\\mathrm{V}\\]

Example 2: Frequency is 50 Hz. Find time period and angular frequency.

\\[T=\\frac{1}{50}=0.02\\,\\mathrm{s}\\]
\\[\\omega=2\\pi(50)=100\\pi\\,\\mathrm{rad\\,s^{-1}}\\]

Example 3: RMS voltage is 100 V. Find peak voltage.

\\[V_0=\\sqrt{2}(100)\\approx 141.4\\,\\mathrm{V}\\]

8. Quick revision and common mistakes

  • Instantaneous voltage: \\(v(t)=V_0\\sin(\\omega t+\\phi)\\).
  • Peak voltage: \\(V_0\\).
  • RMS voltage: \\(V_{\\mathrm{rms}}=V_0/\\sqrt{2}\\).
  • Frequency and period: \\(f=1/T\\).
  • Angular frequency: \\(\\omega=2\\pi f\\).
  • Full-cycle average: \\(V_{\\mathrm{avg}}=0\\).
  • Half-cycle average: \\(V_{\\mathrm{avg}}=2V_0/\\pi\\).
