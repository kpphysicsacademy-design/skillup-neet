Acceleration

A short lesson: meaning, formula and one example.

📘 Learn🎮 Simulator🧠 Practice

📌 Quick Cheat Sheet

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity with time. It is a vector with SI unit m/s² and dimension [LT⁻²].

ConceptRule
Average accelerationa(avg) = (v − u) / Δt
Instantaneous accelerationa = dv/dt = d²x/dt²
Constant accelerationv = u + at
Velocity–time graphSlope = acceleration
Negative accelerationPoints along negative axis; not always slowing down

Worked example: velocity changes from +2 m/s to +14 m/s in 4 s → acceleration = (14 − 2)/4 = +3 m/s².

Direction matters: a body moving at −10 m/s with acceleration −2 m/s² speeds up. Opposite signs of velocity and acceleration mean speed decreases until a possible reversal.

NEET tip: constant speed on a circular path can still involve acceleration because the velocity direction changes.

1. Meaning

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity with time.

a = (v − u) / t

SI unit: m/s² • Dimension: [LT⁻²] • Vector quantity.

2. Important formula

v = u + at

u = initial velocity, v = final velocity, a = acceleration, t = time.

3. Simple example

Velocity changes from 4 m/s to 16 m/s in 3 s.
a = (16 − 4) ÷ 3
a = 4 m/s².

Remember

• Positive or negative signs show direction.
• Zero acceleration means constant velocity.
• Acceleration opposite to velocity reduces speed.
• Slope of a velocity–time graph gives acceleration.
