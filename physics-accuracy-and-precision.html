1. What is accuracy?

Accuracy tells how close a measured value is to the accepted or reference value. A small difference means a more accurate result.

Absolute error = |Measured value − Accepted value|

Example: if the accepted length is 10.0 cm and you measure 9.9 cm, the absolute error is 0.1 cm.

2. What is precision?

Precision describes how close repeated measurements are to one another. A tightly grouped set has high precision, even if it is not close to the accepted value.

Range = Highest reading − Lowest reading

3. Accuracy and precision are different

Accuracy compares a result with a reference. Precision compares measurements with each other.

Measurements can be precise but inaccurate when a systematic error shifts all readings away from the accepted value.

4. Four possible measurement patterns

Accurate and precise: readings cluster near the accepted value.

Precise but inaccurate: readings cluster together but away from the accepted value.

Accurate on average but imprecise: readings are spread around the accepted value.

Neither: readings are scattered and far from the accepted value.

5. Random and systematic errors

Random errors cause readings to vary unpredictably and mainly affect precision. Systematic errors create a consistent bias and mainly affect accuracy.

Repeat measurements to assess spread; calibrate instruments to identify systematic offsets.

6. Mean and measurement spread

The arithmetic mean gives a representative central value for repeated measurements.

Mean = Sum of readings ÷ Number of readings

The range gives a simple measure of spread. A smaller range generally indicates better precision.

7. How to improve measurement quality

Use a suitable calibrated instrument, follow a consistent method, avoid parallax, repeat readings, record units, and report realistic uncertainty. Extra decimal places alone do not guarantee accuracy.
