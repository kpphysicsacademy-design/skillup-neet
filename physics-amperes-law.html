📘 Virtual Learn: Ampere's Law

Understand enclosed current, Amperian loops, solenoids and toroids before the quiz.

4 steps

1. What does Ampere's circuital law say?

The circulation of the magnetic field around any closed path equals μ₀ times the net current passing through a surface bounded by that path.

\(\displaystyle\oint\vec B\cdot d\vec l=\mu_0 I_{\rm enclosed}\)
Important: The law is valid for steady currents and any closed path. High symmetry makes it useful for calculating B.

2. Apply it to a long straight wire

Choose a circular Amperian loop of radius r centred on the wire. By symmetry, B is tangential and has the same magnitude everywhere on the circle.

\(B(2\pi r)=\mu_0 I\quad\Rightarrow\quad B=\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}\)

Thus, for a long straight wire, field strength is directly proportional to current and inversely proportional to distance.

3. Solenoid and toroid

Long solenoid

Inside the ideal long solenoid, the field is nearly uniform and parallel to its axis.

\(B=\mu_0 nI\)

n = turns per unit length.

Ideal toroid

Inside the core at radius r, the field follows circular paths around the toroid.

\(B=\dfrac{\mu_0 NI}{2\pi r}\)

Outside an ideal toroid, the field is approximately zero.

4. Interactive experiment

Change the current and radius of an ideal toroid to see how its magnetic field changes.

\(B=\dfrac{\mu_0NI}{2\pi r}\)
Calculated field inside toroid: 4.00 mT

Increase N or I → field increases. Increase r → field decreases.

Worked example: A long solenoid has 1000 turns per metre and carries 2 A. Its internal field is \(B=4\pi\times10^{-7}\times1000\times2=2.51\times10^{-3}\,T\).
