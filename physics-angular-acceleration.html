Angular Acceleration

Learn how quickly angular velocity changes, how to calculate it, and how it produces tangential acceleration.

ROTATIONAL MOTIONSHORT LESSON

1. Meaning

Angular acceleration (α) is the time rate of change of angular velocity. It is a vector directed along the rotation axis.

αavg = (ω − ω₀) / Δt

Instantaneous angular acceleration is α = dω/dt. SI unit: rad s⁻².

2. Sign of angular acceleration

Choose a positive rotation direction first. If anticlockwise is positive, a change toward more positive angular velocity gives positive α. Negative α does not always mean slowing down; compare the signs of ω and α.

ω and α same sign → speed increases
ω and α opposite signs → speed decreases (at that instant)

3. Rotational equations (constant α)

These equations are used when angular acceleration is constant.

ω = ω₀ + αt
θ = ω₀t + ½αt²
ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ

4. Angular and tangential acceleration

A point at radius r has tangential acceleration when angular velocity changes. Its magnitude is proportional to radius.

at = rα

The radial (centripetal) acceleration is separate: ar = rω². It changes the direction of velocity, while tangential acceleration changes its magnitude.

5. Worked example

A wheel speeds up from 2 rad s⁻¹ to 10 rad s⁻¹ in 4 s. Find average angular acceleration.

α = (10 − 2) / 4 = 2 rad s⁻²

At radius 0.5 m, the tangential acceleration is aₜ = 0.5 × 2 = 1 m s⁻².

Quick check

A fan slows from 20 rad s⁻¹ to 8 rad s⁻¹ in 3 s. Find its average angular acceleration.

α = (8 − 20) / 3 = −4 rad s⁻². The negative sign indicates the angular velocity is decreasing in the chosen positive direction.
