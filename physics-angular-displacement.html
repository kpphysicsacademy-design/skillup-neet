Angular Displacement

Measure how much an object rotates and in which direction.

ROTATIONAL MOTIONSHORT LESSON

1. What is angular displacement?

Angular displacement is the change in angular position of a rotating object. It tells us the angle through which the object has turned, along with its direction.

Δθ = θfinal − θinitial

The SI unit is the radian (rad). Anticlockwise rotation is usually positive and clockwise rotation negative.

2. Degree and radian

A complete revolution is 360°, equal to 2π radians.

180° = π rad

Degrees to radians: multiply by π/180. Radians to degrees: multiply by 180/π.

3. Common angles

45° = π/4 rad
60° = π/3 rad
90° = π/2 rad
180° = π rad
270° = 3π/2 rad
360° = 2π rad

4. Worked example

A wheel turns from 2 rad to 7 rad. Find its angular displacement.

Δθ = 7 − 2 = +5 rad

The positive sign indicates anticlockwise rotation under the usual sign convention.

5. Remember

Angular displacement is a signed quantity. It depends on initial and final angular positions, not on the total path. One complete turn has a displacement of 2π rad, even though the final orientation matches the initial orientation.

Quick check

A disc rotates 90° clockwise. If anticlockwise is positive, what is Δθ?

−π/2 rad. Clockwise is negative, and 90° = π/2 rad.
