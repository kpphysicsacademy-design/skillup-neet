ROTATIONAL MOTION • CONCEPT GUIDE

Angular Momentum of a Rigid Body

Understand how a rotating body's mass distribution and angular velocity determine its angular momentum, and how external torque changes it.

Fixed-axis rotationMoment of inertiaTorque relationConservation
01 • CORE IDEA

Angular momentum of a rigid body

A rigid body consists of particles whose relative positions remain fixed. During rotation, each particle carries angular momentum about the chosen axis. The body's total angular momentum is the vector sum of the contributions from all its particles.

L = Iω

For rotation about a fixed principal or symmetry axis, I is the moment of inertia about that axis and ω is angular velocity.

Key idea: Moment of inertia describes how strongly the body's mass is distributed away from the rotation axis. A larger I gives larger angular momentum at the same angular speed.
02 • PARTICLE MODEL

Adding the contributions of all particles

For particles of masses mᵢ at perpendicular distances rᵢ from a fixed rotation axis, each particle contributes mᵢrᵢ²ω.

L = Σ(mᵢrᵢ²ω) = Iω
I = Σmᵢrᵢ²

For a continuous body, the sum becomes an integral: I = ∫r² dm. The distance is measured perpendicular to the selected axis.

Mass farther from the axis contributes more strongly because distance is squared.
03 • STANDARD BODIES

Moment of inertia controls angular momentum

  • Thin ring about central perpendicular axis: I = MR², so L = MR²ω.
  • Solid disc about central perpendicular axis: I = ½MR², so L = ½MR²ω.
  • Solid sphere about a diameter: I = ⅖MR², so L = ⅖MR²ω.
  • Uniform rod about its centre, perpendicular to length: I = ¹⁄₁₂ML².

For the same mass, radius and angular speed, the mass distribution determines which body has the larger angular momentum.

04 • VECTOR DIRECTION

Direction of angular momentum

For rotation about a principal symmetry axis, the angular momentum vector is along the rotation axis. Use the right-hand rule: curl the fingers in the direction of rotation; the thumb gives the positive axis direction.

⊙
Out of the pageVector points towards you
⊗
Into the pageVector points away from you
In general three-dimensional rotation, L and ω need not be parallel. The simple L = Iω relation is directly applicable along a fixed principal axis.
05 • TORQUE

External torque changes angular momentum

τnet = dL/dt

The net external torque about a fixed reference point equals the rate of change of total angular momentum about that point. For a fixed axis and constant I:

τnet = Iα

A torque applied for a time interval produces an angular impulse:

ΔL = τnet Δt
06 • CONSERVATION

Conservation of angular momentum

When the net external torque on a system is zero, its total angular momentum remains constant.

I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂

If the moment of inertia decreases, angular speed increases to keep angular momentum unchanged. This is seen when a spinning skater pulls their arms inward.

Remember: Angular momentum can be conserved even when rotational kinetic energy changes. Internal work can change the system's rotational kinetic energy while total angular momentum stays constant.
07 • SOLVED EXAMPLES

Apply the relationship

Example 1 — Find angular momentum

A disc has I = 0.8 kg m² and rotates at 5 rad s⁻¹.

  1. Use L = Iω.
  2. L = 0.8 × 5.
  3. Answer: 4 kg m² s⁻¹.
Example 2 — Conservation

A body changes I from 6 kg m² to 2 kg m². Its initial angular speed is 3 rad s⁻¹ and external torque is zero.

  1. I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂.
  2. ω₂ = (6 × 3) / 2.
  3. Answer: 9 rad s⁻¹.
Example 3 — Angular impulse

A constant external torque of 4 N m acts for 2 s.

  1. ΔL = τΔt.
  2. ΔL = 4 × 2.
  3. Answer: 8 kg m² s⁻¹.
08 • EXAM CHECKPOINT

Common mistakes

  • Using the wrong axis when calculating moment of inertia.
  • Forgetting that I depends on mass distribution relative to the selected axis.
  • Applying L = Iω as a universal vector identity for arbitrary 3D rotation.
  • Using conservation when a net external torque acts.
  • Assuming rotational kinetic energy must also be conserved whenever angular momentum is conserved.
Quick memory: Fixed principal axis → L = Iω. Net external torque zero → total angular momentum conserved.
