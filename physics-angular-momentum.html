ROTATIONAL MOTION • CONCEPT GUIDE

Angular Momentum

Understand the rotational effect of motion about a point or axis, connect it with torque, and apply conservation principles to solve problems.

Vector conceptKey formulasSolved examplesExam revision
01 • MEANING

What is angular momentum?

Angular momentum is the rotational equivalent of linear momentum. For a particle, it describes the moment of its linear momentum about a chosen reference point.

L = r × p

Here r is the position vector from the reference point to the particle, and p = mv is its linear momentum. Angular momentum is a vector quantity.

Think about this: A moving particle has angular momentum about a point when its line of motion does not pass through that point. The same particle can have different angular momentum about different reference points.
02 • MAGNITUDE & DIRECTION

Magnitude of angular momentum

|L| = rp sin θ = rmv sin θ

θ is the angle between the position vector r and momentum vector p. Only the component of momentum perpendicular to r contributes.

The magnitude depends on the perpendicular separation between the reference point and the particle's line of motion.

  • θ = 0° or 180°: L = 0; r and p are parallel or antiparallel.
  • θ = 90°: L is maximum and equals rp = rmv.
  • Direction: perpendicular to the plane containing r and p, found by the right-hand rule.
If a particle moves directly toward or away from the reference point, its angular momentum about that point is zero.
03 • ANGLE DEPENDENCE

How does angle affect angular momentum?

For fixed r, m and v, angular momentum varies with sin θ.

0°L = 0Parallel vectors
30°L = ½rmvHalf maximum
90°L = rmvMaximum
180°L = 0Antiparallel

Changing the angle changes the perpendicular component of momentum, not the mass or speed of the particle.

04 • RIGID BODY ROTATION

Angular momentum of a rotating body

For a rigid body rotating about a fixed principal axis, angular momentum is given by:

L = Iω
  • I — moment of inertia about the axis (kg m²).
  • ω — angular velocity (rad s⁻¹).
  • SI unit of L — kg m² s⁻¹.
  • Dimensions — [M L² T⁻¹].
Important: In general three-dimensional rotation, angular momentum and angular velocity need not be parallel. The simple scalar relation L = Iω applies directly to fixed-axis rotation about a principal axis.
05 • TORQUE RELATION

Torque changes angular momentum

The net external torque about a reference point is equal to the time rate of change of angular momentum about that point.

τnet = dL/dt

For constant net torque over a time interval:

ΔL = τnet Δt

A non-zero external torque changes the magnitude, direction or both of the angular momentum vector.

06 • CONSERVATION LAW

Conservation of angular momentum

If the net external torque acting on a system is zero, its total angular momentum remains constant.

Linitial = Lfinal

For a rigid body rotating about the same fixed axis:

I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂
Skater example: When a skater pulls their arms inward, moment of inertia decreases. With negligible external torque, angular speed increases so that angular momentum remains conserved.
07 • SOLVED NUMERICALS

Step-by-step examples

Example 1 — Fixed-axis rotation

A wheel has moment of inertia 2 kg m² and angular velocity 3 rad s⁻¹. Find its angular momentum.

  1. Use L = Iω.
  2. L = 2 × 3.
  3. Answer: 6 kg m² s⁻¹.
Example 2 — Moving particle

A 2 kg particle moves at 4 m s⁻¹ perpendicular to a position vector of length 3 m. Find angular momentum magnitude.

  1. Use L = rmv sin θ.
  2. θ = 90°, so sin θ = 1.
  3. L = 3 × 2 × 4 × 1.
  4. Answer: 24 kg m² s⁻¹.
Example 3 — Conservation

A rotating body changes its moment of inertia from 6 kg m² to 2 kg m². Its initial angular speed is 2 rad s⁻¹. Find final angular speed if external torque is zero.

  1. Use I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂.
  2. ω₂ = (6 × 2) / 2.
  3. Answer: 6 rad s⁻¹.
08 • EXAM CHECKPOINT

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting that angular momentum depends on the chosen reference point.
  • Using cos θ instead of sin θ when θ is the angle between r and p.
  • Confusing torque (N m) with angular momentum (kg m² s⁻¹).
  • Applying L = Iω as a general vector identity for every 3D rotation.
  • Using conservation when a net external torque acts on the system.
Remember: External torque changes total angular momentum. Zero net external torque means total angular momentum is conserved.
CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

Quick concept checks

1. A 3 kg particle moves at 5 m s⁻¹ perpendicular to a 2 m position vector. Find its angular momentum magnitude.

Reveal answer

30 kg m² s⁻¹. L = rmv = 2 × 3 × 5.

2. What happens to angular speed when moment of inertia decreases and no external torque acts?

Reveal answer

Angular speed increases. The product Iω remains constant for fixed-axis rotation.

3. When is a particle's angular momentum zero about a point?

Reveal answer

When r and p are parallel or antiparallel. Their cross product is zero.
