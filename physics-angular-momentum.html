Angular Momentum

Understand the rotational effect of motion with concepts, formulas and solved examples.

Concept notesKey formulasSolved examples

1. Meaning of angular momentum

Angular momentum describes the rotational effect of motion about a chosen point. It depends on position and linear momentum.

L = r × p

Here r is the position vector and p = mv is linear momentum. Angular momentum is a vector quantity.

2. Magnitude and direction

L = rp sin θ = rmv sin θ

θ is the angle between r and p. Only the perpendicular component contributes.

  • At 0° or 180°, L = 0.
  • At 90°, L is maximum and equals rp.
  • Direction is perpendicular to the plane of r and p, given by the right-hand rule.
Motion directly toward or away from the reference point gives zero angular momentum about that point.

3. Rotating rigid body

For rotation about a fixed principal axis:

L = Iω

I is moment of inertia (kg m²) and ω is angular velocity (rad s⁻¹). In general 3D rotation, L and ω need not be parallel.

4. SI unit and dimensions

kg m² s⁻¹

Dimensional formula: [M L² T⁻¹]. Do not confuse angular momentum with torque, whose unit is N m.

5. Torque and angular momentum

τnet = dL/dt

Net external torque equals the rate of change of angular momentum. For constant torque, ΔL = τΔt.

6. Conservation of angular momentum

When net external torque is zero, total angular momentum remains constant.

I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂

A skater pulling their arms inward reduces moment of inertia, so angular speed increases to conserve angular momentum.

7. Solved examples

Example 1: I = 2 kg m² and ω = 3 rad s⁻¹. L = Iω = 2 × 3 = 6 kg m² s⁻¹.
Example 2: A 2 kg particle moves at 4 m s⁻¹ perpendicular to a 3 m radius. L = rmv = 3 × 2 × 4 = 24 kg m² s⁻¹.
Example 3: I changes from 6 to 2 kg m², initial ω = 2 rad s⁻¹. ω₂ = (6 × 2)/2 = 6 rad s⁻¹.

8. Quick recap

Net external torque changes angular momentum. If external torque is zero, angular momentum is conserved. Explore the simulator and then test yourself in Practice.
