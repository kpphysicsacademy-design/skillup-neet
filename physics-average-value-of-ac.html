〰️ Average Value of AC

Understand the mean value of alternating current and voltage, derive the half-cycle result, and distinguish it from the zero average over a complete cycle.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations
Question 1 of 300%

1. What is average value?

The average value is the arithmetic mean of the instantaneous values over a specified time interval. For AC, the interval must be stated because the mean over a complete symmetric sine-wave cycle differs from the mean over one half-cycle.

\[X_{\mathrm{avg}}=\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T x(t)\,dt\]
Always identify whether the question asks for a half-cycle, full cycle, or rectified waveform.

2. Average value over a complete cycle

For a sinusoidal voltage \(v=V_0\sin\omega t\), the positive half-cycle area and negative half-cycle area are equal in magnitude and opposite in sign.

\[V_{\mathrm{avg,full}}=\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T V_0\sin\omega t\,dt=0\]

The same result applies to a sinusoidal current. The algebraic average over one complete cycle is zero.

3. Average value over a positive half-cycle

For the positive half-cycle, integrate from \(0\) to \(\pi\) in phase. The time interval is \(T/2\).

\[V_{\mathrm{avg}}=\frac{2}{T}\int_0^{T/2}V_0\sin\omega t\,dt\]

Using \(\omega=2\pi/T\), the integral of the sine over the positive half-cycle gives:

\[V_{\mathrm{avg}}=\frac{2V_0}{\pi}\approx0.637V_0\]

4. Average current and voltage

The same mathematical result applies to current and voltage because both follow the sinusoidal form.

\[I_{\mathrm{avg}}=\frac{2I_0}{\pi}=0.637I_0\]
\[V_{\mathrm{avg}}=\frac{2V_0}{\pi}=0.637V_0\]

Here, \(I_0\) and \(V_0\) are peak values. These are half-cycle averages, not full-cycle algebraic averages.

5. Full-wave rectified average

A full-wave rectifier reverses the negative half-cycle so that both halves contribute positively to the output. The average output over a complete input cycle therefore equals the mean magnitude of the sine wave.

\[V_{\mathrm{dc}}=\frac{2V_0}{\pi}\]

For an ideal full-wave rectified sinusoid, the average current is similarly \(2I_0/\pi\).

6. Half-wave rectified average

A half-wave rectifier passes one half-cycle and blocks the other. Its output is zero during the blocked half, so its average over the full input period is half the positive-half-cycle average.

\[V_{\mathrm{dc}}=\frac{V_0}{\pi}\approx0.318V_0\]
Do not confuse half-wave rectified average (\(V_0/\pi\)) with the positive half-cycle average (\(2V_0/\pi\)).

7. Worked numerical examples

Example 1: If peak voltage is 100 V, the positive half-cycle average is:

\[V_{\mathrm{avg}}=\frac{2(100)}{\pi}=63.7\\,\mathrm V\]

Example 2: If the half-cycle average current is 6.37 A, then:

\[I_0=\frac{\pi(6.37)}{2}\approx10\\,\mathrm A\]

Example 3: A half-wave rectifier has a 100 V peak input:

\[V_{\mathrm{dc}}=\frac{100}{\pi}=31.8\\,\mathrm V\]

8. Quick comparison and exam tips

  • Pure sine wave, full-cycle algebraic average: zero.
  • Positive half-cycle average: \(2X_0/\pi\approx0.637X_0\).
  • Full-wave rectified average over a full period: \(2X_0/\pi\).
  • Half-wave rectified average over a full period: \(X_0/\pi\approx0.318X_0\).
  • RMS value is a different quantity: for a sine wave it is \(X_0/\sqrt2\).
Exam check: read the interval and waveform before selecting a formula.
