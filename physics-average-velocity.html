Average Velocity

A quick lesson: learn the formula, meaning and one example.

📘 Learn🎮 Simulator🧠 Practice

📌 Quick Cheat Sheet

Average velocity is total displacement divided by elapsed time. It is a vector, measured in m/s (dimension [LT⁻¹]).

ConceptRule
1D average velocityv̄ = (x₂ − x₁) / Δt
Multi-leg journeyNet displacement / total time
Average speedTotal distance / total time
Round tripAverage velocity = 0 for Δt > 0
1D signNegative = opposite to chosen positive axis

Example: x₁ = −5 m, x₂ = +7 m, Δt = 3 s → displacement = +12 m; average velocity = +4 m/s.

Out-and-back: 20 m east, 20 m west in 10 s → average velocity 0 m/s, average speed 4 m/s.

NEET tip: use net displacement, not the sum of distances; the time interval must be positive. In 2D, divide each displacement component by elapsed time.

1. Meaning

Average velocity is displacement divided by total time. It tells how fast position changes and in which direction.

Average velocity = Displacement ÷ Time

SI unit: m/s • Dimension: [LT⁻¹] • It is a vector.

2. Formula

v̄ = (x₂ − x₁) ÷ Δt

Final position minus initial position, divided by elapsed time.

3. Simple example

A student moves from 5 m to 25 m in 4 s.
Displacement = 25 − 5 = 20 m
Average velocity = 20 ÷ 4 = 5 m/s.

Remember

  • Use displacement, not distance.
  • Negative velocity means opposite direction.
  • On a round trip, average velocity is zero.
