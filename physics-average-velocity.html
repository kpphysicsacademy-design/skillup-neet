CORE CONCEPT

What is average velocity?

Average velocity is the total displacement of an object divided by the total time interval. It describes the overall change in position per unit time, with direction.

Average velocity = Net displacement ÷ Total time
Displacement (Δx)

Final position minus initial position. It is a vector and depends only on the start and end positions.

Elapsed time (Δt)

Final time minus initial time. Use the complete time interval, including pauses if the question includes them.

Vector quantity

Average velocity has magnitude and direction. Its sign depends on the positive direction chosen.

SI unit

Metre per second (m/s). Dimensional formula: [LT⁻¹].

FORMULA

Mathematical expression

v̄ = Δx / Δt = (x₂ − x₁) / (t₂ − t₁)

For a positive time interval, the sign of average velocity is the same as the sign of net displacement.

Important distinction: Average velocity uses displacement, not total distance. Average speed uses total distance travelled divided by total time.
WORKED EXAMPLE 1

Motion in one direction

A student moves from 5 m to 25 m in 4 s.
Displacement = 25 − 5 = 20 m
Average velocity = 20 ÷ 4 = 5 m/s in the positive direction.
WORKED EXAMPLE 2

Round-trip motion

A runner completes one lap and returns to the starting point in 80 s.
Net displacement = 0 m
Average velocity = 0 ÷ 80 = 0 m/s.
The average speed is not zero because the runner covered a distance.
EXAM CHECKPOINTS

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Do not substitute total distance for displacement.
  • For multi-stage motion, add all signed displacements and divide by total elapsed time.
  • A negative answer indicates direction opposite to the chosen positive axis.
  • Zero average velocity does not mean the object never moved.
