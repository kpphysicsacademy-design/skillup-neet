📏 Coefficient of Friction

The coefficient of friction tells us how strongly two contacting surfaces resist sliding. It is a ratio, not a force.

μ = friction force ÷ normal reaction

1. Two coefficients

Static coefficient (μₛ)

Used before sliding begins. At the limiting condition, fₛ,max = μₛN.

Kinetic coefficient (μₖ)

Used when the surfaces are already sliding. fₖ = μₖN.

2. Important formulas

fₛ,max = μₛN
fₖ = μₖN
  • μ is dimensionless because it is a ratio of two forces.
  • For many dry surfaces, μₛ is greater than μₖ (not a universal rule).
  • On a horizontal surface with no other vertical force, N = mg.
  • At the angle of repose, μₛ = tan θ.

3. Worked example

Given: friction force = 12 N and normal reaction = 40 N.
Coefficient: μ = 12 ÷ 40 = 0.30.
The coefficient has no unit.

4. Remember

Static friction adjusts to the force applied and can be smaller than μₛN. The value μₛN is the maximum static friction, not the friction acting at every moment.

The coefficient depends on the materials and condition of the contacting surfaces. In the ideal Coulomb model, it does not depend on the apparent contact area.
