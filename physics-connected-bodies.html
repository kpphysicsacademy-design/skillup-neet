📏 Connected Bodies

When two or more objects are linked by a string, their motion is connected. Solve each body using Newton’s laws and apply the string constraint.

Newton’s lawsTensionIdeal pulley

1. Core ideas

  • An ideal string is light, inextensible and remains taut.
  • A light string over a smooth, massless pulley has the same tension throughout.
  • For two bodies connected by a taut string over a fixed pulley, acceleration magnitudes are equal.
  • Tension pulls along the string; a string cannot push.

2. Atwood machine

Two hanging masses m₁ and m₂ are connected over an ideal fixed pulley. If m₂ is heavier, it accelerates downward.

a = (m₂ − m₁)g / (m₁ + m₂)
T = 2m₁m₂g / (m₁ + m₂)
Example: m₁ = 4 kg, m₂ = 6 kg, g = 10 m/s².
a = (6 − 4) × 10 / 10 = 2 m/s².
T = 4(10 + 2) = 48 N.

3. Block on a smooth table + hanging mass

A block m on a smooth horizontal table is connected to a hanging mass M.

a = Mg / (m + M)
T = ma = mMg / (m + M)
Example: m = 4 kg, M = 1 kg, g = 10 m/s².
a = 1 × 10 / 5 = 2 m/s²; T = 4 × 2 = 8 N.

4. Two blocks on a smooth horizontal surface

If an external horizontal force F pulls two connected blocks of masses m₁ and m₂ together, treat them as one system first.

a = F / (m₁ + m₂)

Then isolate one block to calculate the internal tension. Internal tension forces cancel when the two blocks are treated as one system.

5. Reliable problem-solving method

  1. Draw a separate free-body diagram for every body.
  2. Choose a positive direction consistent with the expected motion.
  3. Write ΣF = ma for each body.
  4. Use equal acceleration magnitudes for bodies linked by one taut string over a fixed ideal pulley.
  5. Solve the simultaneous equations and check signs and units.

Remember

The equal-tension rule depends on the ideal string and pulley assumptions. In real systems, pulley inertia, friction or string mass can make tensions differ. For a slack string, tension is zero until it becomes taut.
