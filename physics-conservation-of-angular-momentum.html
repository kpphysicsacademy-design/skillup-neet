ROTATIONAL MOTION • CONCEPT GUIDE

Conservation of Angular Momentum

Understand why spinning objects change their rotation speed when their mass distribution changes, and learn when angular momentum stays constant.

Core principleEquationsReal-life examplesExam revision
01 • PRINCIPLE

What is conservation of angular momentum?

The total angular momentum of a system remains constant if the net external torque acting on the system is zero.

τexternal, net = 0   ⇒   Linitial = Lfinal

Internal torques can redistribute angular momentum between parts of a system, but they do not change the total angular momentum of an isolated system.

Key idea: No net external torque means no change in total angular momentum—not necessarily no rotation.
02 • MATHEMATICAL FORM

Conservation equation

For a rigid body rotating about the same fixed axis, angular momentum is L = Iω. Therefore:

I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂
  • I₁, I₂ — initial and final moments of inertia (kg m²).
  • ω₁, ω₂ — initial and final angular velocities (rad s⁻¹).

This scalar form assumes rotation about the same fixed axis. In general 3D motion, use conservation of the angular momentum vector.

03 • WHY SPEED CHANGES

Moment of inertia and angular speed

If moment of inertia decreases, angular speed increases to keep angular momentum constant. If moment of inertia increases, angular speed decreases.

I decreasesω increasesSpin faster
I increasesω decreasesSpin slower

For example, a skater pulls their arms inward. More mass moves closer to the rotation axis, reducing I and increasing ω.

04 • REAL-LIFE APPLICATIONS

Where do we observe it?

  • Figure skater: Pulling arms inward increases spin rate; extending arms slows the spin.
  • Diver: Tucking the body reduces moment of inertia and increases rotation rate during a somersault.
  • Planetary motion: A planet moving under a central gravitational force has zero torque about the Sun, so its angular momentum about the Sun is conserved.
  • Rotating stool: A person holding weights changes their mass distribution and spin rate by moving the weights inward or outward.
05 • TORQUE CONNECTION

When is angular momentum not conserved?

The rate of change of angular momentum equals the net external torque:

τnet external = dL/dt

If external torque acts over a time interval, angular momentum changes. For constant torque:

ΔL = τnet Δt
Remember: Choose the system and reference point carefully. Conservation applies when the net external torque about that point is zero (or negligible).
06 • SOLVED NUMERICALS

Step-by-step examples

Example 1 — Skater pulls arms inward

A skater has I₁ = 4 kg m² and ω₁ = 2 rad s⁻¹. Their moment of inertia becomes 1.6 kg m². Find the final angular speed.

  1. I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂.
  2. ω₂ = (4 × 2) / 1.6.
  3. Answer: 5 rad s⁻¹.
Example 2 — Moment of inertia doubles

A rotor has I₁ = 3 kg m² and ω₁ = 8 rad s⁻¹. If I₂ = 6 kg m², find ω₂.

  1. ω₂ = I₁ω₁ / I₂.
  2. ω₂ = (3 × 8) / 6.
  3. Answer: 4 rad s⁻¹.
Example 3 — Find initial angular momentum

A body has I = 2.5 kg m² and ω = 6 rad s⁻¹. Find its angular momentum.

  1. L = Iω = 2.5 × 6.
  2. Answer: 15 kg m² s⁻¹.
07 • EXAM CHECKPOINT

Common mistakes

  • Assuming angular momentum is conserved even when a net external torque acts.
  • Thinking angular speed stays constant when moment of inertia changes.
  • Using I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂ without checking that the rotation axis and system assumptions are appropriate.
  • Confusing conservation of angular momentum with conservation of rotational kinetic energy. Kinetic energy can change.
  • Ignoring units: I is in kg m² and ω in rad s⁻¹.
Memory rule: With zero net external torque, Iω stays constant for fixed-axis rotation.
CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

Quick concept checks

1. A skater's moment of inertia changes from 5 to 2 kg m². Initial angular speed is 3 rad s⁻¹. Find final angular speed.

Reveal answer

7.5 rad s⁻¹. ω₂ = (5 × 3) / 2.

2. What condition is required for total angular momentum to remain constant?

Reveal answer

Zero net external torque about the chosen reference point.

3. Does conservation of angular momentum always mean rotational kinetic energy is constant?

Reveal answer

No. Internal work can change rotational kinetic energy while total angular momentum remains conserved.
