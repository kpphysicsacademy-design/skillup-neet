1. Law of conservation of momentum

The total linear momentum of an isolated system remains constant when the net external force (or external impulse) on the system is zero. Momentum may transfer between objects, but the system total does not change.

Total momentum before = Total momentum after

For two bodies moving along one straight line, include signs to represent their directions.

2. Momentum of a system

SystemChoose which objects are included in the system before applying conservation.
External forceMomentum is conserved when the net external impulse is zero or negligible during the interaction.
Internal forcesForces between objects inside the system occur in equal-and-opposite pairs.
DirectionChoose a positive direction and use positive or negative velocities consistently.

3. Two-body collision equation

For two objects interacting in one dimension, if external impulse is negligible:

m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂

Here u₁, u₂ are initial velocities and v₁, v₂ are final velocities. This equation applies to both elastic and inelastic collisions.

4. Perfectly inelastic collision

In a perfectly inelastic collision, the objects stick together and move with a common final velocity. Total momentum is conserved, but kinetic energy is generally not conserved.

v = (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂) / (m₁ + m₂)
Example: A 2 kg cart at 4 m/s hits a stationary 2 kg cart and they stick. v = (2×4 + 2×0)/(2+2) = 2 m/s in the original direction.

5. Elastic and inelastic collisions

Elastic collisionTotal momentum and total kinetic energy are conserved.
Inelastic collisionTotal momentum is conserved in an isolated system, but some kinetic energy changes into other forms.
Perfectly inelasticObjects stick together after collision; kinetic energy loss is maximum for the given initial conditions.
Explosion / recoilParts move in opposite directions while the system's total momentum remains unchanged if external impulse is negligible.

6. Recoil and explosions

If a system starts at rest, its initial total momentum is zero. After an internal explosion or a gun fires a bullet, the parts acquire equal and opposite momenta so the vector sum remains zero.

A 4 kg object at rest breaks into 1 kg and 3 kg pieces. If the 1 kg piece moves right at 8 m/s, then 8 + 3v = 0, so the 3 kg piece moves left at 8/3 m/s.

7. Worked example

Question: A 1 kg trolley moving right at 6 m/s couples with a stationary 2 kg trolley. Find their common velocity.
Initial momentum: 1×6 + 2×0 = 6 kg m/s.
Total mass: 1 + 2 = 3 kg.
Final velocity: v = 6/3 = 2 m/s right.

8. Exam reminders

  • Conserve total momentum only after defining the system and checking external impulse.
  • Momentum is a vector; assign signs before substituting.
  • Momentum is conserved in elastic and inelastic collisions of an isolated system.
  • Kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic collisions.
  • For objects that stick together, use their combined mass and common final velocity.
  • For recoil and explosions from rest, final momenta must add vectorially to zero.
