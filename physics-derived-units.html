1. Meaning of derived units

A derived unit is obtained by combining SI base units through multiplication, division or powers. It measures quantities such as area, speed, force or energy.

Derived unit = product of powers of base units

2. Area and volume

Area is the product of two lengths; volume is the product of three lengths.

  • Area: square metre (m²) = m × m
  • Volume: cubic metre (m³) = m × m × m
Dimensions: L² for area and L³ for volume.

3. Velocity and acceleration

Velocity is displacement per unit time. Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.

  • Velocity: m s⁻¹; [LT⁻¹]
  • Acceleration: m s⁻²; [LT⁻²]

4. Force and momentum

Newton’s second law gives force as mass multiplied by acceleration. Momentum is mass multiplied by velocity.

  • Force: newton (N) = kg m s⁻²; [MLT⁻²]
  • Momentum: kg m s⁻¹; [MLT⁻¹]
  • Impulse: N s = kg m s⁻¹

5. Work, energy and power

  • Work/energy: joule (J) = N m = kg m² s⁻²
  • Power: watt (W) = J s⁻¹ = kg m² s⁻³

Work and energy have the same dimensions; power is the rate of energy transfer.

6. Pressure and density

  • Pressure = force/area; pascal (Pa) = N m⁻² = kg m⁻¹ s⁻²
  • Density = mass/volume; kg m⁻³
One pascal is one newton per square metre.

7. Electrical and other derived units

  • Charge: coulomb (C) = A s
  • Potential difference: volt (V) = kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹
  • Resistance: ohm (Ω) = kg m² s⁻³ A⁻²
  • Frequency: hertz (Hz) = s⁻¹

8. Named units and dimensional checking

Named derived SI units can always be expanded into base units. Dimensional analysis helps check equations, but cannot determine dimensionless numerical constants.

1 J = 1 N m = 1 kg m² s⁻²
