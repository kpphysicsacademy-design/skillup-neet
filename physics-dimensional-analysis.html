Dimensional Analysis

Understand physical dimensions, test equations and solve problems using dimensional methods.

1. What is dimensional analysis?

Dimensional analysis represents physical quantities in terms of fundamental dimensions and uses them to examine physical relationships.

[Q] = Mᵃ Lᵇ Tᶜ

M represents mass, L represents length and T represents time. The powers indicate how the quantity depends on each fundamental dimension.

2. Fundamental dimensions

In mechanics, most quantities can be expressed using three fundamental dimensions:

  • Mass: [M]
  • Length: [L]
  • Time: [T]

Other SI base dimensions include electric current [I], temperature [Θ], amount of substance [N] and luminous intensity [J].

4. Deriving dimensional formulae

Force

From Newton's second law, F = ma.

[F] = [M][LT⁻²] = [MLT⁻²]

Pressure

Pressure = Force / Area.

[P] = [MLT⁻²] / [L²] = [ML⁻¹T⁻²]

5. Principle of dimensional homogeneity

A physically meaningful equation must have identical dimensions on both sides. All terms added or subtracted must also have the same dimensions.

[LHS] = [RHS]

For s = ut + ½at², each term has dimension L: [s] = L, [ut] = (LT⁻¹)(T) = L and [at²] = (LT⁻²)(T²) = L.

6. Applications

  1. Checking dimensional consistency of equations.
  2. Deriving relations between physical quantities up to a dimensionless constant.
  3. Converting units between measurement systems.
  4. Finding dimensions of physical constants and coefficients.

7. Limitations

  • It cannot determine pure numerical constants such as 2, ½ or π.
  • It cannot determine the plus or minus sign in an equation.
  • It cannot distinguish quantities with identical dimensions, such as work and torque.
  • It cannot establish the complete functional form in every physical relation.
A dimensionally correct equation may still be physically incorrect. Dimensional analysis is a consistency test, not a complete proof.
