Dimensional Analysis

Understand physical dimensions, test equations and use dimensional methods to solve physics problems.

1. What is dimensional analysis?

Dimensional analysis is the method of representing physical quantities in terms of fundamental dimensions and using them to examine physical relationships.

[Q] = Mᵃ Lᵇ Tᶜ

Here, M represents mass, L represents length and T represents time. The exponents a, b and c describe the dimensional dependence of Q.

2. Fundamental dimensions

In mechanics, most quantities can be expressed using mass, length and time.

  • Mass: [M]
  • Length: [L]
  • Time: [T]

Other SI base quantities include electric current [I], thermodynamic temperature [Θ], amount of substance [N] and luminous intensity [J].

4. How to obtain a dimensional formula

Start from the defining equation and substitute the dimensions of each quantity.

Example: Force

Newton's second law gives F = ma.

[F] = [M][LT⁻²] = [MLT⁻²]

Example: Pressure

Pressure = Force / Area.

[P] = [MLT⁻²] / [L²] = [ML⁻¹T⁻²]

5. Principle of dimensional homogeneity

A physically meaningful equation must have identical dimensions on both sides. Every term that is added or subtracted must also have the same dimensions.

[LHS] = [RHS]

For s = ut + ½at², each term has dimension L:

  • [s] = L
  • [ut] = (LT⁻¹)(T) = L
  • [at²] = (LT⁻²)(T²) = L

6. Applications of dimensional analysis

  1. Checking dimensional consistency of equations.
  2. Deriving relations between physical quantities up to a dimensionless constant.
  3. Converting units from one system to another.
  4. Finding dimensions of constants and coefficients.

7. Limitations

  • It cannot determine pure numerical constants such as 2, ½ or π.
  • It cannot establish whether an equation has the correct plus or minus sign.
  • It cannot distinguish quantities having the same dimensions, such as work and torque.
  • It is not sufficient for equations involving several independent dimensionless functions.
A dimensionally correct equation may still be physically incorrect. Dimensional analysis is a consistency test, not a complete proof.

8. Exam-ready method

  1. Write the defining relation.
  2. Replace each quantity with its dimensional formula.
  3. Simplify the powers of M, L and T.
  4. Compare dimensions on both sides.
  5. Check that terms joined by addition or subtraction have identical dimensions.
