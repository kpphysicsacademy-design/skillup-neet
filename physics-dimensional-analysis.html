📊 Dimensional Analysis

Understand fundamental dimensions, dimensional formulae, homogeneity and dimensional analysis.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations
Question 1 of 300%

1. What is dimensional analysis?

Dimensional analysis is a method of studying physical relationships by expressing quantities in terms of fundamental dimensions such as M, L and T. It is useful for checking equations and finding possible proportional relations.

[Q] = Mᵃ Lᵇ Tᶜ

2. Principle of dimensional homogeneity

A physically meaningful equation must have the same dimensions on both sides. Every term that is added or subtracted must also have identical dimensions.

s = ut + ½at²

Each term has the dimension of length, so the equation is dimensionally homogeneous.

3. Checking equations by dimensions

Replace each physical quantity with its dimensional formula and simplify. For example, in v² = u² + 2as, both velocity-squared terms and acceleration × displacement have dimensions [L²T⁻²].

If dimensions do not match, the proposed equation is definitely incorrect.

4. Deriving relations using dimensions

Assume a power-law relation, write the dimensions of every variable, and equate the powers of M, L and T.

T ∝ lᵃ gᵇ

For a simple pendulum, dimensional analysis gives a = ½ and b = −½, so T ∝ √(l/g). It cannot determine the numerical factor 2π.

5. Conversion of units

For a quantity with dimensions MᵃLᵇTᶜ, its numerical value changes with the chosen units so that the physical quantity remains unchanged.

  • 1 N = 10⁵ dyne
  • 1 J = 10⁷ erg
  • 1 Pa = 10 dyne cm⁻²

6. Finding dimensions of constants

Use the defining equation and isolate the constant. For example, from F = Gm₁m₂/r², the dimensions of G are [M⁻¹L³T⁻²]. From E = hν, Planck’s constant has dimensions [ML²T⁻¹].

7. Uses of dimensional analysis

  • Test dimensional consistency of equations.
  • Convert physical quantities between unit systems.
  • Find exponents in possible power-law relations.
  • Determine dimensions of physical constants.

8. Limitations

  • It cannot determine dimensionless numerical constants such as 2, π or ½.
  • It cannot establish whether a relation involves addition or subtraction.
  • It cannot distinguish different quantities with the same dimensions, such as work and torque.
  • A dimensionally correct equation may still be physically wrong.
  • It is not sufficient for relations involving many independent dimensionless parameters.
