Dimensional Constants

Understand how physical constants are represented in fundamental dimensions. Start with the defining equation, rearrange it and derive the dimensional formula step by step.

Concept lessonWorked derivationsExam revision

1. What is a dimensional constant?

A physical constant is called dimensional when its dimensional formula is not dimensionless (not equal to M⁰L⁰T⁰I⁰Θ⁰…). Its dimensions can be derived from the physical law or defining equation in which it appears.

[Q] = Mᵃ Lᵇ Tᶜ Iᵈ Θᵉ Nᶠ

M = mass, L = length, T = time, I = electric current, Θ = thermodynamic temperature and N = amount of substance. In SI dimensional notation, the amount-of-substance base dimension is commonly written as mol.

Method: isolate the required constant in its defining equation, replace each quantity with its dimensions, and simplify the powers.

3. Important derivations

Gravitational constant (G)

Newton’s law: F = Gm₁m₂/r². Rearrange: G = Fr²/(m₁m₂).

[G] = (MLT⁻² × L²)/M² = M⁻¹L³T⁻²
Planck constant (h)

Photon energy: E = hν, so h = E/ν. Frequency has dimensions T⁻¹.

[h] = (ML²T⁻²)/(T⁻¹) = ML²T⁻¹
Boltzmann constant (kB)

Thermal energy relation: E = kBT, so divide energy dimensions by temperature.

[kB] = ML²T⁻²Θ⁻¹
Vacuum permittivity (ε₀)

Coulomb’s law gives ε₀ = q²/(4πFr²). Charge has dimensions IT.

[ε₀] = (IT)²/(MLT⁻² × L²) = M⁻¹L⁻³T⁴I²
Vacuum permeability (μ₀)

For a long straight current-carrying wire, B = μ₀I/(2πr). Magnetic field B has dimensions MT⁻²I⁻¹.

[μ₀] = [B][r]/[I] = MLT⁻²I⁻²
Universal gas constant (R)

From PV = nRT, R = PV/(nT). Pressure × volume has dimensions of energy.

[R] = ML²T⁻²Θ⁻¹mol⁻¹
Stefan–Boltzmann constant (σ)

From power per unit area = σT⁴. Power per area has dimensions MT⁻³.

[σ] = MT⁻³Θ⁻⁴
Faraday constant (F)

Faraday constant is charge per mole, F = Nₐe.

[F] = IT mol⁻¹

4. Dimensional and dimensionless constants

Dimensional constantsHave physical dimensions. Examples: G, h, c, kB, e and R.
Dimensionless constantsHave no dimensions. Examples: π, refractive index and coefficient of friction.
“Universal” and “dimensional” describe different properties. A universal constant such as G can also have dimensions.

5. How to solve exam questions

Step 1 — Write the defining equation

Choose the physical law that contains the required constant.

Step 2 — Make the constant the subject

Rearrange the equation before substituting dimensions.

Step 3 — Substitute base dimensions

Use M, L, T, I, Θ and mol consistently.

Step 4 — Simplify powers

Add exponents for multiplication, subtract for division and multiply exponents for powers.

Step 5 — Check the result

Verify that the dimensions agree with the original equation.

6. Common mistakes

Leaving out temperature

Thermal constants such as kB and R include Θ⁻¹.

Mixing up charge and current

Electric charge is current × time, so [Q] = IT.

Forgetting amount of substance

R and Faraday constant are expressed per mole.

Assuming every universal constant is dimensionless

G, h, c and kB all have dimensions.
