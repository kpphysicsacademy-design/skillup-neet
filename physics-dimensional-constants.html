Dimensional Constants

Discover the dimensions of important physical constants. Learn the defining equations, derive each dimensional formula and remember the high-yield results for competitive exams.

Concept guideStep-by-step derivationsExam essentials

1. What is a dimensional constant?

A dimensional constant is a physical constant whose numerical value is associated with a definite set of dimensions. Its dimensions are obtained from the physical equation in which it is defined.

[Q] = Ma Lb Tc Id Θe

Here M, L, T, I and Θ represent mass, length, time, electric current and thermodynamic temperature. Additional base dimensions may be included when required.

Remember: the dimensions of a constant are not found by memorising its unit name. Start from its defining physical relation and rearrange it.

3. Derivations you should know

Gravitational constant (G)

From Newton's law, F = Gm₁m₂/r². Therefore G = Fr²/(m₁m₂).

[G] = (MLT⁻²)(L²)/M² = M⁻¹L³T⁻²
Planck constant (h)

From E = hν, h = E/ν. Frequency has dimensions T⁻¹.

[h] = (ML²T⁻²)/(T⁻¹) = ML²T⁻¹
Boltzmann constant (kB)

From E = kBT, divide energy dimensions by temperature.

[kB] = ML²T⁻²Θ⁻¹
Permittivity of free space (ε₀)

From Coulomb's law, ε₀ = q²/(4πFr²). The numerical factor 4π is dimensionless.

[ε₀] = (IT)² / ((MLT⁻²)(L²)) = M⁻¹L⁻³T⁴I²
Stefan–Boltzmann constant (σ)

From power per unit area = σT⁴. Power per area has dimensions MT⁻³.

[σ] = MT⁻³Θ⁻⁴
Speed of light (c)

From c = distance / time.

[c] = LT⁻¹
Elementary charge (e)

From charge Q = current × time.

[e] = IT
Permeability of free space (μ₀)

From the force per unit length between two long parallel currents, F/l ∝ μ₀I₁I₂/r. Rearranging gives μ₀ dimensions.

[μ₀] = [F][r]/([l][I]²) = MLT⁻²I⁻²
Universal gas constant (R)

From PV = nRT, R = PV/(nT). Pressure × volume has dimensions of energy.

[R] = ML²T⁻²Θ⁻¹mol⁻¹
Faraday constant (F)

Faraday constant is charge per mole: F = NAe.

[F] = IT mol⁻¹
Avogadro constant (Nₐ)

It represents the number of specified entities per mole.

[Nₐ] = mol⁻¹

4. Dimensional constants vs dimensionless constants

Dimensional constantsHave non-zero dimensions, for example G, h, c, kB and e.
Dimensionless constantsHave no dimensions, for example π, refractive index and coefficient of friction.
A pure numerical factor such as 2, 1/2 or 4π is dimensionless. It does not change the dimensional formula.

5. Common mistakes to avoid

Do not omit temperature or amount of substance

Use Θ for thermodynamic temperature and mol for amount of substance when they occur in the relation.

Do not call every universal constant dimensionless

G, h, c and kB are universal constants with dimensions. π and refractive index are dimensionless.

Keep symbols context-specific

F may represent force or the Faraday constant. N may represent newton or Avogadro constant notation in a given context.

6. Quick exam checks

Check the defining equation

Rearrange it to make the required constant the subject.

Substitute dimensions

Replace every physical quantity with its M, L, T, I and Θ dimensions.

Apply powers carefully

For products, add powers. For division, subtract powers. For powers, multiply exponents.

Check consistency

Both sides of a valid physical equation must have the same dimensions.

Do not confuse a dimensional constant with a universal constant: “universal” describes its physical role, while “dimensional” describes its dimensions.
