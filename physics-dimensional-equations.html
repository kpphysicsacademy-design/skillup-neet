1. What is a dimensional equation?

A dimensional equation expresses a physical quantity as a product of powers of fundamental quantities. It shows how the quantity depends on base dimensions.

[Q] = Mᵃ Lᵇ Tᶜ

Here a, b and c are dimensional powers of mass, length and time.

2. Dimensions and dimensional symbols

For mechanics, the main fundamental dimensions are mass (M), length (L) and time (T). Other subjects may also need electric current (I), temperature (Θ), amount of substance and luminous intensity.

A dimensional equation is not the same as a numerical equation or an SI unit expression.

3. Writing dimensional equations

  • Velocity: [v] = [L][T]⁻¹
  • Acceleration: [a] = [L][T]⁻²
  • Force: [F] = [M][L][T]⁻²
  • Momentum: [p] = [M][L][T]⁻¹

4. Dimensional equations of energy and power

  • Work: [W] = [M][L]²[T]⁻²
  • Kinetic energy: [K] = [M][L]²[T]⁻²
  • Power: [P] = [M][L]²[T]⁻³
  • Pressure: [p] = [M][L]⁻¹[T]⁻²

5. Principle of dimensional homogeneity

Every term added or subtracted in a physically meaningful equation must have identical dimensions. The dimensions of the left-hand side and right-hand side must match.

s = ut + ½at²

Each term has dimensions of length, so the equation is dimensionally homogeneous.

6. How to test a dimensional equation

Replace each symbol with its dimensions, simplify each term and compare both sides. If the dimensions differ, the equation is incorrect.

v² = u² + 2as

Each term has dimensions [L²T⁻²], so it passes the dimensional test.

7. Dimensional equations for constants

Use the defining physical relation and rearrange it to isolate the constant. Then substitute dimensions.

  • From F = kx: [k] = [MT⁻²]
  • From F = Gm₁m₂/r²: [G] = [M⁻¹L³T⁻²]
  • From E = hν: [h] = [ML²T⁻¹]

8. Uses and limitations

  • Check dimensional consistency of equations.
  • Convert quantities between unit systems.
  • Find powers in a possible proportional relation.
  • Cannot determine pure numerical constants such as 2 or π.
  • Cannot distinguish quantities with identical dimensions, such as work and torque.
Passing a dimensional test is necessary, but it does not prove that an equation is physically correct.
