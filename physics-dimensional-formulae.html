📐 Dimensional Formulae

Understand fundamental dimensions, dimensional formulae, homogeneity and dimensional analysis.

1. Meaning of a dimensional formula

A dimensional formula expresses a physical quantity in terms of powers of fundamental dimensions. In mechanics, mass, length and time are represented by M, L and T.

[Q] = Mᵃ Lᵇ Tᶜ

The powers a, b and c are called dimensional exponents.

2. Fundamental dimensions and notation

Common base dimensions are mass (M), length (L), time (T), electric current (I) and thermodynamic temperature (Θ). For mechanical formulae, M, L and T are usually sufficient.

A dimension is not a unit: length has dimension L, while metre (m) is an SI unit.

3. Formulae from kinematics

  • Displacement: [L]
  • Velocity = displacement / time: [LT⁻¹]
  • Acceleration = velocity / time: [LT⁻²]
  • Frequency = 1 / time: [T⁻¹]

4. Formulae from Newton’s laws

  • Momentum = mass × velocity: [MLT⁻¹]
  • Force = mass × acceleration: [MLT⁻²]
  • Impulse = force × time: [MLT⁻¹]
  • Spring constant = force / extension: [MT⁻²]

5. Work, energy and power

  • Work = force × displacement: [ML²T⁻²]
  • Kinetic energy = ½mv²: [ML²T⁻²]
  • Power = work / time: [ML²T⁻³]
  • Torque = force × distance: [ML²T⁻²]
Work and torque have the same dimensions, but they are different physical quantities.

6. Formulae in fluids and gravitation

  • Pressure = force / area: [ML⁻¹T⁻²]
  • Density = mass / volume: [ML⁻³]
  • Dynamic viscosity: [ML⁻¹T⁻¹]
  • Gravitational constant G: [M⁻¹L³T⁻²]

7. How to derive a dimensional formula

Start with the defining equation, replace each quantity with its dimensions, and simplify the powers of M, L and T.

Force = mass × acceleration = M × (LT⁻²) = MLT⁻²

For pressure, divide the force dimensions by area: (MLT⁻²) / L² = ML⁻¹T⁻².

8. Dimensional homogeneity and applications

Every term added or subtracted in a valid physical equation must have the same dimensions. Dimensional analysis helps check equations, convert units and derive possible relations.

  • It cannot determine pure numerical constants such as 2 or π.
  • It cannot distinguish quantities with identical dimensions.
  • A dimensionally correct equation is not automatically physically correct.
