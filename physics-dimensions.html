📐 Dimensions

Understand fundamental dimensions, dimensional formulae, homogeneity and dimensional analysis.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations

1. What are dimensions?

Dimensions describe how a physical quantity depends on fundamental quantities such as mass, length and time. They are written using powers of base dimensions.

[Q] = Mᵃ Lᵇ Tᶜ

Here a, b and c are dimensional exponents for mass, length and time.

2. Fundamental dimensions

In mechanics, the fundamental dimensions are mass (M), length (L) and time (T). Other quantities may require electric current (I), temperature (Θ), amount of substance (N) and luminous intensity (J).

Do not confuse a dimension with a unit: length has dimension L, while metre is one unit of length.

3. Dimensional formula of common quantities

  • Area: [L²]; volume: [L³]
  • Velocity: [LT⁻¹]; acceleration: [LT⁻²]
  • Momentum: [MLT⁻¹]; force: [MLT⁻²]
  • Work/energy: [ML²T⁻²]; power: [ML²T⁻³]

4. Dimensions of pressure, density and frequency

  • Pressure = force/area = [ML⁻¹T⁻²]
  • Density = mass/volume = [ML⁻³]
  • Frequency = 1/time = [T⁻¹]
  • Gravitational constant G = [M⁻¹L³T⁻²]

5. Principle of dimensional homogeneity

In a physically meaningful equation, every additive term must have the same dimensions. The dimensions of the left-hand side must match those of the right-hand side.

s = ut + ½at²

Each term has the dimension of length.

6. Checking equations

Dimensional analysis can test whether an equation is dimensionally consistent. For example, in v² = u² + 2as, every term has dimensions [L²T⁻²].

A dimensionally incorrect equation cannot be physically correct, but a dimensionally correct equation is not necessarily correct.

7. Finding relations between quantities

Assume a power-law relation between quantities, then equate the powers of M, L and T on both sides to determine unknown exponents. This method can find proportional relationships.

It cannot determine dimensionless numerical constants such as 2 or π.

8. Uses and limitations

  • Check dimensional consistency of equations.
  • Convert units between systems.
  • Derive possible relations between physical quantities.
  • It cannot distinguish quantities with identical dimensions, such as work and torque.
  • It cannot derive equations involving sums of unlike powers or determine dimensionless constants.
