🌀 Displacement Current

Understand Maxwell’s displacement current, electric flux, charging capacitors and the Ampere–Maxwell law.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations
Question 1 of 300%

1. Why displacement current was introduced

In a charging capacitor, conduction current flows through the connecting wires, but no charge crosses the insulating gap between the plates. Yet a magnetic field exists around the circuit.

To make Ampere’s law consistent for different surfaces spanning the same loop, James Clerk Maxwell introduced displacement current, associated with a changing electric field.

Displacement current is not ordinary charge flow across the capacitor gap. It represents the magnetic effect of a time-varying electric field.

2. Electric flux and displacement current

Electric flux through a surface is \(\Phi_E=\int\vec E\cdot d\vec A\). In vacuum, displacement current is defined as:

\[I_d=\varepsilon_0\frac{d\Phi_E}{dt}\]

Its SI unit is ampere (A), the same as conduction current. A constant electric flux gives zero displacement current through that surface.

3. Displacement current density

The displacement current density in vacuum is:

\[\vec J_d=\varepsilon_0\frac{\partial\vec E}{\partial t}\]

For a uniform electric field perpendicular to a flat plate area \(A\), \(I_d=\varepsilon_0 A\,dE/dt\). Its direction and sign depend on the chosen surface normal and the changing field.

4. Charging parallel-plate capacitor

For an ideal capacitor being charged, the displacement current between the plates equals the conduction current in the connecting wires:

\[I_d=I_c=I\]

This equality ensures current continuity: the same magnetic field is obtained whether the chosen surface cuts the wire or passes through the capacitor gap.

5. Ampere–Maxwell law

Maxwell modified Ampere’s circuital law by adding the displacement-current term:

\[\oint\vec B\cdot d\vec l=\mu_0\left(I_c+I_d\right)\]

In vacuum, substituting \(I_d=\varepsilon_0\,d\Phi_E/dt\) gives:

\[\oint\vec B\cdot d\vec l=\mu_0 I_c+\mu_0\varepsilon_0\frac{d\Phi_E}{dt}\]

6. Dielectrics and electric displacement

In matter, the electric displacement field is:

\[\vec D=\varepsilon_0\vec E+\vec P\]

Here \(\vec P\) is the electric polarisation. In a linear, isotropic dielectric, \(\vec D=\varepsilon\vec E\). The displacement-current contribution is associated with the time variation of electric displacement flux.

7. Numerical relationships

For a parallel-plate capacitor in vacuum, \(E=Q/(\varepsilon_0 A)\). Therefore:

\[I_d=\varepsilon_0 A\frac{dE}{dt}=\frac{dQ}{dt}\]

So the displacement current equals the rate at which charge accumulates on a plate, and equals the wire current for an ideal charging circuit.

Use \(\varepsilon_0=8.85\times10^{-12}\;\mathrm{F\,m^{-1}}\) when required.

8. Importance and quick revision

  • Introduced by Maxwell to complete Ampere’s law.
  • Exists when electric flux changes with time.
  • In vacuum, \(I_d=\varepsilon_0 d\Phi_E/dt\).
  • For an ideal charging capacitor, gap displacement current equals wire conduction current.
  • Its SI unit is ampere.
  • It helps make the theory consistent and leads to the prediction of electromagnetic waves.
