⚡ Energy Stored in an Inductor

Understand how electrical work is converted into magnetic-field energy, use the energy formula and solve numerical problems.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations
Question 1 of 300%

1. Why does an inductor store energy?

When current is increased through an inductor, the changing magnetic flux produces a back emf that opposes the increase. The source must do work against this back emf. For an ideal inductor, this work is stored in the magnetic field around the coil.

Electrical work supplied → Magnetic-field energy
An ideal inductor stores and can return energy; it does not dissipate that stored energy as heat.

2. Derivation of the energy formula

For an inductor of constant inductance L, the induced emf magnitude is L(dI/dt). The instantaneous power supplied is p = vi = LI(dI/dt). Since power is the rate of change of energy, integrate from zero current to final current I.

dU = LI dI
U = ∫₀ᴵ LI dI = ½LI²

Here U is stored energy in joules, L is inductance in henry and I is current in ampere.

3. Meaning of U = ½LI²

Stored energy is directly proportional to inductance when current is fixed, and proportional to the square of current when inductance is fixed.

U ∝ L   (I fixed)
U ∝ I²   (L fixed)
  • Double L at the same current → double energy.
  • Double I at the same inductance → four times energy.
  • Triple I → nine times energy.

4. Magnetic energy density

Energy is distributed through the magnetic field region. For a linear medium, magnetic energy density is energy per unit volume.

u = B²/(2μ)

Here B is magnetic flux density and μ is the permeability of the medium. The SI unit of energy density is J m⁻³.

In vacuum, μ = μ₀, so u = B²/(2μ₀).

5. Energy in a long solenoid

For a long solenoid, the magnetic field inside is approximately uniform and B = μnI, where n is turns per unit length. Its inductance is L = μN²A/l.

U = ½LI²
u = U/(Al) = B²/(2μ)

Al is the approximate volume occupied by the field inside a long solenoid.

6. Energy during current change

For a fixed ideal inductor, a changing current changes its stored energy. If current falls, the magnetic field collapses and the inductor returns energy to the circuit. If current rises, energy is supplied to build the field.

ΔU = ½L(I₂² − I₁²)
Use the difference of the squared currents, not simply the square of the change in current.

7. Worked numerical examples

Example 1: L = 2 H and I = 3 A.

U = ½ × 2 × 3² = 9 J

Example 2: An inductor stores 16 J at 4 A. Find L.

L = 2U/I² = 32/16 = 2 H

Example 3: L = 0.5 H; current rises from 2 A to 4 A.

ΔU = ½ × 0.5 × (16 − 4) = 3 J

8. Quick revision and common mistakes

  • Stored energy: U = ½LI².
  • Magnetic energy density: u = B²/(2μ) for a linear medium.
  • SI unit of energy: joule (J).
  • SI unit of energy density: J m⁻³.
  • At fixed L, energy varies as I².
  • For a current change, ΔU = ½L(I₂² − I₁²).
Avoid forgetting the factor ½ or using (ΔI)² in place of (I₂² − I₁²). Switch to Practice for 30 questions; wrong answers are reviewed only after the quiz ends.
