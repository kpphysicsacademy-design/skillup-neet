Equations of Motion

Use these equations when acceleration is constant and motion is along a straight line.

📘 Learn🎮 Simulator🧠 Practice

📌 Quick Cheat Sheet

The kinematic equations apply to motion along a straight line with constant acceleration. Use signed velocity, displacement and acceleration.

EquationUseful when
v = u + atFind final velocity without displacement
s = ut + ½at²Find displacement without final velocity
v² = u² + 2asTime not given
s = (u + v)t / 2Acceleration constant; average velocity known

Symbols: u = initial velocity (m/s), v = final velocity (m/s), a = constant acceleration (m/s²), t = elapsed time (s), s = signed displacement (m).

Worked example: u = 2 m/s, a = 3 m/s², t = 4 s → v = 2 + 3×4 = 14 m/s; s = 2×4 + ½×3×4² = 32 m.

Braking example: u = +20 m/s, a = −5 m/s² → stopping time = 4 s and stopping displacement = 40 m.

NEET tip: displacement can be negative. These equations do not apply directly to varying acceleration without additional analysis.

Symbols

  • u = initial velocity (m/s)
  • v = final velocity (m/s)
  • a = constant acceleration (m/s²)
  • t = time (s)
  • s = displacement (m)

Three equations

v = u + at

Use when displacement is not needed.

s = ut + ½at²

Use when final velocity is not given.

v² = u² + 2as

Use when time is not given.

Another useful relation

s = (u + v)t ÷ 2

This uses average velocity for constant acceleration.

Simple example

A body starts from rest (u = 0), accelerates at 2 m/s² for 5 s.
v = u + at = 0 + 2 × 5 = 10 m/s
s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½ × 2 × 25 = 25 m

Remember

  • Choose the equation that contains your known values and required value.
  • Keep signs of velocity and acceleration consistent with your chosen direction.
  • These equations require constant acceleration.
