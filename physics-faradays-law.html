⚡ Faraday's Law

Understand electromagnetic induction, Faraday law, Lenz law, motional emf, self and mutual induction, and eddy currents.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations
Question 1 of 300%

1. Electromagnetic induction

Electromagnetic induction is the production of an emf when magnetic flux linked with a circuit changes. In a closed circuit, this emf can drive an induced current.

Changing flux linkage → induced emf

2. Faraday's laws

An emf is induced whenever the magnetic flux linked with a circuit changes. Its magnitude is proportional to the rate of change of flux linkage.

ε = − d(NΦ)/dt

For a stationary N-turn coil with equal flux Φ per turn: ε = −N dΦ/dt.

3. Lenz's law

The induced current flows so that its magnetic field opposes the change in flux that produces it. This is represented by the negative sign in Faraday's law.

A north pole approaching a coil is opposed by a north pole on the near face; withdrawal is opposed by an attractive south face.

4. Factors affecting induced emf

Emf increases with the number of turns and the rate of flux change. Flux changes when magnetic field strength, loop area or orientation changes.

|ε| = N |ΔΦ/Δt|

5. Motional emf

A conducting rod of length l moving at speed v perpendicular to a magnetic field B develops an emf across its ends.

ε = Blv

Example: B = 2 T, l = 0.5 m and v = 4 m/s gives ε = 4 V.

6. Self and mutual induction

Self-induction occurs when changing current in a coil changes its own flux linkage and induces a back emf.

ε = −L dI/dt

Mutual induction occurs when changing current in one coil induces emf in a nearby coil. Transformers use mutual induction.

7. Eddy currents

Eddy currents are circulating currents induced in bulk conductors by changing magnetic flux. They are useful in electromagnetic braking and induction heating but cause unwanted core heating.

Thin insulated laminations reduce eddy-current losses by interrupting large current loops.

8. Worked example and revision

A 100-turn coil experiences a flux change of 0.02 Wb per turn in 0.1 s.

|ε| = N ΔΦ/Δt = 100 × 0.02/0.1 = 20 V
  • Faraday: emf depends on rate of flux-linkage change.
  • Lenz: induced effect opposes the change.
  • More turns or faster change gives larger emf.
  • Motional emf: Blv.
  • Transformer: mutual induction.
  • Laminations reduce eddy-current loss.
