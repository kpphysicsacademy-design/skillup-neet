1. What is a frame of reference?

A frame of reference is a viewpoint together with a coordinate system used to describe an object's position and motion. Motion is described relative to an observer or chosen frame.

Position and velocity are specified relative to a frame

2. Rest and motion are relative

A passenger sitting in a moving bus is at rest relative to the bus but moving relative to the road. Neither description is contradictory: each uses a different frame.

3. Choose an origin and axis

For one-dimensional motion, choose an origin (zero position) and a positive direction. Position is then represented by a coordinate such as x = +5 m or x = −3 m.

Displacement: Δx = xfinal − xinitial

4. Relative position and velocity

The position of A relative to B is the difference between their positions. Relative velocity follows the same vector-difference rule.

vA/B = vA − vB

Choose a positive direction and keep the signs consistent.

5. Same-direction motion

When two objects move in the same direction, their relative speed is the difference of their speeds. If they have equal velocities, one appears at rest to the other.

Relative speed = |v₁ − v₂|

6. Opposite-direction motion

When objects move in opposite directions along one line, their closing or separating speed is the sum of their speed magnitudes.

Closing speed = |v₁| + |v₂|

7. Galilean velocity addition

For ordinary non-relativistic speeds, velocities measured in two frames moving relative to each other are related by vector addition. A passenger walking forward in a train has the train's ground velocity plus the walking velocity relative to the train.

vperson/ground = vperson/train + vtrain/ground

This is the classical approximation used in basic mechanics.
