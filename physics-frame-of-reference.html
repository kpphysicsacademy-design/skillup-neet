1. What is a frame of reference?

A frame of reference is the observer's coordinate system used to describe an object's position and motion. It includes an origin, coordinate axes and a way to measure time.

Motion is described relative to a chosen frame

2. Rest and motion are relative

A passenger sitting in a moving bus is at rest relative to the bus, but moves relative to the road. Both statements are correct because the reference frames are different.

3. Choose the origin and positive direction

Choose a convenient zero position and a positive direction before using equations. Position to the right may be positive and position to the left negative; the choice is yours, but keep it consistent.

Displacement: Δx = x − x₀

4. Relative position and velocity

For two objects measured in the same frame, subtract the velocity of the reference object from the velocity of the observed object.

vA/B = vA − vB

Use algebraic signs to show direction.

5. Same and opposite directions

For motion along one straight line, objects moving in the same direction have relative speed equal to the difference of their speed magnitudes. Moving in opposite directions gives the sum.

Same direction: |v₁ − v₂|
Opposite directions: |v₁| + |v₂|

6. Inertial frame

A frame at rest or moving with constant velocity (without rotation) relative to an inertial frame is also inertial. Newton's laws hold in an inertial frame without adding a fictitious force.

aframe = 0 (relative to an inertial frame)

Example: a train moving in a straight line with constant velocity is approximately an inertial frame.

7. Non-inertial frame

A frame that accelerates or rotates relative to an inertial frame is non-inertial. To use Newton's laws in this frame, introduce an appropriate pseudo force.

Example: when a bus accelerates forward, a standing passenger appears to move backward relative to the bus.

8. Galilean velocity addition

At ordinary speeds, add velocities vectorially when changing between frames. A person walking forward inside a moving train has the train's ground velocity plus the person's velocity relative to the train.

vperson/ground = vperson/train + vtrain/ground

Quick example

A train moves east at 20 m/s. A passenger walks east at 2 m/s relative to the train.

vpassenger/ground = 20 + 2 = 22 m/s east

If the passenger walks west at 2 m/s, the ground velocity is 20 − 2 = 18 m/s east.

Remember these points

1. Always state the reference frame. 2. Measure both velocities in a common frame before subtracting. 3. Keep the chosen sign convention consistent. 4. Newton's laws in their usual form apply in inertial frames.
