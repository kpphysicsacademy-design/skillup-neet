🪂 Free-fall Motion

Understand how gravity changes the velocity of an object moving vertically. Explore dropped objects and objects thrown upward or downward.

LearnSimulatorPractice

1. What is free fall?

Free fall is motion in which gravity is the only force acting on an object. In the ideal model, air resistance is neglected. Near Earth's surface, all freely falling objects have the same acceleration regardless of their mass.

g ≈ 9.8 m/s² downward

2. Choose a sign convention

Choose one direction as positive before using equations. If upward is positive, gravitational acceleration is −g. If downward is positive, it is +g. The physics does not change; only the signs do.

Upward positive: a = −g
Downward positive: a = +g

3. Equations of vertical motion

For constant acceleration, use the usual equations with acceleration due to gravity.

v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as

For upward-positive motion, substitute a = −g. Here u is initial velocity, v is final velocity, s is displacement and t is elapsed time.

4. Object dropped from rest

When an object is released without an initial push, its initial velocity is zero. Taking downward as positive:

v = gt    h = ½gt²    v² = 2gh
Example: A stone is dropped from rest for 3 s. Taking g = 10 m/s², its speed is v = 10 × 3 = 30 m/s and distance fallen is h = ½ × 10 × 3² = 45 m.

5. Object thrown vertically upward

Take upward as positive. Gravity slows the object during ascent. At the highest point, vertical velocity becomes zero for an instant, but acceleration remains −g.

At maximum height: v = 0
Time to rise: t = u/g
Maximum rise: H = u²/(2g)

If it returns to its launch height, and air resistance is ignored, total flight time is 2u/g and its return speed equals its launch speed.

6. Important facts

  • Acceleration due to gravity always points downward near Earth's surface.
  • At the highest point, velocity is zero but acceleration is not zero.
  • For equal launch and landing heights, ascent time equals descent time.
  • In ideal free fall, mass does not affect the acceleration.
  • Distance fallen from rest is proportional to t².

Quick revision

v = u − gt   •   s = ut − ½gt²

These forms use upward as positive. Always check the chosen direction and the sign of displacement before substituting values.
