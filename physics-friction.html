Friction

Friction is the contact force that opposes relative sliding, or the tendency of sliding, between surfaces. It acts parallel to the surfaces in contact.

Limiting friction: fₛ,max = μₛN

1. Static friction

Static friction acts when surfaces do not slide relative to each other. It adjusts to the applied force up to a maximum value.

0 ≤ fₛ ≤ μₛN

2. Kinetic friction

Kinetic (sliding) friction acts when surfaces slide against each other. In the simple dry-friction model:

fₖ = μₖN

Usually μₛ is greater than μₖ for common dry surfaces.

3. Friction on a horizontal surface

When no other vertical forces act, the normal reaction is N = mg. Therefore, maximum static friction is μₛmg and kinetic friction is μₖmg.

4. Friction on an inclined plane

For a block on an incline at angle θ, N = mg cosθ. The component of weight down the plane is mg sinθ. Friction opposes the actual or impending relative motion.

At limiting equilibrium: tanθ = μₛ

5. Applications

  • Static friction provides grip while walking and allows tyres to roll without slipping.
  • Lubricants reduce direct contact between surface irregularities.
  • Friction can be useful for braking and grip, but it also causes wear and energy loss.

Remember

Static friction is not always equal to μₛN; that is its maximum value. First determine the direction of impending or actual motion, then draw a free-body diagram.

Worked example

A 5 kg block slides on a horizontal floor. If μₖ = 0.2 and g = 10 m/s², calculate kinetic friction.

N = mg = 5 × 10 = 50 N
fₖ = μₖN = 0.2 × 50 = 10 N

Exam tip: use the normal force perpendicular to the surface, not automatically the object's weight.
