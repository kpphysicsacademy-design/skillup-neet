LESSON 1

Position–time (x–t)

The graph tells where the object is at each moment. Slope tells velocity.

Velocity = slope = Δx / Δt
  • Flat line → at rest
  • Steeper line → greater speed
  • Positive / negative slope → positive / negative velocity

Try: Position changes from 2 m to 14 m in 4 s. Velocity = (14 − 2) ÷ 4 = 3 m/s.

🚀
LESSON 2

Velocity–time (v–t)

The graph shows velocity and direction. Slope gives acceleration; signed area gives displacement.

Acceleration = Δv / Δt
Displacement = signed area
  • Flat line → constant velocity
  • Line sloping upward → positive acceleration
  • Area below time axis counts negative
  • Total distance = add absolute areas

Try: Velocity rises from 0 to 8 m/s in 4 s. Acceleration = 2 m/s²; displacement = ½ × 4 × 8 = 16 m.

📈
LESSON 3

Acceleration–time (a–t)

This graph shows how velocity changes. Area gives change in velocity.

Δv = signed area under a–t graph

Try: Acceleration is 3 m/s² for 2 s. Change in velocity = 3 × 2 = 6 m/s.

🧭
LESSON 4

Distance vs displacement

Displacement includes direction. Distance is the complete path travelled and never decreases.

  • v–t signed area → displacement
  • Sum of absolute v–t areas → distance
  • When velocity crosses zero, direction may reverse

LESSON 5

Remember the graph rules

x–t slopeVelocity
v–t slopeAcceleration
v–t areaDisplacement
a–t areaChange in velocity

Always check the axes, units, time interval and signs before calculating.
