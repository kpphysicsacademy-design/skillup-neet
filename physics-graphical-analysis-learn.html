Graphical Analysis

Motion graphs turn changing position, velocity and acceleration into visual information. Learn what the slope and area of each graph tell you.

LearnSimulatorPractice

1. Read the axes first

The horizontal axis is usually time. Identify the vertical quantity and its unit before interpreting a graph. A graph's shape alone is not enough; its axes and scale determine its physical meaning.

Position–time (x–t)

Shows where an object is at each instant.

Velocity–time (v–t)

Shows the object's velocity and direction.

Acceleration–time (a–t)

Shows how quickly velocity changes.

Distance–time

Distance never decreases; its slope gives speed.

2. Position–time graph

Slope = Δx / Δt = average velocity
  • Horizontal line: position is constant; the object is at rest.
  • Straight line with constant slope: constant velocity.
  • Positive slope: motion in the positive direction.
  • Negative slope: motion in the negative direction.
  • Changing slope: velocity is changing.
Example: Position changes from 2 m to 14 m in 4 s. Average velocity = (14 − 2)/4 = 3 m/s.

3. Velocity–time graph

Slope = Δv / Δt = acceleration
Signed area = displacement
  • Horizontal line: constant velocity and zero acceleration.
  • Upward straight line: constant positive acceleration.
  • Downward straight line: constant negative acceleration.
  • Area below the time axis is negative and must be subtracted when finding net displacement.
  • Total distance is the sum of the absolute areas.
Example: Velocity rises uniformly from 0 to 8 m/s in 4 s. Acceleration = 8/4 = 2 m/s². Displacement = triangular area = ½ × 4 × 8 = 16 m.

4. Acceleration–time graph

Signed area = Δv
  • Horizontal line above zero: constant positive acceleration.
  • Horizontal line below zero: constant negative acceleration.
  • Line on zero: velocity remains constant.
  • Area above and below the axis contributes positive and negative changes in velocity.
Example: Constant acceleration of 3 m/s² for 2 s changes velocity by Δv = 3 × 2 = 6 m/s.

5. Distance, displacement and direction

Displacement is signed change in position. Distance is the total path length. On a velocity–time graph, signed area gives displacement; adding the magnitudes of all areas gives distance. A velocity graph crossing zero indicates a possible reversal of direction.

6. A reliable graph-solving method

  1. Read both axis labels and units.
  2. Mark the time interval asked in the question.
  3. For slope, choose two points and calculate rise ÷ run.
  4. For area, split the region into rectangles, triangles or trapezia.
  5. Keep signs for velocity and acceleration; use magnitudes only when finding total distance.
  6. Write the final unit: m/s, m/s², m or m/s as appropriate.
