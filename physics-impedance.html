⚡ Impedance

Understand impedance in AC circuits, calculate it for RL, RC and RLC combinations, and connect it with phase angle, power factor and resonance.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations
Question 1 of 300%

1. Meaning of impedance

Impedance is the total opposition offered by an AC circuit to the flow of alternating current. It combines resistance and reactance and is represented by \(Z\).

\[Z=\frac{V_{rms}}{I_{rms}}\]

Its SI unit is the ohm (Ω). Impedance determines the current for a given applied AC voltage.

Resistance dissipates energy; reactance arises from inductors and capacitors. Impedance accounts for their combined AC effect.

2. Impedance in a pure circuit

For an ideal resistor, inductor and capacitor connected separately, impedance magnitude is respectively:

\[Z_R=R,\qquad Z_L=X_L=\omega L,\qquad Z_C=X_C=\frac{1}{\omega C}\]

A pure resistor has voltage and current in phase. In an inductor current lags voltage by 90°, while in a capacitor current leads voltage by 90°.

3. Series R-L circuit

In a series R-L circuit, resistance and inductive reactance are perpendicular in the impedance diagram.

\[Z=\sqrt{R^2+X_L^2}\]

The current lags the supply voltage by a phase angle \(\phi\), where \(\tan\phi=X_L/R\).

Do not add R and X_L arithmetically to find the magnitude of impedance.

4. Series R-C circuit

In a series R-C circuit, resistance and capacitive reactance combine at right angles in the impedance diagram.

\[Z=\sqrt{R^2+X_C^2}\]

Current leads the supply voltage. The magnitude of the phase angle satisfies \(\tan|\phi|=X_C/R\).

5. Series R-L-C circuit

In a series RLC circuit, inductive and capacitive reactances oppose each other. The net reactance is their difference.

\[Z=\sqrt{R^2+(X_L-X_C)^2}\]

Complex form: \(\underline Z=R+j(X_L-X_C)\). If \(X_L>X_C\), the circuit is inductive; if \(X_C>X_L\), it is capacitive.

6. Phase angle and power factor

The phase angle describes the phase difference between supply voltage and current in a series RLC circuit.

\[\tan\phi=\frac{X_L-X_C}{R},\qquad \cos\phi=\frac{R}{Z}\]

Power factor is \(\cos\phi\). It is 1 for a purely resistive circuit and at series resonance.

Positive phase angle: inductive (current lags). Negative phase angle: capacitive (current leads).

7. Worked numerical examples

Example 1 — R-L: \(R=3\,\Omega\), \(X_L=4\,\Omega\).

\[Z=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5\,\Omega\]

Example 2 — R-C: \(R=6\,\Omega\), \(X_C=8\,\Omega\).

\[Z=\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=10\,\Omega\]

Example 3 — RLC: \(R=8\,\Omega\), \(X_L=10\,\Omega\), \(X_C=4\,\Omega\).

\[Z=\sqrt{8^2+(10-4)^2}=10\,\Omega\]

8. Resonance and quick revision

At series resonance, inductive and capacitive reactances are equal and cancel each other.

\[X_L=X_C,\qquad Z=R,\qquad \cos\phi=1\]
  • Impedance unit: ohm (Ω).
  • Series R-L: \(Z=\sqrt{R^2+X_L^2}\).
  • Series R-C: \(Z=\sqrt{R^2+X_C^2}\).
  • Series RLC: \(Z=\sqrt{R^2+(X_L-X_C)^2}\).
  • At resonance, series impedance is minimum and equals R.
  • For fixed RMS voltage, \(I=V/Z\).
