📐 Inclined Plane

Understand how gravity, normal reaction and friction act on a block placed on a sloping surface.

1. Resolve the weight

The weight mg always acts vertically downward. For a plane inclined at angle θ to the horizontal, resolve it into two components:

Along the plane: mg sin θ
Perpendicular to plane: mg cos θ

The first component tends to move the block down the slope. The second presses the block against the surface.

2. Normal reaction

When no other force acts perpendicular to the surface, the normal reaction balances the perpendicular component of weight.

N = mg cos θ

As θ increases from 0° to 90°, normal reaction decreases from mg to zero.

3. Smooth inclined plane

A smooth plane has no friction in the ideal model. Applying Newton’s second law along the slope:

mg sin θ = ma
a = g sin θ

Acceleration is independent of the block’s mass.

4. Rough inclined plane

Friction acts parallel to the surface and opposes relative motion or its tendency.

  • Block sliding down: kinetic friction acts up the plane.
  • Block moving up: kinetic friction acts down the plane.
  • Block tending to slide down while at rest: static friction acts up the plane.
Sliding down: a = g(sin θ − μₖ cos θ)

5. Angle of repose

At the angle where a block is just about to slide down:

μₛ = tan θ

Worked example

A 4 kg block rests on a smooth 30° incline. Take g = 10 m/s².
Down-slope component = 4 × 10 × 0.5 = 20 N.
Normal reaction = 40 × 0.866 = 34.6 N.
Acceleration = 10 × 0.5 = 5 m/s².

Quick check

At θ = 0°, the plane is horizontal. At θ = 90°, it is vertical. Choose axes parallel and perpendicular to the plane to simplify force calculations.
