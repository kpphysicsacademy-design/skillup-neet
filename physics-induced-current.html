⚡ Induced Current

Learn how changing magnetic flux produces induced emf and current, and understand Faraday's law, Lenz's law and energy conversion.

1. Electromagnetic induction

Electromagnetic induction is the phenomenon in which an emf is produced in a circuit when the magnetic flux linked with it changes. If the circuit is closed, the induced emf drives an induced current.

Changing magnetic flux → induced emf → current (closed circuit)
A changing magnetic field is not the only way to induce emf. Relative motion between a conductor and a magnetic field can also change the flux.

2. Magnetic flux

Magnetic flux measures the magnetic field passing through a surface. For a uniform magnetic field over a flat area,

Φ = BA cos θ

Here B is magnetic field strength, A is area and θ is the angle between B and the area vector (normal to the surface). SI unit: weber (Wb).

3. How can magnetic flux change?

Flux can change if the magnetic field strength changes, the area of the loop changes, or the angle between the field and the area vector changes. Moving a magnet or coil relative to the other can change flux through the coil.

Φ = BA cos θ

Changing any one of B, A or θ can induce emf. The circuit need not move if the magnetic field itself changes with time.

4. Faraday's law of induction

The magnitude of induced emf is equal to the rate of change of magnetic flux. For a coil with N turns,

ε = −N dΦ/dt

The magnitude is N|dΦ/dt|. More turns or a faster change in flux gives a greater induced emf.

For a finite time interval, average induced emf magnitude = N|ΔΦ/Δt|.

5. Lenz's law and direction

The induced current flows in such a direction that the magnetic field produced by it opposes the change in magnetic flux that caused it. This is represented by the negative sign in Faraday's law.

ε = −N dΦ/dt

If the external flux through a loop increases, the induced field acts to oppose that increase. If the external flux decreases, the induced field acts to oppose the decrease.

6. Induced current and resistance

When the circuit is closed, induced current is found using Ohm's law. The emf is the source of the current, while the circuit resistance determines its magnitude.

I = ε/R

For the same induced emf, a smaller resistance gives a larger current. In an open circuit, an induced emf may exist but no sustained current flows.

7. Motional induction and applications

A conductor moving through a magnetic field can experience charge separation due to magnetic force. For a straight rod of length ℓ moving perpendicular to a uniform field B at speed v, the motional emf is

ε = Bℓv

Electromagnetic induction is used in electric generators, transformers, induction cooktops, dynamic microphones and many sensing devices.

8. Worked examples and quick revision

Example: A 50-turn coil experiences a flux change of 0.02 Wb per turn in 0.1 s. The average induced emf magnitude is:

|ε| = N|ΔΦ/Δt| = 50 × 0.02/0.1 = 10 V

Quick revision:

  • Flux: Φ = BA cos θ.
  • Faraday's law: ε = −N dΦ/dt.
  • Lenz's law gives the direction of induced current.
  • Closed circuit current: I = ε/R.
  • Motional emf for perpendicular geometry: ε = Bℓv.
  • SI units: flux — weber; emf — volt; current — ampere.
