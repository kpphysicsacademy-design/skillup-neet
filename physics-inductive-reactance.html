🌀 Inductive Reactance

Understand how an inductor opposes alternating current, calculate inductive reactance, and explore its dependence on frequency and inductance.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations
Question 1 of 300%

1. Meaning of inductive reactance

Inductive reactance is the opposition an inductor offers to alternating current because changing current produces a changing magnetic field and a self-induced emf. It is measured in ohms (Ω).

\[X_L=\frac{V_L}{I}\]
An ideal inductor has no resistance, but it opposes AC through self-induction.

2. Formula and symbols

Inductive reactance depends on angular frequency and inductance.

\[X_L=\omega L=2\pi fL\]

Here, \(X_L\) is in ohms, \(L\) is inductance in henry, \(f\) is frequency in hertz and \(\\omega=2\\pi f\) is angular frequency.

Use frequency in hertz and inductance in henry before substituting.

3. Dependence on frequency

For a fixed inductor, inductive reactance is directly proportional to frequency.

\[X_L\propto f\]

If frequency doubles, inductive reactance doubles. If frequency is reduced to one-third, reactance also becomes one-third.

4. Dependence on inductance

At fixed frequency, a coil with greater inductance offers greater opposition to AC.

\[X_L\propto L\]

Doubling the inductance doubles \(X_L\); tripling the inductance triples \(X_L\).

Both frequency and inductance increase inductive reactance linearly.

5. Inductor connected to DC

For steady DC, frequency is zero. Therefore an ideal inductor has zero inductive reactance after the current becomes steady. A real coil still has wire resistance.

\[f=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad X_L=0\]

At switching, self-induction opposes the change in current.

6. Phase relationship in a pure inductor

In a purely inductive AC circuit, voltage leads current by 90°, or equivalently, current lags voltage by 90°.

\[i=I_0\sin(\omega t-\pi/2)\]
Memory cue: in an inductor, current lags voltage.

7. Worked numerical examples

Example 1: (L=0.2\,H), (f=50\,Hz).

\[X_L=2\pi(50)(0.2)=62.8\,\Omega\]

Example 2: (X_L=31.4\,\Omega), (f=50\,Hz).

\[L=\frac{31.4}{2\pi(50)}=0.1\,H\]

Example 3: (L=0.5\,H), (f=100\,Hz).

\[X_L=2\pi(100)(0.5)=314\,\Omega\]

8. Quick revision and exam tips

  • Formula: (X_L=2\pi fL=\omega L).
  • SI unit: ohm (Ω).
  • Reactance increases with frequency and inductance.
  • At steady DC, ideal (X_L=0).
  • In a pure inductor, current lags voltage by 90°.
  • Convert mH to H: (1\,mH=10^{-3}\,H).
  • Convert kHz to Hz before calculation.
