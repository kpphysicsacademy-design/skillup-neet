🌀 Inductor

Learn how an inductor opposes changes in current, stores magnetic energy and behaves in DC and RL circuits.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations
Question 1 of 300%

1. What is an inductor?

An inductor is a passive electrical component, usually made from a coil of conducting wire. When current through it changes, its magnetic field changes and an emf is induced in the same coil. This self-induced emf opposes the change in current.

An inductor opposes changes in current
An inductor does not oppose steady current in an ideal DC circuit after the transient has ended.

2. Inductance and SI unit

Inductance measures how strongly a coil produces a back emf for a given rate of change of current. Its SI unit is the henry (H).

ε = −L (dI/dt)

Here L is inductance, I is current and ε is the self-induced emf. The negative sign represents Lenz's law.

3. Meaning of one henry

An inductor has an inductance of one henry when a current changing at one ampere per second produces an induced emf of one volt in magnitude.

1 H = 1 V·s/A
For a fixed inductor, a faster change in current produces a larger induced emf.

4. Factors affecting inductance

Inductance depends on the number of turns, coil dimensions, shape, spacing and magnetic permeability of the core. A high-permeability core generally increases inductance.

For a long solenoid: L = μN²A/l

N is the number of turns, A is cross-sectional area, l is length and μ is core permeability.

5. Back emf and Lenz's law

When current increases, the induced emf acts to oppose the increase. When current decreases, the induced emf acts to oppose the decrease and tends to maintain current.

ε = −L (dI/dt)

The direction of induced emf follows Lenz's law; the magnitude is L|dI/dt|.

6. Energy stored in an inductor

Work is required to build up current against the back emf. This energy is stored in the magnetic field around the coil.

U = ½LI²

For constant L, doubling the current increases stored energy fourfold.

7. Inductor in DC and RL circuits

Immediately after a DC supply is connected, an inductor opposes the rise of current. In a series RL circuit, the time constant is L/R. After a long time with ideal DC, the current becomes steady and the ideal inductor behaves like a short circuit.

τ = L/R
At steady state, dI/dt = 0, so the ideal inductor's induced emf is zero.

8. Worked examples and quick revision

Example 1: A 2 H inductor carries a current changing at 3 A/s.

|ε| = L|dI/dt| = 2 × 3 = 6 V

Example 2: A 4 H inductor carries 2 A.

U = ½LI² = ½ × 4 × 2² = 8 J

Quick revision:

  • Back emf: ε = −L dI/dt.
  • SI unit: henry (H).
  • Long solenoid: L = μN²A/l.
  • Stored magnetic energy: U = ½LI².
  • RL time constant: τ = L/R.
  • Ideal inductor at DC steady state acts as a short circuit.
