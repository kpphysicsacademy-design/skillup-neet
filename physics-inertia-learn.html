🛑 Inertia

Why does your body move forward when a bus stops suddenly? Inertia explains why objects resist changes in their state of motion.

1. Meaning of inertia

Inertia is the property of a body by which it resists any change in its state of rest or uniform motion in a straight line. It is not a force; it is a property of matter.

Greater mass → Greater inertia

Mass is the quantitative measure of inertia. An object with more mass needs a greater net force to produce the same acceleration.

2. Types of inertia

Inertia of restA coin tends to remain at rest when the card beneath it is flicked away.
Inertia of motionA passenger tends to move forward when a moving bus stops suddenly.
Inertia of directionA passenger seems to lean sideways when a car takes a sharp turn.
Key ideaAn object resists a change in velocity—change in speed, direction or both.

3. Newton's first law

A body remains at rest or continues to move with uniform velocity in a straight line unless acted upon by a non-zero net external force.

ΣF = 0 ⇒ a = 0

This law is also called the law of inertia. Zero net force allows an object to remain at rest or move with constant velocity.

4. Everyday examples

  1. Dust comes out of a carpet when it is beaten.
  2. Passengers jerk backward when a stationary bus starts suddenly.
  3. Passengers jerk forward when a moving bus brakes suddenly.
  4. A coin drops into a glass when the card below it is pulled quickly.

5. Worked example

Question: A 2 kg object and a 6 kg object experience the same net force of 12 N. Compare their accelerations.
For 2 kg: a = F/m = 12/2 = 6 m/s²
For 6 kg: a = 12/6 = 2 m/s²
Conclusion: The greater-mass object has greater inertia and smaller acceleration for the same force.

6. Remember for exams

  • Inertia depends on mass, not speed.
  • Inertia is not a force and does not itself change motion.
  • Newton's first law describes inertia.
  • For a fixed net force, acceleration is inversely proportional to mass.
