Instantaneous Velocity

A short lesson about velocity at one moment.

📘 Learn🎮 Simulator🧠 Practice

📌 Quick Cheat Sheet

Instantaneous velocity is the rate of change of position at a particular instant. In one dimension it can be positive, negative or zero. It is a vector; SI unit m/s.

ConceptRule
Instantaneous velocityv(t) = dx/dt
Limit definitionv = lim(Δt→0) Δx/Δt
Instantaneous speed|v| in 1D; |**v**| in any dimension
Position–time graphTangent slope gives signed velocity
At a turning pointVelocity can be zero momentarily

Worked example: x(t) = 3t² m (t in s) → v(t) = 6t m/s. At t = 2 s, velocity = +12 m/s, instantaneous speed = 12 m/s.

Negative example: x(t) = 10 − 4t m → velocity = −4 m/s and instantaneous speed = 4 m/s.

NEET tip: instantaneous speed is never negative. A negative tangent slope means negative velocity, not negative speed.

1. Meaning

Instantaneous velocity is the velocity of an object at a particular instant.

v = dx / dt

It is the rate of change of position with time. SI unit: m/s. It is a vector.

2. Position–time graph

Instantaneous velocity equals the slope of the tangent to the position–time graph at that instant.

v = slope = dx / dt

3. Simple example

Position: x = 3t²
Velocity: v = dx/dt = 6t
At t = 2 s: v = 6 × 2 = 12 m/s.

Remember

• Average velocity is measured over a time interval.
• Instantaneous velocity is measured at one moment.
• Negative velocity shows motion in the negative direction.
• Speed is the magnitude of velocity.
