📏 Kinematic Problem Solving

Turn a word problem into known values, select the right equation, and solve it with correct units and signs.

📘 Learn🎮 Simulator🧠 Practice

1. Identify the five quantities

u — Initial velocityVelocity at the beginning (m/s)
v — Final velocityVelocity at the end (m/s)
a — AccelerationRate of change of velocity (m/s²)
t — TimeDuration of motion (s)
s — DisplacementChange in position (m)

2. Choose the equation

v = u + at

Use when displacement is not needed.

s = ut + ½at²

Use when final velocity is not needed.

v² = u² + 2as

Use when time is not given.

s = (u + v)t / 2

Use average velocity when acceleration is constant.

3. A reliable solving method

  1. Read the question and identify the direction of motion.
  2. Write the known values: u, v, a, t or s.
  3. Convert all values to SI units.
  4. Choose an equation containing the known values and the unknown.
  5. Substitute with signs, calculate, and write the unit.
  6. Check whether the answer is physically reasonable.

4. Worked example

Question: A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 2 m/s² for 5 s. Find its final velocity and displacement.
Given: u = 0, a = 2 m/s², t = 5 s
Final velocity: v = u + at = 0 + 2 × 5 = 10 m/s
Displacement: s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½ × 2 × 25 = 25 m

5. Common mistakes

  • Using distance when the equation requires displacement.
  • Dropping the negative sign for retardation or opposite-direction motion.
  • Mixing km/h with m/s. Convert using km/h × 5/18.
  • Using these equations when acceleration is not constant.
  • For vertical motion, choose a positive direction and keep the sign of g consistent.
