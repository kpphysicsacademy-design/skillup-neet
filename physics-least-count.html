📌 Quick Cheat Sheet

Least count (LC) is the smallest scale increment that an instrument can directly resolve. A smaller LC improves resolution, not necessarily accuracy.

InstrumentLeast count
Millimetre ruler1 mm
Direct Vernier (n VSD = (n − 1) MSD)1 MSD / n
Screw gaugePitch / circular divisions

Vernier example: 1 MSD = 1 mm, 10 VSD = 9 MSD → LC = 1/10 = 0.1 mm. Reading = MSR + (coinciding VSD × LC).

Screw example: pitch = 0.5 mm, 50 circular divisions → LC = 0.01 mm. Reading = pitch-scale reading + (circular reading × LC).

Exam tip: apply zero-error correction where applicable. Never confuse least count with accuracy or uncertainty.

📘 Learn: Least Count

Build the concept step by step before using the instruments.

1. What is least count?

Least count (LC) is the smallest value that an instrument can measure or resolve directly. It is equal to the value of the smallest scale division for a simple graduated instrument.

Least count = value of one smallest scale division

2. Main scale and vernier scale

Vernier calipers use a main scale and a sliding vernier scale. In a standard direct vernier, if n vernier divisions equal (n − 1) main-scale divisions, the least count is:

LC = 1 MSD − 1 VSD = 1 MSD / n

Example: 10 VSD = 9 MSD and 1 MSD = 1 mm, so LC = 1/10 = 0.1 mm.

3. Reading a vernier calipers

First note the main-scale reading just to the left of the vernier zero. Then find the vernier division that coincides with a main-scale mark.

Observed reading = MSR + (coinciding VSD × LC)

Example: MSR = 2.3 cm, 6th division coincides and LC = 0.01 cm. Reading = 2.3 + 6 × 0.01 = 2.36 cm.

4. Pitch of a screw gauge

Pitch is the linear distance travelled by the screw in one complete rotation. It can be found by dividing the distance moved by the number of complete rotations.

Pitch = distance moved / number of rotations

5. Least count of a screw gauge

The circular scale is divided into equal divisions. Divide the pitch by the total number of circular-scale divisions.

LC = Pitch / Number of circular-scale divisions

Example: pitch = 1 mm and 100 divisions, therefore LC = 0.01 mm.

6. Zero error and zero correction

Close the instrument gently before measuring. If the zero marks do not coincide, the instrument has a zero error. Apply the zero correction with the opposite sign of the zero error.

Correct reading = Observed reading − Zero error

A positive zero error is subtracted; a negative zero error is added.

7. Resolution is not the same as accuracy

A smaller least count allows finer readings, but it does not guarantee accuracy. Calibration, zero error, alignment, parallax and measurement technique also affect the result.
