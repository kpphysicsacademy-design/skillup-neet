🔎 Lenses

Understand convex and concave lenses, principal rays, image formation, lens formula, magnification and power.

1. What is a lens?

A lens is a transparent optical medium bounded by two refracting surfaces, at least one of which is usually curved. It forms images by refraction.

A lens refracts light; a mirror forms images by reflection.

2. Types of lenses

  • Convex (converging): thicker at the centre and thinner at the edges.
  • Concave (diverging): thinner at the centre and thicker at the edges.
  • In air, a convex lens has positive focal length; a concave lens has negative focal length.

3. Important terms

  • Optical centre (O): a point on the principal axis through which a ray passes approximately undeviated in a thin lens.
  • Principal axis: line joining the centres of curvature of the lens surfaces.
  • Principal focus (F₁, F₂): point where paraxial parallel rays converge, or appear to diverge, after refraction.
  • Focal length (f): distance from optical centre to principal focus.
  • Aperture: effective diameter of the lens.

4. Principal rays and image construction

  • A ray parallel to the principal axis refracts through the far focus of a convex lens, or appears to come from the near focus of a concave lens.
  • A ray through the optical centre of a thin lens passes approximately undeviated.
  • A ray directed through the near focus of a convex lens emerges parallel to the principal axis.

5. Lens formula and magnification

Using the Cartesian sign convention, distances are measured from the optical centre. The direction of incident light is positive.

1/f = 1/v − 1/u
m = hᵢ/hₒ = v/u

Here u is object distance, v is image distance and m is linear magnification. A negative m indicates an inverted image.

6. Image formation by a convex lens

  • Object at infinity: image at F₂, real, inverted and highly diminished.
  • Beyond 2F₁: image between F₂ and 2F₂, real, inverted and diminished.
  • At 2F₁: image at 2F₂, real, inverted and same size.
  • Between F₁ and 2F₁: image beyond 2F₂, real, inverted and enlarged.
  • At F₁: image at infinity.
  • Between O and F₁: image on the object side, virtual, erect and enlarged.

7. Image formation by a concave lens

For a real object, a concave lens always forms a virtual, erect and diminished image between the optical centre and the principal focus on the object side.

Concave lenses are used to correct myopia (short-sightedness).

8. Power of a lens and quick revision

P = 1/f (f in metres)

The SI unit of power is dioptre (D). For thin lenses in contact, equivalent power is the algebraic sum:

P = P₁ + P₂ + …
  • Convex lens: positive f and positive power.
  • Concave lens: negative f and negative power.
  • Use metres when calculating power in dioptres.
  • For a real object, convex lens gives a virtual image when the object is inside its focal length.
