🧲 Lenz's Law

Master induced-current direction, opposing magnetic effects, conservation of energy, magnetic braking and eddy currents.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations
Question 1 of 300%

1. Statement of Lenz's law

The induced current flows in such a direction that the magnetic field produced by it opposes the change in magnetic flux that caused induction. It determines direction, not magnitude.

Induced effect opposes the change in flux
Lenz's law opposes a CHANGE in flux, not necessarily the magnetic field itself.

2. Connection with Faraday's law

Faraday's law gives induced emf magnitude. Its negative sign expresses Lenz's law and determines direction.

ε = −N dΦ/dt

For magnitude, use |ε| = N|ΔΦ/Δt|. The sign depends on the chosen positive direction for the loop.

3. Approaching and receding magnets

When a north pole approaches a coil, the near face becomes north to repel it. When the north pole moves away, the near face becomes south to attract it and oppose the decrease in flux.

The induced magnetic pole opposes the relative motion/change responsible for induction.

4. Increasing and decreasing flux

If external magnetic flux in a chosen direction increases, the induced field points opposite to that direction. If the flux decreases, the induced field points in the same direction to oppose the decrease.

Increasing flux → oppose increase
Decreasing flux → oppose decrease

5. Direction using the right-hand rule

Identify the direction of changing external flux. Choose the induced field that opposes the change. Then use the right-hand grip rule: thumb along the induced field through the loop; curled fingers give current direction.

Viewed from a face: anticlockwise current makes that face north; clockwise current makes it south.

6. Conservation of energy

Lenz's law follows conservation of energy. If induced current reinforced the change that produced it, energy could be generated without external work. Instead, an external agent must work against the opposing magnetic force.

External work → electrical energy + heat

7. Magnetic braking and eddy currents

A moving magnet near a conductor induces eddy currents. Their magnetic effects oppose motion, producing magnetic braking. This principle is used in trains and damping systems.

Thin insulated laminations reduce large eddy-current loops and energy loss in transformer cores.

8. Worked example and exam revision

A north pole approaches a coil. The near face becomes north, so the coil repels the approaching magnet. If the magnet is withdrawn, the near face becomes south, attracting it.

Approach → repel
Withdrawal → attract
  • Induced current opposes the change in flux.
  • Negative sign in Faraday's law represents Lenz's law.
  • Increasing and decreasing flux require opposite responses.
  • Use the right-hand grip rule to find current direction.
  • Lenz's law is consistent with conservation of energy.
  • Magnetic braking is an application.
