PHYSICS CONCEPT

Limiting Friction

Limiting friction is the maximum value of static friction that acts when two surfaces are just about to slide relative to one another. It is the threshold between rest and slipping.

flim = μsN

How it works

  1. When a small tangential force is applied, static friction adjusts to balance it.
  2. As the applied force increases, static friction also increases.
  3. At impending motion, static friction reaches its maximum value: limiting friction.
  4. If the applied force becomes greater than this maximum, slipping begins and kinetic friction acts.

Important relationships

  • For static contact: 0 ≤ fs ≤ μsN.
  • At the limiting condition: fs = flim = μsN.
  • μs = flim/N; the coefficient is dimensionless.
  • For many ordinary dry surfaces, μk is less than μs.
  • Friction acts parallel to the contact surface and opposes actual or impending relative motion.

Worked example

A 5 kg block rests on a horizontal floor. Take g = 10 m/s² and μs = 0.4. Find its limiting friction.

Normal reaction: N = mg = 5 × 10 = 50 N
Limiting friction: flim = μsN = 0.4 × 50 = 20 N

On an inclined plane

For a block just about to slide down a rough incline, friction acts up the plane. At the threshold, mg sin θ = μsmg cos θ, therefore tan θ = μs. The corresponding angle is called the angle of repose.

Common mistake

Do not assume static friction always equals μsN. It only reaches this value at impending motion.

Exam tip

First calculate the normal reaction. Then use flim = μsN.

Remember: Limiting friction is a maximum value, not the friction acting at every moment. Static friction self-adjusts below this limit whenever possible.
