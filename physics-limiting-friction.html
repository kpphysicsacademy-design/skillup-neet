PHYSICS CONCEPT

Limiting Friction

Limiting friction is the maximum value of static friction just before two surfaces begin to slide relative to each other.

flim = μsN

How it works

  1. When a small push is applied, static friction adjusts to balance it.
  2. As the push increases, static friction increases up to its maximum value.
  3. At impending motion, static friction reaches limiting friction.
  4. If the applied force exceeds this limit, sliding begins and kinetic friction acts.

Important relationships

  • 0 ≤ fs ≤ μsN
  • μs = flim/N (dimensionless)
  • For many ordinary dry surfaces, μk is less than μs.
  • Friction acts parallel to the contact surface and opposes impending relative motion.

Worked example

A 5 kg block rests on a horizontal floor. Take g = 10 m/s² and μs = 0.4.

N = mg = 5 × 10 = 50 N
flim = μsN = 0.4 × 50 = 20 N

Remember: actual static friction is not always μsN. It adjusts to the applied tangential force and reaches μsN only at the limiting condition.
