PHYSICS CONCEPT

Limiting Friction

Limiting friction is the maximum static friction just before an object starts to slide.

flim = μsN

How does it work?

  • Static friction prevents an object from starting to slide.
  • It increases when the applied force increases.
  • Just before slipping, it reaches its maximum value: limiting friction.
  • After slipping starts, kinetic friction acts.

Important formulas

  • Static friction: 0 ≤ fs ≤ μsN
  • At the limit: flim = μsN
  • Coefficient: μs = flim/N (no unit)
  • Horizontal surface: N = mg

Quick example

A 5 kg block rests on a horizontal floor. Take g = 10 m/s² and μs = 0.4.

N = mg = 5 × 10 = 50 N
flim = 0.4 × 50 = 20 N

On an inclined plane

At the point of slipping down the plane, friction acts up the slope.

tan θ = μs

This angle is called the angle of repose.

Remember: Static friction adjusts as needed. It equals μsN only at the limiting condition.
