🧲 Magnetic Dipole

Learn and practise magnetic dipole moment, torque, potential energy, equilibrium and magnetic field applications.

1. What is a magnetic dipole?

A magnetic dipole is an instrument used to measure the potential difference (voltage) between two points in an electric circuit. Its two terminals are connected across the component being measured.

Voltage is a potential difference: it is measured between two points, not at a single point alone.

2. Circuit connection and resistance

A magnetic dipole is connected in parallel with the component. An ideal magnetic dipole has infinite resistance and draws no current. A practical magnetic dipole has very high resistance to minimize the loading effect.

Ideal magnetic dipole: Rᵥ → ∞

Because the meter is in parallel, the potential difference across the magnetic dipole is the same as the potential difference across the component.

3. Why high resistance matters

A real magnetic dipole draws a small current. If its resistance is not sufficiently high compared with the component resistance, it changes the equivalent resistance of the branch and can alter the voltage being measured. This is called the loading effect.

Iᵥ = V/Rᵥ

For a fixed measured voltage V, increasing the input resistance Rᵥ reduces the current drawn by the meter.

4. Convert a galvanometer into a magnetic dipole

A sensitive galvanometer can be converted into a magnetic dipole by connecting a large resistance, called a multiplier, in series with its coil.

V = Iᵍ(G + R)
R = V/Iᵍ − G

G is galvanometer resistance, Iᵍ is full-scale deflection current, V is the required voltage range and R is the multiplier resistance.

A larger voltage range requires a larger series multiplier so the galvanometer current does not exceed its full-scale value.

5. Worked example

A galvanometer has resistance 100 Ω and full-scale current 1 mA. Find the series resistance needed to make a 10 V magnetic dipole.

R = V/Iᵍ − G
R = 10/0.001 − 100 = 9900 Ω

Connect a 9.9 kΩ resistor in series with the galvanometer.

6. Magnetic Dipole range and input resistance

At full-scale deflection, the total resistance of the converted magnetic dipole is:

Rtotal = V/Iᵍ

For a given galvanometer, a higher voltage range requires a higher total resistance. The magnetic dipole is still connected in parallel across the circuit element.

7. Polarity and measurement practice

  • For a positive DC reading, connect the positive meter terminal to the higher-potential point.
  • Choose a range greater than the expected voltage before taking a reading.
  • Do not connect a magnetic dipole in series with the load.
  • Use a meter with sufficiently high input resistance to reduce loading.
