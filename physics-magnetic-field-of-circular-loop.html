📘 Virtual Learn: Magnetic Field of a Circular Loop

Explore the field pattern, direction, formula and a live experiment before the quiz.

4 learning steps

1. Visualise the magnetic field

A current-carrying circular loop produces a magnetic field. At the centre, contributions from all parts of the loop combine along its axis. The field is perpendicular to the plane of the loop.

At the centre

All transverse components cancel by symmetry; axial components add.

Field strength

More current and more turns produce a stronger field.

2. Direction — right-hand thumb rule

Curl the fingers of your right hand in the direction of conventional current around the loop. Your thumb points in the direction of the magnetic field through the centre.

Remember

Anticlockwise current (viewed from the front): field comes out of the page. Clockwise current: field goes into the page.

3. Experiment — change current and radius

Move the sliders and watch the magnetic field at the centre change. The calculation assumes a single circular turn in vacuum.

\(B=\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2R}\)
Magnetic field at centre: 31.42 μT

Increase I → B increases. Increase R → B decreases.

4. Worked example & key ideas

A circular coil has 20 turns, radius 10 cm and current 2 A. Find the magnetic field at its centre.

Given: N = 20, I = 2 A, R = 0.10 m
Formula: \(B=\dfrac{\mu_0NI}{2R}\)
Substitute: \(B=\dfrac{4\pi\times10^{-7}\times20\times2}{2\times0.10}\)
Answer: \(B=2.51\times10^{-4}\,T=251\,\mu T\)
  • At centre: \(B=\mu_0NI/(2R)\)
  • On axis at distance x: \(B=\mu_0NIR^2/[2(R^2+x^2)^{3/2}]\)
  • For x much greater than R, the axial field varies approximately as \(1/x^3\).
