🧲 Magnetic Flux

Understand magnetic flux, area vectors, orientation, flux linkage, Gauss law for magnetism and electromagnetic induction.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations
Question 1 of 300%

1. Meaning of magnetic flux

Magnetic flux measures the magnetic field passing through a surface. It depends on field strength, area and orientation.

Φ = B · A = BA cos θ

θ is the angle between B and the area vector.

2. Area vector and orientation

The area vector is perpendicular to the surface. Measure θ from this normal, not from the plane.

B perpendicular to the plane → maximum flux. B parallel to the plane → zero flux.

3. Positive, negative and zero flux

Flux is positive when B points along the chosen normal, negative when opposite, and zero when B lies in the surface.

0°: +BA | 90°: 0 | 180°: −BA

4. SI unit and dimensions

The SI unit is weber (Wb). One weber equals one tesla metre squared or one volt-second.

1 Wb = 1 T m² = 1 V s

Dimensions: [M L² T⁻² A⁻¹].

5. Flux through a coil

For N turns, each linked by the same flux Φ, total flux linkage is NΦ. This is used in electromagnetic induction.

Flux linkage = NΦ

6. Gauss law for magnetism

The net magnetic flux through any closed surface is zero. Magnetic field lines form continuous closed loops.

∮ B · dA = 0
This law applies to closed surfaces; open-surface flux can be non-zero.

7. Changing magnetic flux

Changing B, area or orientation changes flux. A changing flux through a circuit induces emf.

ε = −N dΦ/dt

The negative sign expresses Lenz law: the induced effect opposes the change that produces it.

8. Worked example and revision

A 0.2 m² surface is perpendicular to a 0.5 T field. Its area vector is parallel to B.

Φ = BA = 0.5 × 0.2 = 0.1 Wb
  • Flux is scalar; sign depends on the chosen normal.
  • Maximum magnitude at θ = 0° or 180°.
  • Zero flux at θ = 90°.
  • Closed-surface net flux is zero.
  • Changing flux can induce emf.
