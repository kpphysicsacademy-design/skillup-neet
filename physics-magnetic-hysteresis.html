🧲 Magnetic Hysteresis

Understand the B-H hysteresis loop, saturation, retentivity, coercivity, hysteresis loss and soft and hard magnetic materials.

1. What is magnetic hysteresis?

In a ferromagnetic material, magnetic flux density or magnetization does not follow the applied magnetic field instantly. The magnetic response depends on the previous magnetic state. This lag is called hysteresis.

A complete cycle of magnetization and demagnetization produces a closed B-H hysteresis loop.

2. The B-H hysteresis loop

Starting from an unmagnetized specimen, increasing H raises B toward positive saturation. Reducing H to zero leaves remanence. A reverse field is then needed to remove it. Continuing the cycle traces the complete loop.

B-H loop: magnetic flux density B versus field strength H

The loop shows saturation, remanence, coercivity and the energy dissipated during cyclic magnetization.

3. Saturation and retentivity

Saturation: the state where most magnetic domains are aligned and further increase in H produces only a small increase in magnetization.

Retentivity (remanence): the magnetic flux density remaining when the applied field is reduced to zero after magnetization.

At H = 0, B may remain non-zero

4. Coercivity

After a specimen has been magnetized, a reverse field is required to reduce its residual magnetization or flux density to zero. The magnitude of this reverse field is called coercivity.

Coercive field = reverse H needed to remove remanence

Hard magnetic materials have high coercivity; soft magnetic materials have low coercivity.

5. Hysteresis energy loss

The area enclosed by the B-H loop gives the energy dissipated per unit volume in one complete cycle.

Energy loss per unit volume per cycle = ∮ H dB

This energy is converted mainly into heat. For a fixed loss per cycle, power loss increases with the frequency of magnetization cycles.

7. Reducing hysteresis loss

Choose a soft magnetic core material with a narrow B-H loop and low coercivity. Silicon steel and suitable ferrites are used in magnetic cores. Proper material selection reduces heating and improves the efficiency of transformers and electrical machines.

A narrow loop means a smaller area, so less energy is lost in each cycle.

8. Exam quick revision

  • Hysteresis: lag of magnetic response behind applied field.
  • Retentivity: residual magnetism at H = 0.
  • Coercivity: reverse field needed to remove remanence.
  • Loop area: energy loss per unit volume per cycle.
  • Soft magnets: narrow loop, low coercivity.
  • Hard magnets: high coercivity and retentivity.
