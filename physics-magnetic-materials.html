1. Classification of magnetic materials

Materials respond differently to an applied magnetic field. The main classes are diamagnetic, paramagnetic and ferromagnetic. Ferrimagnetic and antiferromagnetic ordering are also important in solid-state physics.

ClassResponse
DiamagneticWeakly repelled
ParamagneticWeakly attracted
FerromagneticStrongly magnetized; may retain magnetization
AntiferromagneticOpposing equal moments; near-zero net moment
FerrimagneticOpposing unequal moments; non-zero net moment

2. Diamagnetism

An applied magnetic field induces magnetic moments that oppose the applied field. Diamagnetic materials have a small negative susceptibility and are weakly repelled.

χm < 0

Examples include bismuth, copper, silver and water. Diamagnetism is present in all materials, but may be masked by stronger magnetic effects.

3. Paramagnetism

Paramagnetic atoms or ions have permanent magnetic moments that tend to align with an applied field. Thermal motion opposes alignment, so the net effect is usually weak attraction.

χm > 0 (small)

Examples include aluminium, platinum and oxygen. In many paramagnets, susceptibility decreases as temperature increases.

4. Ferromagnetism and magnetic domains

In ferromagnets such as iron, cobalt and nickel, exchange interactions favor parallel alignment of neighboring magnetic moments. The material is divided into domains, each with substantial internal alignment.

In an unmagnetized sample, domains point in different directions. An applied field moves domain walls and rotates moments, producing a large net magnetization.

Above the Curie temperature, long-range ferromagnetic order disappears and the material becomes paramagnetic.

5. Hysteresis, retentivity and coercivity

Magnetization does not follow the same path when a magnetic field is increased and decreased. This lag is called hysteresis.

Loop area = energy loss per unit volume per cycle

Retentivity: ability to retain magnetization after removing the applied field. Coercivity: reverse field needed to reduce residual magnetization to zero.

7. Antiferromagnetism and ferrimagnetism

In antiferromagnets, neighboring magnetic moments align antiparallel with equal magnitude, so the net moment is approximately zero. Above the Néel temperature, antiferromagnetic order is lost.

In ferrimagnets, moments on different sublattices are also opposed, but their magnitudes are unequal, leaving a net magnetization. Many ferrites are ferrimagnetic.

8. Exam quick revision

  • Diamagnetic: weak repulsion, negative susceptibility.
  • Paramagnetic: weak attraction, small positive susceptibility.
  • Ferromagnetic: domains, strong magnetization, Curie temperature.
  • Soft magnets: low coercivity and low hysteresis loss.
  • Hard magnets: high coercivity and retentivity.
