🪞 Mirror Formula

Master 1/f = 1/v + 1/u, signed distances, magnification and mirror-formula numericals.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations
Question 1 of 300%

1. What is the mirror formula?

The mirror formula connects the object distance (u), image distance (v) and focal length (f) of a spherical mirror. It is valid for paraxial rays and uses the Cartesian sign convention.

\\(\\frac{1}{f}=\\frac{1}{v}+\\frac{1}{u}\\)
Use signed distances in the formula. Do not substitute only positive magnitudes.

2. Meaning of u, v and f

  • u: signed distance of the object from the pole P.
  • v: signed distance of the image from the pole P.
  • f: signed focal length of the mirror.
  • R: signed radius of curvature, with R = 2f.

3. Cartesian sign convention

  • Take the pole as the origin and principal axis as the x-axis.
  • Measure all distances from the pole.
  • Distances measured in the direction of incident light are positive; opposite are negative.
  • Heights above the principal axis are positive; below it are negative.
For a real object in front of a mirror, u is usually negative. A concave mirror has f negative; a convex mirror has f positive.

4. Deriving the image-distance relation

Starting with the mirror formula, isolate the reciprocal of image distance:

\\(\\frac{1}{v}=\\frac{1}{f}-\\frac{1}{u}\\)

Combining the fractions gives:

\\(v=\\frac{fu}{u-f}\\)

This rearranged form is convenient for numerical calculations.

5. Magnification

Linear magnification is the ratio of image height to object height:

\\(m=\\frac{h_i}{h_o}=-\\frac{v}{u}\\)
  • m negative: image is inverted.
  • m positive: image is erect.
  • |m| greater than 1: enlarged; |m| less than 1: diminished.

6. Worked numerical example

A concave mirror has f = −15 cm and an object is placed at u = −30 cm.

\\(\\frac{1}{v}=\\frac{1}{-15}-\\frac{1}{-30}=-\\frac{1}{30}\\)

Therefore v = −30 cm. The image is real, inverted and the same size. Magnification is m = −v/u = −1.

Substitute signed values first, then interpret the sign of the answer.

7. Special cases and image position

  • Object at infinity: image at F.
  • Object at C (u = 2f in magnitude): image at C, same size.
  • Object at F: image at infinity.
  • Object between F and P for a concave mirror: virtual, erect and enlarged image.
  • A convex mirror forms a virtual, erect and diminished image for a real object.

8. Quick revision and common errors

  • Mirror formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u.
  • Magnification: m = −v/u = hᵢ/hₒ.
  • Radius of curvature: R = 2f.
  • Concave f is negative; convex f is positive.
  • Check units and signs before calculating.
Common error: using a positive object distance for an ordinary real object in front of a mirror.
