⚙️ Moment of Inertia

Learn how mass distribution controls a body's resistance to rotational acceleration.

Rotational dynamicsTheoremsNEET/JEE essentials

1. What is moment of inertia?

Moment of inertia (I) is the rotational analogue of mass. It tells us how difficult it is to change the rotational motion of a body about a specified axis.

I = Σ mᵢrᵢ²

Here, mᵢ is each particle's mass and rᵢ is its perpendicular distance from the axis. For a continuous body, I = ∫r² dm.

Key idea: Mass placed farther from the axis contributes much more because distance is squared.

2. Standard moments of inertia

  1. Thin ring, central perpendicular axis: I = MR²
  2. Uniform disc / solid cylinder, symmetry axis: I = ½MR²
  3. Solid sphere, diameter: I = ⅖MR²
  4. Thin spherical shell, diameter: I = ⅔MR²
  5. Uniform rod, centre and perpendicular: I = ML²/12
  6. Uniform rod, end and perpendicular: I = ML²/3

Always identify the axis before choosing a formula.

3. Radius of gyration

I = Mk²   ⇒   k = √(I/M)

Radius of gyration k is the distance from the axis at which the entire mass could be imagined concentrated to produce the same moment of inertia.

SI unit: metre (m). Dimension of I: [M L²].

4. Parallel-axis theorem

I = ICM + Md²

Moment of inertia about any axis parallel to an axis through the centre of mass equals the centre-of-mass moment plus Md², where d is the separation between axes.

Example: If ICM = 2 kg m², M = 3 kg and d = 2 m, then I = 2 + 3(2²) = 14 kg m².

5. Perpendicular-axis theorem

Iz = Ix + Iy

For a plane lamina, the moment about an axis perpendicular to its plane equals the sum of moments about two mutually perpendicular axes in the plane, all passing through the same point.

Do not apply this theorem directly to an arbitrary three-dimensional body.

6. Worked examples

Example A: A 2 kg particle is 3 m from an axis.

I = mr² = 2 × 3² = 18 kg m²

Example B: A 4 kg body has k = 0.5 m.

I = Mk² = 4 × 0.5² = 1 kg m²

Example C: A hoop and disc have equal M and R. Their moments are MR² and ½MR² respectively, so the hoop has twice the disc's moment about the central symmetry axis.

7. Exam checkpoints

  • Moment of inertia depends on mass distribution and the chosen axis.
  • For fixed torque, α = τ/I; larger I means smaller angular acceleration.
  • For geometrically similar bodies at constant density, I scales as length⁵.
  • For equal mass and scaled dimensions, I scales as length².
A hoop has greater I than a solid disc of equal mass and radius. The correct reason is that more of the hoop's mass lies farther from the axis.
