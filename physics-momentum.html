1. What is linear momentum?

Linear momentum is the quantity of motion of a body. It is the product of its mass and velocity. Momentum is a vector and points in the direction of velocity.

p = m × v

SI unit: kg m/s (equivalently N s). Dimensions: [M L T⁻¹].

2. Mass and velocity

Mass (m)Measured in kilograms (kg). For a given velocity, greater mass means greater momentum.
Velocity (v)Measured in m/s. For a given mass, doubling velocity doubles momentum.
DirectionMomentum has the same direction as velocity. Its sign depends on the chosen positive direction.
At restIf velocity is zero, linear momentum is zero, whatever the mass.

3. Momentum is a vector

In one-dimensional motion, choose a positive direction. Motion in the opposite direction has negative velocity and negative momentum. In two or three dimensions, momentum components must be added as vectors.

A 2 kg cart moves at 3 m/s east. Its momentum is 6 kg m/s east. At 3 m/s west, its momentum is 6 kg m/s west (or −6 kg m/s when east is positive).

4. Impulse and change in momentum

Impulse is the product of net force and the time interval for which it acts. It equals the change in momentum.

J = F_net × Δt = Δp

SI unit: N s, equivalent to kg m/s. A larger stopping time reduces average force for the same momentum change.

5. Newton’s second law in momentum form

The net external force is the rate of change of momentum:

F_net = Δp / Δt

For constant mass, this becomes the familiar force–acceleration relationship.

6. Conservation of linear momentum

If the net external force on a system is zero, its total momentum remains constant. Internal forces occur in equal-and-opposite pairs and do not change the system's total momentum.

For two bodies in an isolated collision: total momentum before collision = total momentum after collision. In one dimension, use signs to account for direction.

7. Worked example

Question: A 4 kg trolley moves at 5 m/s east. Find its momentum.
Formula: p = mv
Substitution: p = 4 × 5 = 20 kg m/s
Answer: 20 kg m/s east.

8. Exam reminders

  • Momentum is a vector; speed alone does not give its direction.
  • Use kg for mass and m/s for velocity.
  • Impulse equals change in momentum, not final momentum.
  • Momentum is conserved for an isolated system (zero net external impulse).
  • Define a positive direction before substituting values in collision problems.
