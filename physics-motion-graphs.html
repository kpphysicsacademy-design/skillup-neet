Motion Graphs

Graphs show how position, velocity and acceleration change with time.

📘 Learn🎮 Simulator🧠 Practice

📌 Quick Cheat Sheet

Read a motion graph by identifying its axes, then interpreting the slope or signed area.

GraphSlopeSigned area
Position–time (x–t)Velocity (m/s)No standard kinematic displacement rule
Velocity–time (v–t)Acceleration (m/s²)Displacement (m)
Acceleration–time (a–t)Rate of change of acceleration (jerk)Change in velocity (m/s)

Example 1: position changes from 2 m to 14 m in 4 s along a straight x–t segment → slope = +3 m/s.

Example 2: constant velocity −4 m/s for 3 s → v–t signed area = −12 m (displacement), whereas distance = 12 m.

Example 3: acceleration +2 m/s² for 5 s → a–t area = +10 m/s change in velocity.

NEET tip: area below the time axis is negative. A flat x–t graph means rest; a flat v–t graph means constant velocity.

1. Position–time graph

It shows the position of an object at different times.

Slope = Δ position ÷ Δ time = Velocity
  • Horizontal line: object is at rest.
  • Straight sloping line: constant velocity.
  • Changing slope: changing velocity.
  • Positive slope: positive velocity; negative slope: negative velocity.

2. Velocity–time graph

It shows how velocity changes with time.

Slope = Acceleration
Area under graph = Displacement
  • Horizontal line: constant velocity.
  • Upward straight line: constant positive acceleration.
  • Downward straight line: negative acceleration.
  • Below the time axis: negative velocity.

3. Acceleration–time graph

It shows acceleration at different times.

Area under graph = Change in velocity

A horizontal line means acceleration is constant.

Simple example

Velocity = 4 m/s for 5 s.
Area under the velocity–time graph = 4 × 5 = 20 m displacement.

Remember

  • Position–time slope gives velocity.
  • Velocity–time slope gives acceleration.
  • Velocity–time area gives displacement.
  • Speed–time area gives distance.
