⚡ Motional EMF

Understand how a moving conductor produces induced emf through magnetic force on charges, flux change and energy conversion.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations
Question 1 of 300%

1. What is motional EMF?

Motional emf is the potential difference induced across a conductor because it moves through a magnetic field. Free charges in the conductor experience magnetic force and become separated, producing a potential difference.

ε = Bℓv (when v ⟂ B and rod ⟂ v)
Motional emf is electromagnetic induction caused by the motion of a conductor relative to a magnetic field.

2. Magnetic force on moving charges

A charge q moving with velocity v in magnetic field B experiences the magnetic Lorentz force. The force separates positive and negative charges inside the rod until an electric field develops.

F = q(v × B)   ;   |F| = |q|vB sin θ

The magnetic force is perpendicular to the velocity and magnetic field. It causes charge separation; in steady state the electric force balances the magnetic force along the rod.

3. Derivation of ε = Bℓv

For a straight rod of length ℓ moving at speed v perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field B, the magnetic force magnitude on a charge is |q|vB. At equilibrium, |q|E = |q|vB, so E = vB. The potential difference across the rod is Eℓ.

ε = Eℓ = Bℓv

This simple expression applies when the rod, velocity and magnetic field have the mutually perpendicular geometry described above.

4. Direction of induced emf

Use the direction of v × B for positive charges. The right-hand rule gives the magnetic-force direction; positive charges move toward one end and negative charges toward the other. Fleming's right-hand rule is also used in generator contexts.

Reverse the direction of motion or reverse B and the polarity reverses. If v is parallel or antiparallel to B, v × B = 0.

5. Sliding rod and changing magnetic flux

When a conducting rod slides on rails and completes a closed circuit, the area of the loop changes. Therefore magnetic flux changes and an induced current flows.

Φ = BA   ;   |ε| = |dΦ/dt| = Bℓv

For a uniform field perpendicular to the loop, the rate of change of area is ℓv. This gives the same motional emf as the magnetic-force derivation.

6. Lenz's law and induced current

The induced current flows in a direction that opposes the change in magnetic flux producing it. In a sliding-rod circuit, the magnetic force on the current-carrying rod opposes its motion.

ε = − dΦ/dt
The negative sign expresses Lenz's law. It does not mean the magnitude of emf is negative.

7. Power and conservation of energy

An external agent must do work to keep the rod moving at constant speed against the magnetic opposing force. This mechanical power is converted into electrical power and then often dissipated as heat in the circuit.

P = Fv = εI = I²R = ε²/R

For a simple circuit of total resistance R, current I = ε/R. The relations assume steady speed and ideal rails/rod unless otherwise stated.

8. Worked examples and quick revision

Example: A rod of length 0.50 m moves at 4.0 m s⁻¹ perpendicular to a 2.0 T magnetic field. The induced emf is:

ε = Bℓv = 2.0 × 0.50 × 4.0 = 4.0 V

Quick revision:

  • General magnetic force: F = q(v × B).
  • For the standard perpendicular geometry: ε = Bℓv.
  • For a closed circuit: I = ε/R.
  • Flux-change approach: |ε| = |dΦ/dt|.
  • Induced current opposes the change in flux (Lenz's law).
  • Mechanical work is converted into electrical energy and heat.
