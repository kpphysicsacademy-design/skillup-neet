⚡ Moving Coil Galvanometer

Practise the operating principle, torque equation, sensitivity, damping and meter conversion.

1. Working principle

A moving-coil galvanometer detects small electric currents. A current-carrying coil placed in a magnetic field experiences a torque, causing the pointer to deflect.

Deflecting torque: τ = NIAB

N = number of turns, I = current, A = coil area and B = magnetic field.

2. Restoring torque and deflection

The suspension fibre or spring produces a restoring torque proportional to angular deflection θ.

τᵣ = kθ

At equilibrium, deflecting torque equals restoring torque:

NIAB = kθ   ⇒   θ = (NAB/k) I

Therefore, deflection is directly proportional to current.

3. Radial magnetic field

Curved pole pieces and a soft-iron cylindrical core create a radial magnetic field. The plane of the coil stays parallel to the field, keeping the torque maximum at every deflection.

  • Produces a uniform, linear scale.
  • Makes θ proportional to I.
  • Soft iron strengthens and shapes the magnetic field.

4. Sensitivity and figure of merit

Current sensitivity: θ/I = NAB/k
Voltage sensitivity: θ/V = NAB/(kG)
Figure of merit: I/θ = k/(NAB)

G is coil resistance. Increase N, A or B, or decrease k, to improve current sensitivity. A smaller figure of merit means greater sensitivity.

5. Damping

The aluminium frame produces eddy currents as the coil moves. These currents oppose the motion and help the pointer settle quickly without prolonged oscillations. This is electromagnetic damping.

6. Convert into an ammeter

Connect a low resistance called a shunt S in parallel with the galvanometer (resistance G, full-scale current Ig) to measure a larger current I.

S = IgG / (I − Ig)

The shunt carries most of the current. The ammeter has very low resistance and is connected in series.

7. Convert into a voltmeter

Connect a high resistance R in series with the galvanometer to measure voltage V.

R = V/Ig − G

The voltmeter has very high resistance and is connected in parallel across the component.

8. Exam quick revision

  • Principle: torque on a current-carrying coil.
  • Radial field gives a uniform scale.
  • Aluminium frame provides eddy-current damping.
  • Ammeter: low shunt in parallel; very low resistance; series connection.
  • Voltmeter: high resistance in series; very high resistance; parallel connection.
  • A moving-coil galvanometer is primarily a DC instrument.
