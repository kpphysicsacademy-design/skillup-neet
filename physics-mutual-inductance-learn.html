🧲 Mutual Inductance

Learn how a changing current in one coil induces an emf in another coil. Understand flux linkage, mutual inductance, coupling and transformer applications.

🌱 Easy Mode📘 Learn✨ Worked examples
📘 Learn🧠 Practice · 30 MCQs →

1. Mutual induction

Consider two coils placed close to each other. The first is the primary coil and the second is the secondary coil. When current in the primary changes, the magnetic field it produces also changes. The changing magnetic flux linked with the secondary induces an emf in it. This phenomenon is called mutual induction.

Changing primary current → changing flux → induced emf

Sequence of events

  1. Current in the primary coil changes.
  2. The magnetic field and flux produced by it change.
  3. Some of this changing flux links the secondary coil.
  4. An emf is induced in the secondary coil.
Remember: Current does not travel from one coil to the other. The changing magnetic field links the coils and induces emf.

2. Magnetic flux linkage

Magnetic flux through one turn is Φ. If a coil has N turns and the same flux passes through each turn, its flux linkage is NΦ.

Flux linkage = NΦN = number of turns; Φ = magnetic flux through one turn

Φ₂₁ represents the flux through each turn of coil 2 due to current in coil 1. The flux linkage of coil 2 is N₂Φ₂₁.

3. Coefficient of mutual inductance (M)

Mutual inductance measures how effectively a changing current in one coil produces flux linkage in the other coil.

M = N₂Φ₂₁ / I₁For a linear magnetic system with fixed coil arrangement

The induced emf in coil 2 due to changing current in coil 1 is:

ε₂ = −M (dI₁/dt)Magnitude: |ε₂| = M |dI₁/dt|

The negative sign represents Lenz's law: the induced emf opposes the change that produces it.

SI unitHenry (H)
Dimensional formula[ML²T⁻²A⁻²]

4. Meaning of one henry

Two coils have mutual inductance 1 H if a current changing at 1 A s⁻¹ in one coil induces an emf of magnitude 1 V in the other coil.

1 H = 1 V per (A s⁻¹)

5. Factors affecting mutual inductance

  • Number of turns: More turns generally increase flux linkage.
  • Coil area and shape: Affect the magnetic flux produced and captured.
  • Separation: Closer coils generally have stronger coupling.
  • Orientation: Alignment changes how much flux links the second coil.
  • Core material: A high-permeability core can increase linked flux.
  • Leakage flux: Flux that does not link the other coil reduces coupling.

6. Coefficient of coupling (k)

The coefficient of coupling describes the fraction of magnetic flux produced by one coil that links the other coil. Its value lies between zero and one.

M = k√(L₁L₂)0 ≤ k ≤ 1; L₁ and L₂ are self-inductances
k = 0No mutual flux linkage in the ideal limiting case.
k = 1Perfect coupling; all relevant flux links the other coil.

7. Worked examples

Example 1 — induced emf

M = 0.5 H and primary current changes at 4 A s⁻¹. Find induced emf magnitude.

|ε₂| = M |dI₁/dt| = 0.5 × 4 = 2 V

Example 2 — mutual inductance

L₁ = 4 H, L₂ = 9 H and k = 0.5. Find M.

M = k√(L₁L₂) = 0.5 × √(4 × 9) = 0.5 × 6 = 3 H

Example 3 — current change over time

M = 0.2 H and current changes by 3 A in 0.1 s.

ΔI/Δt = 3/0.1 = 30 A s⁻¹
|ε₂| = 0.2 × 30 = 6 V

8. Transformer application

A transformer transfers electrical energy between two windings using mutual induction. Alternating current in the primary creates changing magnetic flux in the core; this flux links the secondary and induces an alternating emf.

Vₛ / Vₚ = Nₛ / NₚFor an ideal transformer, voltage ratio equals turns ratio
TypeTurnsEffect
Step-upNₛ > NₚRaises voltage
Step-downNₛ < NₚLowers voltage

Steady DC does not produce continuing induced emf after the switching transient because the magnetic flux becomes constant. Laminated transformer cores reduce eddy-current losses.

⭐ Quick revision

  • Changing current in one coil induces emf in another coupled coil.
  • Flux linkage = NΦ.
  • Induced emf: ε₂ = −M(dI₁/dt).
  • Magnitude: |ε₂| = M|dI₁/dt|.
  • Coupling relation: M = k√(L₁L₂), where 0 ≤ k ≤ 1.
  • SI unit of mutual inductance: henry (H).
  • Transformers work on mutual induction.
