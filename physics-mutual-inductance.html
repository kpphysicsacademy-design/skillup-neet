Electromagnetic Induction

Mutual Inductance

Understand how a changing current in one coil induces an emf in another, and how this principle powers transformers.

Intermediate30 questionsConcept + numericals

1. What is mutual induction?

Mutual induction is the phenomenon in which a change of current in one coil changes the magnetic flux linked with a nearby coil, inducing an emf in that second coil. The first coil is often called the primary and the second the secondary.

e₂ = −M (dI₁/dt)

Here, e₂ is the induced emf in coil 2, I₁ is current in coil 1, and M is the mutual inductance. The minus sign expresses Lenz’s law.

2. Definition using flux linkage

For a linear arrangement, mutual inductance is the flux linkage of one coil due to unit current in the other coil.

M = N₂Φ₂₁ / I₁

N₂ is the number of turns in coil 2; Φ₂₁ is the flux through each turn of coil 2 caused by current I₁ in coil 1.

3. SI unit and dimensions

The SI unit is the henry (H). One henry is the mutual inductance that produces an emf of 1 volt when the current in the other coil changes at 1 ampere per second.

1 H = 1 V·s A⁻¹ = 1 Wb A⁻¹

Dimensional formula: [M L² T⁻² A⁻²].

4. Factors affecting mutual inductance

  • Number of turns in both coils.
  • Area, shape and relative orientation of the coils.
  • Distance and degree of magnetic coupling between coils.
  • Permeability of the medium or core material.

Closer, well-aligned coils with a suitable high-permeability core generally have stronger coupling.

5. Coefficient of coupling

The coefficient of coupling k measures the fraction of magnetic flux produced by one coil that links the other coil.

M = k√(L₁L₂)   ;   0 ≤ k ≤ 1

For ideal perfect coupling, k = 1. In practical coils, some flux is leakage flux, so k is usually less than 1.

6. Worked example

Problem: Two coils have mutual inductance 0.4 H. Current in the primary changes uniformly from 2 A to 7 A in 0.5 s. Find the magnitude of induced emf in the secondary.
Solution: |dI/dt| = (7 − 2)/0.5 = 10 A s⁻¹.
|e₂| = M|dI/dt| = 0.4 × 10 = 4 V.

7. Applications: transformer

A transformer transfers electrical energy between two windings through mutual induction. Alternating current in the primary creates changing magnetic flux in the core; this changing flux induces emf in the secondary.

Vₛ / Vₚ = Nₛ / Nₚ   (ideal transformer)

Step-up transformers have Nₛ greater than Nₚ; step-down transformers have Nₛ less than Nₚ. A transformer requires changing magnetic flux and therefore does not operate normally on steady DC.

8. Quick revision

  • Mutual induction requires changing current / changing linked flux.
  • e₂ = −M(dI₁/dt); magnitude is M|dI₁/dt|.
  • Unit of M: henry (H).
  • M = k√(L₁L₂), with k between 0 and 1.
  • Transformers work on mutual induction.
Exam tip: distinguish self-induction (emf in the same coil) from mutual induction (emf in a different, coupled coil).
