🛑 Newton’s First Law

Why does a passenger move forward when a bus stops suddenly? Newton’s first law explains how objects resist changes in their motion.

1. Statement of the law

An object remains at rest or continues moving with uniform velocity in a straight line unless acted upon by a non-zero net external force.

ΣF = 0 ⇒ a = 0 ⇒ velocity remains constant

Zero net force does not mean zero velocity. An object may be at rest or move with constant velocity.

2. Law of inertia

Newton’s first law is also called the law of inertia. Inertia is the tendency of an object to resist a change in its state of rest or uniform straight-line motion.

Inertia of restA passenger tends to remain at rest when a bus starts suddenly.
Inertia of motionA passenger tends to continue moving when a bus stops suddenly.
Inertia of directionPassengers lean sideways when a vehicle turns.
Measure of inertiaMass; greater mass means greater inertia.

3. Balanced and unbalanced forces

Balanced forcesVector sum is zero. No acceleration; velocity stays constant.
Unbalanced forcesResultant force is non-zero. Velocity changes; acceleration occurs.
Example: A book resting on a table has weight downward and normal reaction upward. If these are equal, the net force is zero and the book remains at rest.

4. Everyday applications

  1. Seat belts restrain passengers when a car brakes suddenly.
  2. Dust separates from a carpet when it is beaten because dust tends to maintain its state.
  3. A coin drops into a glass when the card beneath it is flicked away quickly.
  4. Passengers lean outward relative to a turning vehicle because their bodies tend to continue in a straight line.

5. Worked example

Question: A trolley moves east at 4 m/s. The resultant force on it is zero. Find its velocity after 6 seconds.
Principle: Zero net force means zero acceleration.
Answer: The trolley continues at 4 m/s east.

6. Exam reminders

  • Inertia is a property, not a force.
  • Zero net force means zero acceleration, not necessarily zero velocity.
  • Constant velocity requires zero resultant force.
  • Newton’s first law defines an inertial frame of reference.
