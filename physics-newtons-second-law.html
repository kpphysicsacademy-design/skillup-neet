1. Statement of Newton’s second law

The net external force acting on a body equals the rate of change of its momentum and acts in the direction of that change.

F_net = change in momentum / change in time

For constant mass, momentum p = mass × velocity (p = mv), so:

F_net = m × a   |   a = F_net/m

2. Understand the quantities

Net force (Fnet)Vector sum of all external forces. SI unit: newton (N).
Mass (m)Measure of inertia. SI unit: kilogram (kg).
Acceleration (a)Rate of change of velocity. SI unit: m/s².
Momentum (p)p = mv. SI unit: kg·m/s.

3. Force–mass relationship

  • At constant mass, acceleration is directly proportional to net force.
  • At constant net force, acceleration is inversely proportional to mass.
  • Double the net force → double the acceleration (same mass).
  • Double the mass → half the acceleration (same net force).
  • Zero net force means zero acceleration; velocity may still be constant and non-zero.

4. SI unit of force

1 N = 1 kg·m/s²

A force of one newton produces an acceleration of 1 m/s² in a 1 kg body.

5. Worked example

Question: A 4 kg trolley experiences a net horizontal force of 20 N. Find its acceleration.
Formula: a = Fnet/m = 20/4
Answer: 5 m/s² in the direction of the net force.

6. Calculate net force first

A 2 kg block is pulled right by 16 N and friction acts left with 6 N.
Fnet = 16 − 6 = 10 N right
a = 10/2 = 5 m/s² right.

7. Exam reminders

  • Use net external force, not an individual force.
  • Resolve forces along chosen axes before applying F = ma.
  • Acceleration points in the direction of net force, not necessarily velocity.
  • The momentum form is general; F = ma applies for constant mass.
