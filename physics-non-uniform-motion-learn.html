📈 Non-uniform Motion

Learn how motion changes when an object covers unequal distances in equal time intervals or its direction changes.

📘 Learn🎮 Simulator🧠 Practice

1. What is non-uniform motion?

Motion is non-uniform when velocity changes with time. This may happen because speed changes, direction changes, or both change.

Example: a city bus repeatedly speeds up, slows down and stops in traffic.

2. Average speed

Average speed is the total distance travelled divided by the total time taken.

Average speed = Total distance / Total time

Use the complete journey distance and elapsed time—not the arithmetic mean of speeds unless the time intervals are equal.

3. Average velocity

Average velocity is total displacement divided by total time. It includes direction.

Average velocity = Total displacement / Total time

Average speed is a scalar; average velocity is a vector. They can have different magnitudes.

4. Acceleration

Acceleration measures the rate of change of velocity.

aavg = (v − u) / Δt

SI unit: m/s². Acceleration can be positive, negative or zero depending on the chosen direction and velocity change.

5. Reading motion graphs

  • Position–time: slope gives velocity. A changing slope means changing velocity.
  • Velocity–time: slope gives acceleration.
  • Velocity–time area: signed area gives displacement.
  • Speed–time area: area gives distance travelled.
  • A horizontal velocity–time line means constant velocity.

6. Worked example

A cyclist travels 40 m in 5 s and then 60 m in the next 10 s. Find average speed.
Total distance = 40 + 60 = 100 m
Total time = 5 + 10 = 15 s
Average speed = 100/15 = 6.67 m/s.

7. Remember

  • Changing speed or direction means changing velocity.
  • Non-uniform motion can have non-zero acceleration.
  • Average speed is never less than the magnitude of average velocity.
  • Instantaneous velocity is the slope of the tangent to a position–time graph.
