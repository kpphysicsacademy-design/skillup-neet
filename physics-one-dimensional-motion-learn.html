📏 One-dimensional Motion — Learn

Understand position, displacement, velocity, acceleration, equations of motion and graphs.

📘 Learn🎮 Simulator🧠 Practice

1. Motion in one dimension

One-dimensional motion occurs along a single straight line. We describe an object's location using one coordinate, usually x, measured from a chosen origin.

Choose one direction as positive. Motion in the opposite direction is represented by negative velocity or displacement.

2. Position, distance and displacement

Position tells where an object is. Distance is the total path length, while displacement is the change in position.

Displacement: Δx = xf − xi

Distance is non-negative and depends on the path. Displacement may be positive, negative or zero.

3. Speed and velocity

Average speed = Total distance / Total time
Average velocity = Displacement / Time interval

Speed is scalar; velocity is signed in one dimension and indicates direction. Instantaneous velocity is the rate of change of position at a particular instant.

4. Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. It can be positive or negative depending on the selected positive direction.

aavg = (v − u) / t

A negative acceleration does not always mean slowing down. Compare the signs of velocity and acceleration to determine whether speed increases or decreases.

5. Equations of uniformly accelerated motion

When acceleration is constant, the following equations apply:

v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as
s = (u + v)t / 2

Here u = initial velocity, v = final velocity, a = constant acceleration, t = time and s = displacement.

6. Motion graphs

  • Position–time graph slope gives velocity.
  • Velocity–time graph slope gives acceleration.
  • Signed area under a velocity–time graph gives displacement.
  • A horizontal position–time graph means the object is at rest.
  • A straight-line velocity–time graph represents constant acceleration.

7. Worked example

A car starts with velocity 4 m/s and accelerates uniformly at 3 m/s² for 2 s.
Final velocity: v = u + at = 4 + 3 × 2 = 10 m/s
Displacement: s = ut + ½at² = 4 × 2 + ½ × 3 × 4 = 14 m.

Key reminders

  • Use displacement—not distance—in equations with signed velocity.
  • Keep the sign convention consistent throughout a problem.
  • Equations of motion above require constant acceleration.
  • SI units: position and displacement in m, velocity in m/s, acceleration in m/s².
