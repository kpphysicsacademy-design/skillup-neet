🔭 Optical Instruments

Explore the human eye, simple and compound microscopes, astronomical telescopes and resolving power.

1. What are optical instruments?

Optical instruments use lenses or mirrors to form images and help us observe objects that are very small, very far away, or difficult to see clearly.

  • Microscopes provide enlarged views of tiny nearby objects.
  • Telescopes provide angular magnification of distant objects.
  • The human eye forms a real image on the retina.

2. The human eye and accommodation

The eye lens changes its focal length with help from the ciliary muscles. This ability is called accommodation.

  • Near point of a normal eye: 25 cm.
  • Far point of a normal eye: infinity.
  • The image on the retina is real and inverted.
A normal eye sees distant objects with relaxed accommodation.

3. Defects of vision and correction

  • Myopia: distant objects appear blurred; corrected using a concave lens.
  • Hypermetropia: nearby objects appear blurred; corrected using a convex lens.
  • Presbyopia: reduced accommodation with age; may require suitable converging or bifocal lenses.
  • Astigmatism: commonly corrected using a cylindrical lens.

4. Simple microscope

A simple microscope is a single convex lens of short focal length used as a magnifier. The object is placed within its focal length to obtain a virtual, erect and magnified image.

\(M=1+\frac{D}{f}\quad\text{(near-point adjustment)}\)

For final image at infinity (normal adjustment), magnifying power is M = D/f, where D = 25 cm.

5. Compound microscope

A compound microscope uses two convex lenses: an objective of very short focal length and an eyepiece. The objective forms a real, inverted, enlarged intermediate image; the eyepiece magnifies it.

\(M\approx\frac{L}{f_o}\,\frac{D}{f_e}\)

Here L is tube length, fₒ is objective focal length and fₑ is eyepiece focal length (normal near-point approximation).

6. Astronomical telescope

A refracting astronomical telescope uses a large-focal-length objective and a short-focal-length eyepiece to view distant objects. In normal adjustment, the final image is at infinity.

\(M=\frac{f_o}{f_e}\quad\text{(magnitude)}\)

Its tube length in normal adjustment is approximately fₒ + fₑ.

7. Reflecting telescope and resolving power

A reflecting telescope uses a concave primary mirror to collect light. Large apertures collect more light and improve angular resolution.

For a circular aperture, the Rayleigh criterion gives the minimum angular separation approximately as θ = 1.22 λ/a, where a is aperture diameter.

Smaller θ means better resolving ability. Aperture and wavelength both matter.

8. Quick revision and common errors

  • Normal eye near point D = 25 cm; far point = infinity.
  • Myopia → concave lens; hypermetropia → convex lens.
  • Simple microscope: M = 1 + D/f (near point), D/f (infinity).
  • Compound microscope: objective then eyepiece.
  • Telescope normal adjustment: M = fₒ/fₑ; tube length = fₒ + fₑ.
  • Do not confuse linear magnification with angular magnifying power.
