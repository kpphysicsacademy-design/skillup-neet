📏 Physical Quantities

Learn measurement, SI base and derived units, scalars and vectors, prefixes, conversions and dimensions.

1. Physical quantities and measurement

A physical quantity is a property that can be measured and expressed as a numerical value multiplied by a unit. Examples include length, mass, time and temperature.

\(Q = n\times u\)

Here Q is the measured quantity, n is its numerical value and u is the unit.

2. Fundamental and derived quantities

Fundamental quantities are independent quantities. Derived quantities are obtained by combining fundamental quantities.

  • Base: length, mass, time, electric current, thermodynamic temperature, amount of substance and luminous intensity.
  • Derived: area, volume, speed, acceleration, force, energy and pressure.

3. SI base quantities and units

  • Length — metre (m)
  • Mass — kilogram (kg)
  • Time — second (s)
  • Electric current — ampere (A)
  • Thermodynamic temperature — kelvin (K)
  • Amount of substance — mole (mol)
  • Luminous intensity — candela (cd)
There are seven SI base quantities and seven corresponding base units.

4. SI derived units

Derived units are formed from base units. For example:

  • Area: m²
  • Volume: m³
  • Speed: m s⁻¹
  • Acceleration: m s⁻²
  • Force: newton (N) = kg m s⁻²
  • Energy: joule (J) = kg m² s⁻²
  • Pressure: pascal (Pa) = N m⁻²

5. Scalars and vectors

A scalar has magnitude only; a vector has both magnitude and direction.

  • Scalars: mass, time, distance, speed, temperature and energy.
  • Vectors: displacement, velocity, acceleration and force.
Distance is scalar; displacement is vector. Speed is scalar; velocity is vector.

6. Units, prefixes and conversions

SI prefixes represent powers of ten. Common examples:

  • kilo (k) = 10³; centi (c) = 10⁻²; milli (m) = 10⁻³
  • micro (μ) = 10⁻⁶; nano (n) = 10⁻⁹
  • 1 km = 1000 m; 1 m = 100 cm = 1000 mm.

7. Dimensions of physical quantities

Dimensions express a quantity in terms of fundamental dimensions. Common symbols are [M] for mass, [L] for length and [T] for time.

  • Velocity: [LT⁻¹]
  • Acceleration: [LT⁻²]
  • Force: [MLT⁻²]
  • Work/energy: [ML²T⁻²]
  • Pressure: [ML⁻¹T⁻²]

8. Measurement essentials and common errors

  • Always write a numerical value with its unit.
  • Use standard SI symbols: no plural form and no full stop after a unit symbol.
  • Dimensional consistency is necessary for a valid physical equation.
  • Significant figures indicate the precision of a measured value.
  • Do not confuse a base quantity with a derived quantity.
