📘 Learn: Position and Displacement

Understand where an object is, how its position changes, and why displacement is different from distance.

1Position

Position tells the location of an object relative to a chosen origin and coordinate system. In one dimension, we represent it using a signed coordinate such as +4 m or −3 m.

Position = coordinate measured from the origin

2Origin and direction

The origin is the reference point where x = 0. Choose a positive direction, such as right or east. Positions in the opposite direction are negative. The sign depends on the coordinate convention.

3Initial and final position

The initial position is where motion begins, written as xᵢ. The final position is where it ends, written as x𝒇. Their coordinates let us calculate the net change in position.

4Displacement

Displacement is the change from initial to final position. It is a vector: its magnitude tells how much the position changed and its sign or direction tells which way.

Δx = x𝒇 − xᵢ

5Distance vs displacement

Distance is the total length of the actual path travelled and is a scalar. Displacement depends only on the starting and ending positions. Distance is always greater than or equal to the magnitude of displacement.

Distance ≥ |Displacement|

6Direction and sign

If right is positive, a positive displacement means net motion to the right and a negative displacement means net motion to the left. A negative value does not mean a negative distance.

7Motion in two dimensions

For perpendicular changes in x and y, the displacement magnitude is found using Pythagoras. The displacement vector is the straight-line arrow from the initial point to the final point.

|Δr| = √[(Δx)² + (Δy)²]
