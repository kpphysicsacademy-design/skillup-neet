🔍 Power of a Lens

Master P = 1/f, dioptres, positive and negative lens power, and combinations of thin lenses.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations

1. Meaning of power of a lens

Power tells how strongly a lens converges or diverges light. It is the reciprocal of its focal length in metres.

\(P=\frac{1}{f}\)

Its SI-derived unit is the dioptre (D), equivalent to m⁻¹.

2. Dioptre and focal length

A lens with focal length 1 metre has a power of 1 dioptre. Shorter focal length means greater magnitude of power.

\(1\,D=1\,m^{-1}\)
Always convert focal length from centimetres to metres before using P = 1/f.

3. Sign of lens power

  • Convex (converging) lens: positive focal length and positive power.
  • Concave (diverging) lens: negative focal length and negative power.
  • A lens with zero power would have an infinite focal length.

4. Calculate power from focal length

For a convex lens of focal length +20 cm, convert f = +0.20 m.

\(P=\frac{1}{+0.20}=+5\,D\)

For a concave lens of focal length −50 cm, P = 1/(−0.50) = −2 D.

5. Calculate focal length from power

Rearrange the relation to obtain focal length from the given power.

\(f=\frac{1}{P}\)

A lens of +4 D has f = +0.25 m = +25 cm. A lens of −2 D has f = −0.50 m = −50 cm.

6. Comparing lens strengths

The magnitude of power indicates lens strength. A +8 D lens is more powerful than a +2 D lens because its focal length magnitude is smaller.

A larger positive power means stronger convergence; a larger negative magnitude means stronger divergence.

7. Combination of thin lenses in contact

When thin lenses are placed in contact, their powers add algebraically.

\(P_{eq}=P_1+P_2+P_3+\cdots\)

For +3 D and −1 D lenses in contact, equivalent power = +2 D.

8. Quick revision and common errors

  • P = 1/f when f is in metres.
  • Power is measured in dioptres (D).
  • Convex lens power is positive; concave lens power is negative.
  • For lenses in contact, add signed powers.
  • Do not use centimetres directly in the reciprocal formula.

1. The thin lens formula

The thin lens formula relates object distance (u), image distance (v) and focal length (f) for a thin lens using the Cartesian sign convention.

\( \frac{1}{f}=\frac{1}{v}-\frac{1}{u} \)
For a real object, u is negative. Convex lens f is positive; concave lens f is negative.

2. Meaning of u, v and f

  • u: signed object distance from optical centre O.
  • v: signed image distance from O.
  • f: signed focal length.
  • m: linear magnification, image height divided by object height.

3. Cartesian sign convention

  • Take O as origin and principal axis as x-axis.
  • Measure every distance from O.
  • Distances in the direction of incident light are positive; opposite are negative.
  • Heights above the axis are positive; below are negative.
For an ordinary real object on the left, u is negative. Convex f is positive; concave f is negative.

4. Rearranging the formula

Rearrange the lens formula to calculate image distance directly:

\( \frac{1}{v}=\frac{1}{f}+\frac{1}{u} \)
\( v=\frac{fu}{u+f} \)

Substitute signed values for u and f before calculating.

5. Magnification

Linear magnification is the ratio of image height to object height:

\( m=\frac{h_i}{h_o}=\frac{v}{u} \)
  • m negative: image inverted.
  • m positive: image erect.
  • |m| greater than 1: enlarged; |m| less than 1: diminished.

6. Worked numerical example

A convex lens has f = +15 cm and an object is placed at u = −30 cm.

\( \frac{1}{v}=\frac{1}{15}-\frac{1}{-30} \)

Correct substitution gives 1/v = 1/15 + 1/30 = 1/10, so v = +10 cm. The image is real and inverted; m = 10/(−30) = −1/3.

Keep the minus sign in the formula: subtracting a negative object distance becomes addition.

7. Special cases and image position

  • Object at infinity: image at F₂.
  • Object at 2F₁: image at 2F₂, same size.
  • Object between F₁ and 2F₁: image beyond 2F₂, enlarged.
  • Object at F₁: image at infinity.
  • Object inside F₁: virtual, erect and enlarged image on object side.
  • A concave lens forms a virtual, erect and diminished image for a real object.

8. Quick revision and common errors

  • Lens formula: 1/f = 1/v − 1/u.
  • Magnification: m = v/u = hᵢ/hₒ.
  • Power: P = 1/f, with f in metres.
  • Thin lenses in contact: equivalent powers add.
  • Convex f positive; concave f negative.
Do not use the mirror formula 1/f = 1/v + 1/u for a lens.
