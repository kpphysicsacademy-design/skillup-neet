⚖️ Principle of Homogeneity

Understand why every term in a valid physical equation must have matching dimensions. Learn to test sums, equations and function arguments step by step.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations
Question 1 of 300%

1. Meaning of dimensional homogeneity

The principle of dimensional homogeneity states that a physically meaningful equation must have the same dimensions on its two sides. Every term that is added or subtracted must also have identical dimensions.

[LHS] = [RHS]
Dimensions must match even when the numerical values or units are different.

2. Why can only like dimensions be added?

Addition combines quantities of the same physical kind. Length can be added to length, energy to energy, and velocity to velocity—but length cannot be added to time or force.

3 m + 2 m = 5 m

An expression such as 3 m + 2 s has no physical meaning.

3. Testing an equation term by term

Replace each physical quantity with its dimensional formula. Simplify products and powers, then compare all terms.

s = ut + ½at²
  • [s] = L
  • [ut] = (LT⁻¹)(T) = L
  • [at²] = (LT⁻²)(T²) = L

All terms have dimension L, so the equation passes the test.

4. Homogeneity in equations of motion

Consider the equation for final velocity:

v = u + at

[v] = [u] = LT⁻¹ and [at] = (LT⁻²)(T) = LT⁻¹. Thus, the sum is dimensionally valid.

A term may be a product of quantities; simplify its complete dimensions before comparing.

5. Squared and higher-power terms

For an equation such as v² = u² + 2as, compare the dimensions of the complete terms, including powers.

[v²] = [u²] = [as] = L²T⁻²

The numerical factor 2 is dimensionless and does not affect the dimensional formula.

6. Dimensionless arguments of functions

The argument of a trigonometric, exponential or logarithmic function must be dimensionless.

  • sin(ωt): ωt is dimensionless because [ω] = T⁻¹.
  • cos(kx): kx is dimensionless because [k] = L⁻¹.
  • e^(−t/τ): t/τ is dimensionless.
The function's argument—not necessarily its amplitude—must be dimensionless.

7. What homogeneity can and cannot prove

Dimensional homogeneity is a necessary consistency condition, not proof that an equation is physically correct. A dimensionally correct equation may still have a wrong sign, missing term, incorrect numerical constant or vector direction.

For example, dimensional analysis cannot distinguish between ½mv² and 3mv².

8. A reliable exam method

  1. Write the dimensional formula of the left-hand side.
  2. Find the dimensions of every separate term on the right-hand side.
  3. Check that terms joined by + or − match.
  4. Compare the dimensions on both sides.
  5. Check that function arguments are dimensionless.

If any required comparison fails, the equation is dimensionally inconsistent.
