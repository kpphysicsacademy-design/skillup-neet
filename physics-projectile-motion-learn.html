🚀 Projectile Motion

Learn how an object moves in two dimensions when it is launched into the air and gravity acts on it.

📘 Learn🎮 Simulator🧠 Practice

1. What is projectile motion?

Projectile motion is the two-dimensional motion of an object projected into the air and then allowed to move under gravity alone. Air resistance is neglected in the ideal model.

Examples: a kicked football, a thrown ball, or a stone launched at an angle.

2. Resolve the launch velocity

If the object is projected with initial speed u at an angle θ above the horizontal, split its velocity into perpendicular components.

uₓ = u cos θ   |   uᵧ = u sin θ

Horizontal velocity remains constant. Vertical velocity changes because of downward gravitational acceleration.

3. Horizontal and vertical motion

Horizontal: x = (u cos θ)t
Vertical: y = (u sin θ)t − ½gt²

The two motions occur simultaneously and are independent. The acceleration is aₓ = 0 and aᵧ = −g when upward is positive.

4. Time of flight

For a projectile that lands at the same vertical level from which it was launched:

T = 2u sin θ / g

Time of flight is the total time in the air. This formula assumes equal launch and landing heights.

5. Maximum height

At the highest point, the vertical component of velocity becomes zero, but gravity still acts downward.

H = u² sin² θ / 2g

6. Horizontal range

Range is the horizontal distance travelled before the projectile returns to its launch height.

R = u² sin 2θ / g

For a fixed launch speed on level ground, the maximum range occurs at 45°.

7. Important observations

  • The trajectory is a parabola in the ideal model.
  • Horizontal acceleration is zero; vertical acceleration is constant and downward.
  • At the highest point, vertical velocity is zero while horizontal velocity is unchanged.
  • Complementary angles θ and (90° − θ) give equal ranges for the same speed on level ground.
  • Doubling launch speed makes the range four times as large at the same angle.

8. Worked example

A ball is projected at 20 m/s at 30°. Take g = 10 m/s² and assume it lands at its launch height.
Time: T = 2 × 20 × sin 30° / 10 = 2 s
Maximum height: H = (20² × sin² 30°) / 20 = 5 m
Range: R = 20² × sin 60° / 10 = 20√3 ≈ 34.6 m
