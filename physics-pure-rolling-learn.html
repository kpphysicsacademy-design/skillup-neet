Pure Rolling

Understand rolling without slipping and the motion of every point on a rolling wheel.

vcm = Rω

Pure rolling combines translation of the centre of mass and rotation about the centre. The point touching a stationary surface is instantaneously at rest relative to the ground.

1. No-slip condition

vcm = Rω

Here R is wheel radius, vcm is centre speed and ω is angular speed in rad/s. If this relation is not satisfied, the wheel slips at the contact.

Example: R = 0.25 m and v = 3 m/s gives ω = 12 rad/s.

2. Velocity at different points

Top point
2v

Translation and rotation add.

Contact point
0

The two velocity contributions cancel.

Centre
v

Centre-of-mass translation.

Side point
√2v

Perpendicular velocity components.

3. Acceleration condition

acm = Rα

For a wheel rolling without slipping on a fixed straight surface, the magnitudes of linear and angular acceleration are related by a = Rα.

4. Total kinetic energy

K = ½Mv² + ½Icmω²

Substitute ω = v/R to obtain:

K = ½Mv² [1 + Icm/(MR²)]

5. Standard rolling bodies

Solid sphere

I = ⅖MR²

K = ⁷⁄₁₀Mv²

Solid cylinder

I = ½MR²

K = ¾Mv²

Thin hoop

I = MR²

K = Mv²

Hollow sphere

I = ⅔MR²

K = ⅚Mv²

6. Static friction

Static friction may provide the torque needed to start or maintain rolling. In ideal pure rolling, it does no work at the instantaneous contact point because that point has zero velocity relative to the surface.

Remember: Zero instantaneous velocity does not mean zero acceleration.

7. Worked example

A solid cylinder of mass 4 kg rolls at 5 m/s. Find its total kinetic energy.

K = ¾Mv² = ¾ × 4 × 25 = 75 J.

Quick revision

  • Pure rolling: v = Rω.
  • Top speed = 2v; contact speed = 0.
  • Total energy is translational plus rotational.
  • One revolution advances the wheel by 2πR.
