📻 Radio Waves

Understand radio-wave properties, production, antennas, modulation, propagation and communication applications.

1. What are radio waves?

Radio waves are electromagnetic waves with relatively long wavelengths and low frequencies. They are transverse waves consisting of mutually perpendicular electric and magnetic fields, and they can travel through vacuum.

Key idea: radio waves do not require a material medium.

2. Position in the electromagnetic spectrum

Radio waves occupy the long-wavelength, low-frequency end of the electromagnetic spectrum. Their wavelength is generally longer than that of microwaves.

Radio → Microwave → Infrared → Visible → UV → X-ray → Gamma

Moving from radio waves towards gamma rays, frequency and photon energy increase while wavelength decreases.

3. Speed, wavelength and frequency

In vacuum, radio waves travel at the speed of light. Their frequency and wavelength are related by:

\(c=f\lambda\)

Here, \(c\approx3.0\times10^8\;\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), \(f\) is frequency and \(\lambda\) is wavelength.

Example: for a 100 MHz signal, \(\lambda=3\times10^8/(100\times10^6)=3\;\mathrm m\).

4. Production and transmission

Radio waves are produced by accelerating electric charges. In a transmitting antenna, an alternating current makes charges oscillate, producing changing electric and magnetic fields that radiate outward.

5. Reception and antenna

An incoming radio wave induces a tiny alternating emf and current in a receiving antenna. A receiver selects the desired frequency, amplifies and demodulates the signal, then converts the information into sound or data.

  • Transmitting antenna: converts electrical signal energy into radio waves.
  • Receiving antenna: converts part of the incoming wave energy into an electrical signal.
  • Tuning circuit: selects a desired station or channel.

6. Modulation: AM and FM

Modulation places information onto a high-frequency carrier wave.

  • Amplitude modulation (AM): carrier amplitude varies with the message signal; carrier frequency is approximately constant.
  • Frequency modulation (FM): instantaneous carrier frequency varies with the message signal; carrier amplitude is approximately constant.

7. Propagation and applications

Radio-wave propagation depends on frequency, antenna height, terrain and the ionosphere.

  • Ground waves can follow the Earth's surface, especially at lower frequencies.
  • Sky waves may be refracted back towards Earth by ionospheric layers in suitable frequency bands.
  • Line-of-sight propagation is important at VHF and higher frequencies.

Applications include AM/FM broadcasting, television, two-way radio, navigation, communication and radio astronomy.

8. Quick revision

  • Radio waves are electromagnetic and transverse.
  • They travel at \(3\times10^8\;\mathrm{m/s}\) in vacuum.
  • \(c=f\lambda\): higher frequency means shorter wavelength in vacuum.
  • Accelerating charges and oscillating antenna currents produce radio waves.
  • AM varies amplitude; FM varies frequency.
  • Some lower-frequency bands use ionospheric sky-wave propagation.
Remember: antennas transmit and receive electromagnetic signals; modulation carries information.
