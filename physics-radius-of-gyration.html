🔄 Radius of Gyration

Understand the effective distance that represents how a body's mass is distributed around a chosen axis.

1. Meaning and definition

Radius of gyration (k) is the distance from an axis at which the entire mass of a body may be assumed concentrated without changing its moment of inertia.

I = Mk²
Think of k as the root-mean-square distance of the body's mass from the selected axis. It depends on the axis, not just the object's shape.

2. Important formulas

k = √(I/M)   and   I = Mk²
  • For particles: k² = (Σmᵢrᵢ²)/(Σmᵢ)
  • For equal masses: k = √[(r₁²+r₂²+…+rₙ²)/n]
  • Dimension: [L]
  • SI unit: metre (m)

3. Standard examples

  • Thin ring about central perpendicular axis: I = MR², so k = R.
  • Uniform disc about central perpendicular axis: I = ½MR², so k = R/√2.
  • Uniform rod about centre: I = ML²/12, so k = L/√12.
  • Uniform rod about one end: I = ML²/3, so k = L/√3.
  • Solid sphere about diameter: I = ⅖MR², so k = R√(2/5).

4. Link with parallel-axis theorem

k² = kCM² + d²

If an axis is parallel to the centre-of-mass axis and separated by d, the parallel-axis theorem gives this relation after dividing by mass.

Example: kCM = 3 m and d = 4 m:

k = √(3² + 4²) = 5 m

5. Solved numericals

Example 1: I = 16 kg m², M = 4 kg.

k = √(16/4) = 2 m

Example 2: M = 2 kg, k = 3 m.

I = 2 × 3² = 18 kg m²

Example 3: I = 9 kg m², k = 1.5 m.

M = 9/(1.5²) = 4 kg

6. Common exam mistakes

  • Do not confuse k (length) with I (kg m²).
  • Always specify the axis before quoting k.
  • If I increases four times at fixed mass, k doubles—not quadruples.
  • If mass increases four times at fixed I, k becomes half.
Quick check: A ring and disc have equal mass and radius. The ring has k = R, while the disc has k = R/√2. Therefore the ring has the larger radius of gyration about the central axis.
