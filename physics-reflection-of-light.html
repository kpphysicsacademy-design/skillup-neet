💡 Reflection of Light

Understand the laws of reflection, ray diagrams, plane mirrors, diffuse reflection and spherical mirrors.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations

1. What is reflection of light?

Reflection is the change in direction of light when it strikes a surface and returns into the original medium. Smooth polished surfaces can produce clear images, while rough surfaces scatter reflected light.

\(\text{Angle of incidence}=\text{Angle of reflection}\)

Light rays are models used to show the direction in which light travels.

2. Laws of reflection

Two laws govern reflection:

  • The incident ray, reflected ray and normal at the point of incidence lie in the same plane.
  • The angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence.

3. Normal, angle of incidence and angle of reflection

The normal is an imaginary line perpendicular (90°) to the reflecting surface at the point where the incident ray strikes it.

  • Angle of incidence (i): between incident ray and normal.
  • Angle of reflection (r): between reflected ray and normal.
  • For reflection, (i=r).
If a ray makes 30° with the mirror surface, it makes 60° with the normal.

4. Regular and diffuse reflection

Regular (specular) reflection: parallel incident rays reflect in an orderly, parallel manner from a smooth surface.

Diffuse reflection: parallel incident rays reflect in many directions from a rough surface. Each ray still obeys the law of reflection at its local point.

\(i=r\quad\text{for each reflected ray}\)

5. Reflection by a plane mirror

A plane mirror forms an image that is virtual, erect and the same size as the object. The image appears as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.

  • Image is laterally inverted (left and right appear interchanged).
  • Image cannot be obtained on a screen.
  • Object distance equals image distance in magnitude.
\(|v|=|u|\qquad m=+1\)

6. Multiple reflection and applications

When light reflects successively from two or more mirrors, multiple images may form. The number and arrangement depend on the angle between mirrors and object position.

Periscopes use plane mirrors or prisms to redirect light. Kaleidoscopes use repeated reflections between inclined mirrors to create patterns.

7. Spherical mirrors: concave and convex

A spherical mirror has a reflecting surface that forms part of a sphere. A concave mirror has its reflecting surface curved inward and can converge parallel rays. A convex mirror curves outward and diverges reflected rays.

  • Pole (P): centre of the mirror surface.
  • Centre of curvature (C): centre of the parent sphere.
  • Principal focus (F): point where parallel paraxial rays converge or appear to diverge.
  • Radius of curvature (R) and focal length (f): (R=2f) in magnitude for a spherical mirror under paraxial approximation.

8. Quick revision

  • Reflection means light returns from a surface into the original medium.
  • The incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in one plane.
  • Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection; measure both from the normal.
  • Regular reflection occurs at smooth surfaces; diffuse reflection at rough surfaces.
  • Plane mirror image: virtual, erect, same size, laterally inverted.
  • For spherical mirrors, (R=2f) (magnitude).
Remember: a rough surface causes diffuse reflection because its local normals vary, not because the law of reflection stops applying.
