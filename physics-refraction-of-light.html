💡 Reflection of Light

Understand the laws of reflection, ray diagrams, plane mirrors, diffuse reflection and spherical mirrors.

1. What is refraction?

Refraction is the change in direction of light when it passes obliquely from one transparent medium to another because its speed changes. At normal incidence, the speed changes but the ray does not bend.

The frequency remains constant at a boundary; speed and wavelength change.

2. Optical density and refractive index

The absolute refractive index of a medium compares the speed of light in vacuum with its speed in that medium.

n = c / v

Here c is the speed of light in vacuum and v is its speed in the medium. A larger refractive index means a lower speed of light.

3. Laws of refraction

  • The incident ray, refracted ray and normal lie in the same plane.
  • For a given pair of media, the ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of refraction is constant.
n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r

Angles i and r are measured from the normal, not from the surface.

4. Bending towards and away from the normal

On entering a medium with higher refractive index, light slows down and bends towards the normal. On entering a lower-index medium, it speeds up and bends away from the normal. A ray along the normal has zero angular deviation.

Air → glass: towards normal. Glass → air: away from normal.

5. Speed, wavelength and frequency

At a stationary boundary, the frequency is unchanged. Since v = fλ, a reduction in speed produces a proportional reduction in wavelength.

v = fλ   and   λ₂ / λ₁ = n₁ / n₂

For light entering glass from air, wavelength decreases while frequency remains the same.

6. Relative refractive index

The refractive index of medium 2 relative to medium 1 is the ratio of the speed in medium 1 to the speed in medium 2.

n₂₁ = n₂ / n₁ = v₁ / v₂

For air-to-glass, taking n₁ ≈ 1 gives n₂₁ ≈ n₂.

7. Total internal reflection

When light travels from an optically denser medium to a rarer medium, the refracted ray bends away from the normal. At the critical angle C, the refracted ray travels along the boundary.

sin C = n₂ / n₁   (n₁ > n₂)

For incidence greater than C, total internal reflection occurs. Applications include optical fibres, prisms and the brilliance of diamonds.

8. Applications and quick revision

  • Apparent depth: a submerged object appears shallower when viewed from air.
  • Optical fibres guide light by repeated total internal reflection.
  • A prism refracts light at its two faces and can disperse white light.
  • Mirage formation involves refraction through layers of air at different temperatures.
  • Always measure angles from the normal and use consistent medium labels in Snell’s law.
