🏃 Relative Motion

Describe how an object moves when observed from another moving object or reference frame.

📘 Learn🎮 Simulator🧠 Practice
Progress: 0/3 pages completed

1. Reference frame

Motion is described relative to an observer or reference frame. A passenger sitting in a moving bus is at rest relative to the bus but moves relative to the road.

2. Relative position

rAB = rA − rB

The position of A relative to B is the vector from B to A.

3. Relative velocity

vAB = vA − vB

Subtract the velocity of the observer from the velocity of the object. Relative velocity is a vector, so signs and directions matter.

Same direction

Relative speed is the difference of speeds. Example: 20 − 12 = 8 m/s.

Opposite directions

Relative speed is the sum of speeds. Example: 20 + 12 = 32 m/s.

4. Motion in two dimensions

Resolve velocities into perpendicular components and add or subtract components separately.

|v| = √(vx² + vy²)

A swimmer moving north at 3 m/s in a river flowing east at 4 m/s has a ground speed of 5 m/s.

5. Boats and streams

Downstream

Boat and current point in the same direction: vd = u + v.

Upstream

Boat opposes the current: vu = u − v.

6. Rain and moving observers

The apparent direction of rain depends on rain velocity relative to the observer: vrain,observer = vrain − vobserver.

7. Worked example

Car A travels east at 25 m/s and car B travels east at 17 m/s. Find A's velocity relative to B.

Solution: Take east as positive. vAB = 25 − 17 = 8 m/s east.

8. Common mistakes

  • Reversing the order in vAB = vA − vB.
  • Adding speeds when both objects move in the same direction.
  • Ignoring vector components in two-dimensional motion.
  • Mixing km/h and m/s.
