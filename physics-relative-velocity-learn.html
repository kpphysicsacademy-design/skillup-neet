Relative Velocity

Understand motion as seen by a moving observer. Learn how to compare velocities in the same direction, opposite directions and two dimensions.

LearnSimulatorPractice

1. What is relative velocity?

Relative velocity is the velocity of one object as measured from another moving or stationary object. It tells how quickly and in which direction the separation between two objects changes.

Velocity of A relative to B = v⃗A − v⃗B

2. Motion along a straight line

Choose one direction as positive and give every velocity its correct sign. Subtract the observer's velocity from the object's velocity.

vAB = vA − vB
  • Same direction: relative speed is usually the difference of speeds.
  • Opposite directions: relative speed is the sum of speeds.
  • A negative result indicates direction opposite to the chosen positive axis.

3. Two-dimensional relative velocity

Velocity is a vector. Subtract x-components and y-components separately, then calculate the magnitude and direction of the relative velocity.

vAB,x = vA,x − vB,x
vAB,y = vA,y − vB,y
|v⃗AB| = √(vAB,x² + vAB,y²)

4. Objects moving in perpendicular directions

When two velocities are perpendicular, use Pythagoras to find the magnitude of their relative velocity. For example, if A moves east at 6 m/s and B moves north at 8 m/s, then the relative velocity components are (−6, 8) m/s.

|v⃗BA| = √(6² + 8²) = 10 m/s

5. Rain, river and wind problems

These problems use the same vector rule. The velocity of a boat relative to the ground is the velocity of the boat relative to water plus the velocity of water relative to the ground. A plane's ground velocity is its velocity relative to air plus wind velocity.

v⃗object/ground = v⃗object/medium + v⃗medium/ground

6. Worked examples

Example 1 — same direction: Car A moves east at 20 m/s and car B at 12 m/s. Taking east as positive, vAB = 20 − 12 = 8 m/s east.

Example 2 — opposite directions: Two cyclists move towards each other at 6 m/s and 4 m/s. Their closing speed is 6 + 4 = 10 m/s.

Example 3 — perpendicular: A boat's velocity north is 4 m/s and river current east is 3 m/s. Ground speed = √(4² + 3²) = 5 m/s.

Quick revision

  • Always identify the observer or reference frame.
  • Use signed components; do not add speeds blindly.
  • Relative velocity is a vector difference.
  • For perpendicular components, use Pythagoras.
  • For medium-based motion, use vector addition along the chain of reference frames.
