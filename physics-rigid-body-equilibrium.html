⚖️ Rigid Body Equilibrium

A rigid body is in equilibrium when it has no linear acceleration and no angular acceleration. Forces and turning effects must balance together.

ΣF = 0    and    Στ = 0

Equilibrium does not mean that no forces act. Several forces may act on a body, provided their vector sum and their net torque are both zero.

1. Translational equilibrium

The resultant external force must be zero along every independent direction.

ΣFx = 0    ΣFy = 0

For a body at rest, this means the centre of mass has no acceleration. For example, the upward support force on a horizontal table balances the downward weight of a stationary book.

2. Rotational equilibrium

The algebraic sum of all external torques about a chosen point must be zero.

Στ = 0    or    Στclockwise = Στanticlockwise

Choose a sign convention and use it consistently. In a planar problem, anticlockwise is often taken as positive and clockwise as negative.

Taking moments about a support or pivot can eliminate unknown forces whose lines of action pass through that point.

3. Torque and moment arm

τ = rF sin θ = Fd⊥
  • r is the distance from pivot to the point of application.
  • θ is the angle between the position vector and force.
  • d⊥ is the perpendicular distance from pivot to the force's line of action.
  • Torque is maximum when force is perpendicular to r and zero when its line passes through the pivot.

SI unit: N m. Torque is a vector; its direction follows the right-hand rule.

4. Centre of gravity and support reactions

The weight of a body acts through its centre of gravity. For a uniform beam in a uniform gravitational field, this is its midpoint.

For a beam supported at two points, use vertical force balance to relate the support reactions to the total downward load, then use torque balance to determine their distribution.

5. Worked example: balanced lever

A 40 N load is placed 0.5 m to the left of a pivot. What downward force at 1.0 m to the right balances the lever?

40 × 0.5 = F × 1.0
F = 20 N

Clockwise and anticlockwise moments are equal, so the net torque is zero.

6. A reliable problem-solving method

  1. Draw a free-body diagram and show every external force.
  2. Choose positive x and y directions.
  3. Write ΣFx = 0 and ΣFy = 0.
  4. Choose a convenient pivot and write Στ = 0.
  5. Use perpendicular lever arms and check units and signs.

Remember: complete rigid-body equilibrium requires both force balance and torque balance. Practise with the simulator before attempting the quiz.
