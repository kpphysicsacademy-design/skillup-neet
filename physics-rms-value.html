📐 RMS Value

Understand root mean square values of alternating current and voltage, derive the sinusoidal RMS formula, and apply it to heating and power calculations.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations
Question 1 of 300%

1. Meaning of RMS value

RMS means root mean square. It is the square root of the mean of the squares of the instantaneous values over one complete cycle. For AC, it gives the equivalent DC value that produces the same average heating effect in a resistor.

\[X_{\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{\langle x^2\rangle}\]

The method works for current and voltage. RMS values are commonly used to specify AC supplies and calculate resistive heating.

RMS is not the ordinary arithmetic average. A sinusoidal AC has zero full-cycle average but a non-zero RMS value.

2. RMS current for a sinusoidal wave

Consider a sinusoidal current \(i=I_0\sin\omega t\). Its RMS value is the square root of the mean of \(i^2\) over one period T.

\[I_{\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T i^2\,dt}\]

Substituting \(i=I_0\sin\omega t\), and using the mean value of \(\sin^2\) over one complete cycle as 1/2:

\[I_{\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{I_0^2\left(\frac12\right)}=\frac{I_0}{\sqrt2}\]

3. RMS voltage for a sinusoidal wave

Similarly, for \(v=V_0\sin\omega t\), the RMS voltage is obtained by taking the square root of the mean square voltage.

\[V_{\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T v^2\,dt}\]
\[V_{\mathrm{rms}}=\frac{V_0}{\sqrt2}\approx0.707V_0\]

The factor 0.707 applies to a pure sinusoidal waveform, not automatically to every possible AC waveform.

4. RMS, peak and average values

For a sinusoidal current or voltage, RMS is 0.707 times the peak value. The average over a complete cycle is zero because the positive and negative halves cancel. The average over one positive half-cycle is \(2/\pi\) times the peak.

\[X_{\mathrm{rms}}=0.707X_0\]
\[X_{\mathrm{avg,half}}=\frac{2X_0}{\pi}\approx0.637X_0\]
\[X_{\mathrm{avg,full}}=0\]

Do not confuse RMS value with average value or peak value.

5. Heating effect and power in a resistor

For a resistor R carrying AC, the instantaneous power is \(p=i^2R\). The average power over a cycle can be expressed using RMS current or RMS voltage.

\[P_{\mathrm{avg}}=I_{\mathrm{rms}}^2R\]
\[P_{\mathrm{avg}}=\frac{V_{\mathrm{rms}}^2}{R}=V_{\mathrm{rms}}I_{\mathrm{rms}}\]

These forms apply to a pure resistor, where voltage and current are in phase.

6. RMS value of constant DC and other waveforms

For a constant DC quantity, the instantaneous value does not change, so its RMS value equals its constant value. The familiar peak-to-RMS ratio of \(\sqrt2\) is specific to a sinusoidal waveform.

\[X_{\mathrm{rms,DC}}=X_{\mathrm{DC}}\]

For a general periodic waveform, calculate the mean of its square over one period and then take the square root.

Always check the waveform before applying \(X_{\mathrm{rms}}=X_0/\sqrt2\).

7. Worked numerical examples

Example 1: Peak current is 10 A. Find RMS current.

\[I_{\mathrm{rms}}=\frac{10}{\sqrt2}=7.07\,\mathrm A\]

Example 2: RMS voltage is 220 V. Find peak voltage.

\[V_0=\sqrt2(220)\approx311\,\mathrm V\]

Example 3: A 20 Ω resistor is connected to 100 V RMS.

\[I_{\mathrm{rms}}=\frac{100}{20}=5\,\mathrm A\]
\[P=\frac{100^2}{20}=500\,\mathrm W\]

8. Quick revision and common mistakes

  • Definition: \(X_{\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{\text{mean of }X^2}\).
  • Sinusoidal current: \(I_{\mathrm{rms}}=I_0/\sqrt2\).
  • Sinusoidal voltage: \(V_{\mathrm{rms}}=V_0/\sqrt2\).
  • Full-cycle average of a sine wave: zero.
  • Positive half-cycle average: \(2X_0/\pi\).
  • Resistive power: \(P=I_{\mathrm{rms}}^2R=V_{\mathrm{rms}}^2/R\).
  • Constant DC: RMS equals its constant value.
