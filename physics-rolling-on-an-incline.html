⛰️ Rolling on an Incline

Understand how translation and rotation combine when a rigid body rolls down a sloping surface without slipping.

acm = g sin θ / [1 + Icm/(mR²)]

Gravity pulls the body down the plane. Static friction acts up the plane for a body released from rest, providing the torque that increases its angular speed.

1. Forces on the rolling body

  • Weight mg acts vertically downward.
  • Normal reaction N = mg cos θ, perpendicular to the plane.
  • Component of weight down the plane = mg sin θ.
  • Static friction f acts up the plane for the usual rolling-down-from-rest case.
mg sin θ − f = ma
fR = Icm α,   a = Rα

2. Acceleration for different bodies

Solid sphereI = 2mR²/5
a = 5g sinθ/7
Solid cylinder / discI = mR²/2
a = 2g sinθ/3
Thin hoopI = mR²
a = g sinθ/2
Hollow sphere (thin)I = 2mR²/3
a = 3g sinθ/5

For the same incline, the smaller the ratio I/(mR²), the greater the linear acceleration.

3. Speed after descending height h

Using conservation of mechanical energy, the loss in gravitational potential energy becomes translational plus rotational kinetic energy.

mgh = ½mv² + ½Icm(v²/R²)
v² = 2gh / [1 + Icm/(mR²)]

The final speed depends on the shape (moment of inertia) and vertical drop, not on mass or the path length for ideal rolling.

4. Worked example

A solid sphere rolls from rest down a 30° incline. Take g = 9.8 m/s². Find its acceleration.

a = (5/7)g sin 30°
a = (5/7) × 9.8 × 0.5 = 3.5 m/s²

The sphere accelerates at 3.5 m/s² down the plane.

5. Key exam reminders

  • Pure rolling: v = Rω and a = Rα.
  • Static friction prevents slipping; it is not necessarily equal to μN.
  • For an ideal stationary incline, static friction does no work at the instantaneous contact point.
  • Check the moment of inertia about the centre of mass before substituting.
