Rotational Kinematics

Understand how a rotating object changes its angular position, angular velocity and angular acceleration with time.

CORE CONCEPTFORMULAS + EXAMPLES

1. What is rotational kinematics?

Rotational kinematics describes the motion of a body rotating about a fixed axis, without discussing the forces or torques causing that motion. It uses angular quantities in the same way linear kinematics uses displacement, velocity and acceleration.

Linear motion ↔ Rotational motion
x ↔ θ   |   v ↔ ω   |   a ↔ α

2. Angular displacement (θ)

Angular displacement is the change in angular position of a rotating body. It is measured in radians (rad). Choose a sign convention: anticlockwise is usually positive and clockwise negative.

Δθ = θ − θ₀

One complete revolution = 2π rad = 360°. Half a revolution = π rad; one quarter = π/2 rad.

3. Angular velocity (ω)

Angular velocity tells how quickly angular displacement changes with time. Average angular velocity is displacement divided by elapsed time; instantaneous angular velocity is its time derivative.

ωavg = Δθ / Δt    |    ω = dθ/dt

SI unit: rad s⁻¹. Positive and negative signs indicate the chosen direction of rotation.

4. Angular acceleration (α)

Angular acceleration describes the rate of change of angular velocity.

αavg = Δω / Δt    |    α = dω/dt

SI unit: rad s⁻². If α is positive, angular velocity increases in the positive direction; whether the object speeds up depends on the signs of ω and α.

Remember: If ω and α have the same sign, the magnitude of angular velocity increases. If their signs are opposite, it decreases.

5. Equations for constant angular acceleration

When angular acceleration is constant, the three rotational kinematic equations are:

ω = ω₀ + αt
Δθ = ω₀t + ½αt²
ω² = ω₀² + 2αΔθ

Here, ω₀ is initial angular velocity, ω is final angular velocity, t is elapsed time and Δθ is angular displacement.

These equations require constant α. For variable angular acceleration, use the definitions ω = dθ/dt and α = dω/dt.

6. Connection with linear motion

For a point at perpendicular distance r from the axis of a rigid rotating body:

Tangential speed: v = rω
Tangential acceleration: at = rα
Radial acceleration: ar = rω²

All points on a rigid body have the same angular displacement, angular velocity and angular acceleration, but their linear speeds depend on their distance from the axis.

7. Worked example

Question: A wheel starts from rest and has constant angular acceleration 2 rad s⁻² for 5 s. Find its final angular velocity and angular displacement.

Given: ω₀ = 0, α = 2 rad s⁻², t = 5 s.

Final angular velocity: ω = ω₀ + αt = 0 + 2 × 5 = 10 rad s⁻¹.

Angular displacement: Δθ = ω₀t + ½αt² = 0 + ½ × 2 × 25 = 25 rad.
