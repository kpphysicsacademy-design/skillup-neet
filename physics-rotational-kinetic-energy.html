🔄 Rotational Kinetic Energy

Understand the energy of a rotating body, connect it with moment of inertia and solve common exam numericals.

Krot = ½ Iω²

I = moment of inertia about the rotation axis; ω = angular velocity in rad s⁻¹. The energy is measured in joules (J).

1. Meaning

When a rigid body rotates, every particle moves in a circular path around the axis. The total kinetic energy of all its particles is called rotational kinetic energy.

A larger moment of inertia or a larger angular speed means more rotational kinetic energy. For fixed I, energy depends on the square of angular speed.

2. Derivation from particle motion

  1. For a particle of mass m at distance r, its linear speed is v = rω.
  2. Its kinetic energy is ½mv² = ½m(rω)².
  3. Add the energy of all particles: K = ½ω²Σmr².
  4. Since I = Σmr², therefore K = ½Iω².

4. Rolling body

A body rolling without slipping has both translational motion of its centre of mass and rotation about its centre of mass.

Ktotal = ½Mvcm² + ½Icmω²

For pure rolling, vcm = Rω. Do not omit either energy term.

5. Work–energy relation

Wnet = ΔKrot

Work done by the net torque changes rotational kinetic energy. For a constant torque in the direction of angular displacement, W = τθ.

6. Solved examples

Example 1: I = 2 kg m² and ω = 3 rad/s.

K = ½ × 2 × 3² = 9 J

Example 2: A flywheel has I = 4 kg m² and rotational energy 18 J. Find ω.

18 = ½ × 4 × ω² ⇒ ω = 3 rad/s

Example 3: If angular speed doubles while I stays constant, K′ = 4K.

7. Exam checkpoints

  • Convert rpm to rad/s before substitution: ω = 2πN/60.
  • Use the moment of inertia for the stated axis.
  • Square angular velocity; do not use K ∝ ω.
  • For rolling, add translational and rotational energies.
  • Rotational kinetic energy cannot be negative.
Quick check: If I = 0.5 kg m² and ω = 4 rad/s, K = ½ × 0.5 × 16 = 4 J.
