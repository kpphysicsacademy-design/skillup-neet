🧲 Self Inductance

Understand how a changing current induces an emf in the same coil, how inductance depends on coil geometry, and how energy is stored in a magnetic field.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations
Question 1 of 300%

1. What is self-induction?

When current in a coil changes, the magnetic field produced by that current also changes. The changing magnetic flux linked with the same coil induces an emf in it. This phenomenon is called self-induction.

ε = −L (dI/dt)
Self-induction is the electrical analogue of inertia: an inductor opposes a change in current, not the current itself.

2. Self-inductance and flux linkage

For a linear coil, flux linkage NΦ is proportional to current I. The constant of proportionality is the self-inductance L.

L = NΦ/I   ;   NΦ = LI

Here N is the number of turns and Φ is magnetic flux through each turn. The SI unit of L is the henry (H).

3. Meaning of one henry

A coil has an inductance of one henry if a current changing at one ampere per second induces an emf of one volt in it (magnitude).

1 H = 1 V·s·A⁻¹

Inductance depends on the magnetic environment and geometry of the coil, rather than the instantaneous current for a linear medium.

4. Inductance of a long solenoid

For a long solenoid of N turns, length l, cross-sectional area A and core permeability μ, assuming an ideal long solenoid:

L = μN²A/l

More turns and a larger cross-sectional area increase inductance. A longer solenoid decreases inductance. A higher-permeability core generally increases it.

5. Back emf and Lenz's law

The induced emf opposes the change in current that produces it. If current is increasing, the self-induced emf acts against the increase. If current is decreasing, it acts to sustain the current.

ε = −L (dI/dt)
The negative sign is Lenz's law. It does not mean the emf magnitude is always negative.

6. Energy stored in an inductor

Work must be done to establish current against the self-induced emf. This energy is stored in the magnetic field of the inductor.

U = ½LI²

For a fixed inductance, doubling the current makes the stored energy four times as large.

7. LR circuit and current growth

When a resistor R and inductor L are connected to a DC source, the inductor delays the growth of current. The characteristic time constant is:

τ = L/R

For a series LR circuit switched on, current approaches its steady value exponentially: I = (V/R)(1 − e−t/τ). After a long time with ideal DC, the inductor's induced emf becomes zero.

8. Worked examples and quick revision

Example 1: An inductor of 3 H carries a current changing at 2 A/s. The induced emf magnitude is:

|ε| = L|dI/dt| = 3 × 2 = 6 V

Example 2: A 2 H inductor carries 3 A. Its stored energy is:

U = ½ × 2 × 3² = 9 J

Quick revision:

  • Self-induced emf: ε = −L dI/dt.
  • Inductance: L = NΦ/I for a linear coil.
  • Long solenoid: L = μN²A/l.
  • Magnetic energy: U = ½LI².
  • LR time constant: τ = L/R.
  • Unit: henry (H).
