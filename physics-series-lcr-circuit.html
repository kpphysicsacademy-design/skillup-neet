🔄 Series LCR Circuit

Study a series resistor–inductor–capacitor circuit, its impedance, resonance, phase relationships, power factor and frequency response.

🌱 Easy Mode🧠 30 questions✨ Explanations
Question 1 of 300%

1. Series LCR circuit

A series LCR circuit consists of a resistor \(R\), inductor \(L\) and capacitor \(C\) connected in series to an AC source. The same instantaneous current flows through all three components.

\[V=V_R+V_L+V_C\quad\text{(phasor sum)}\]

The resistor voltage is in phase with current; inductor voltage leads current by 90°; capacitor voltage lags current by 90°.

2. Reactances and impedance

Inductive and capacitive reactances depend on the AC frequency:

\[X_L=\omega L=2\pi fL,\qquad X_C=\frac{1}{\omega C}=\frac{1}{2\pi fC}\]

The net reactance is \(X_L-X_C\), and the impedance magnitude is:

\[Z=\sqrt{R^2+(X_L-X_C)^2}\]

Hence the RMS current is \(I=V/Z\).

3. Phasor diagram and phase angle

In the phasor diagram, \(V_R\) lies along the current reference, while \(V_L\) and \(V_C\) point in opposite perpendicular directions.

\[\tan\phi=\frac{X_L-X_C}{R},\qquad \cos\phi=\frac{R}{Z}\]

If \(X_L>X_C\), current lags voltage (inductive). If \(X_C>X_L\), current leads voltage (capacitive).

4. Resonance in a series LCR circuit

Series resonance occurs when inductive and capacitive reactances become equal.

\[X_L=X_C\quad\Rightarrow\quad \omega_0L=\frac{1}{\omega_0C}\]

At resonance, net reactance is zero, impedance is minimum and equals \(R\), and current is maximum for a fixed supply voltage.

At resonance, voltage and current are in phase and the power factor is unity.

5. Resonant frequency

The angular resonant frequency and ordinary resonant frequency are:

\[\omega_0=\frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}},\qquad f_0=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}\]

The ideal resonant frequency depends on L and C, not on R. Increasing either L or C lowers the resonant frequency.

6. Behaviour below and above resonance

  • Below resonance: \(X_C>X_L\); circuit is capacitive and current leads voltage.
  • At resonance: \(X_C=X_L\); circuit is resistive and current is maximum.
  • Above resonance: \(X_L>X_C\); circuit is inductive and current lags voltage.
As frequency rises, \(X_L\) increases while \(X_C\) decreases.

7. Quality factor and bandwidth

For a series LCR circuit, the quality factor at resonance is:

\[Q=\frac{\omega_0L}{R}=\frac{1}{\omega_0CR}\]

The bandwidth is the difference between the upper and lower half-power frequencies:

\[\Delta f=f_2-f_1=\frac{R}{2\pi L},\qquad Q=\frac{f_0}{\Delta f}\]

A higher Q means a sharper, narrower resonance curve.

8. Power and quick revision

Average power consumed by the series LCR circuit is:

\[P=VI\cos\phi=I^2R\]
  • Impedance: \(Z=\sqrt{R^2+(X_L-X_C)^2}\).
  • At resonance: \(Z=R\), \(I_{max}=V/R\).
  • Resonant frequency: \(f_0=1/(2\pi\sqrt{LC})\).
  • Power factor at resonance: 1.
  • Bandwidth: \(\Delta f=R/(2\pi L)\).
  • Quality factor: \(Q=f_0/\Delta f\).
