1. What is a base quantity?

A base quantity is one of the agreed physical quantities chosen as an independent starting point for a system of measurement. Other quantities can be expressed using combinations of base quantities.

Seven SI base quantities → derived quantities

Examples of derived quantities include area, velocity, force, pressure and energy.

2. The seven SI base quantities

The International System of Units (SI) defines exactly seven base quantities and seven corresponding base units.

  • Length — metre (m)
  • Mass — kilogram (kg)
  • Time — second (s)
  • Electric current — ampere (A)
  • Thermodynamic temperature — kelvin (K)
  • Amount of substance — mole (mol)
  • Luminous intensity — candela (cd)

3. Correct unit names and symbols

Unit names are written in lowercase in text, even when the symbol is uppercase. Symbols are case-sensitive and do not take a plural ending or a full stop.

  • metre — m; kilogram — kg; second — s
  • ampere — A; kelvin — K; mole — mol; candela — cd
  • Write 5 kg, not 5 kgs; write 300 K, not 300 °K.
Symbols named after people begin with a capital letter: A, K. Other listed symbols are lowercase.

4. Base dimensions

In dimensional analysis, the seven base dimensions are represented by symbols such as [L], [M], [T], [I], [Θ], [N] and [J]. They represent length, mass, time, electric current, thermodynamic temperature, amount of substance and luminous intensity.

  • Speed: [L T⁻¹]
  • Force: [M L T⁻²]
  • Energy: [M L² T⁻²]

5. SI base units and derived units

Derived units are products or quotients of base units. Some derived units have special names and symbols.

  • Area: m²; volume: m³
  • Velocity: m s⁻¹; acceleration: m s⁻²
  • Force: newton (N) = kg m s⁻²
  • Energy: joule (J) = kg m² s⁻²
  • Pressure: pascal (Pa) = kg m⁻¹ s⁻²

6. Modern definitions use constants

Since 20 May 2019, all seven SI base units are defined by assigning exact numerical values to seven defining constants of nature or specified atomic properties.

  • metre — speed of light in vacuum, c
  • kilogram — Planck constant, h
  • second — caesium-133 hyperfine transition frequency, ΔνCs
  • ampere — elementary charge, e
  • kelvin — Boltzmann constant, k
  • mole — Avogadro constant, Nₐ
  • candela — luminous efficacy, Kcd, at 540 × 10¹² Hz

7. Important SI writing rules

Leave a space between the numerical value and unit symbol, except for plane-angle symbols such as °. Unit symbols are mathematical entities and are not followed by a plural s.

  • Correct: 12 m, 7.2 kg, 25 °C, 300 K.
  • Incorrect: 12m, 7.2 kgs, 300 °K.
  • Use a leading zero for values below one: 0.5 m.

8. How to identify base and derived quantities

A quantity is base if it belongs to the seven agreed independent quantities. If its unit can be expressed as a combination of base units, it is derived.

Quick check: current is a base quantity measured in ampere; charge is derived because 1 C = 1 A·s.
