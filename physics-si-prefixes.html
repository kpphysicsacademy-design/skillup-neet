1. Why do we use SI prefixes?

SI prefixes are placed before unit names or symbols to represent decimal multiples and submultiples. They make very large and very small measurements easier to write and compare.

prefixed value = power of ten × unit

For example, 1 kilometre = 10³ metres and 1 millimetre = 10⁻³ metres.

2. Common larger prefixes

These prefixes represent multiples of the base unit.

  • deca (da) = 10¹; hecto (h) = 10²
  • kilo (k) = 10³; mega (M) = 10⁶
  • giga (G) = 10⁹; tera (T) = 10¹²
  • peta (P) = 10¹⁵; exa (E) = 10¹⁸
  • zetta (Z) = 10²¹; yotta (Y) = 10²⁴
  • ronna (R) = 10²⁷; quetta (Q) = 10³⁰

3. Common smaller prefixes

These prefixes represent submultiples of the unit.

  • deci (d) = 10⁻¹; centi (c) = 10⁻²
  • milli (m) = 10⁻³; micro (µ) = 10⁻⁶
  • nano (n) = 10⁻⁹; pico (p) = 10⁻¹²
  • femto (f) = 10⁻¹⁵; atto (a) = 10⁻¹⁸
  • zepto (z) = 10⁻²¹; yocto (y) = 10⁻²⁴
  • ronto (r) = 10⁻²⁷; quecto (q) = 10⁻³⁰

4. Prefix symbols are case-sensitive

Capitalization matters. A lowercase m means milli (10⁻³), while uppercase M means mega (10⁶). Likewise, k is kilo, and K is the unit symbol for kelvin—not a prefix.

Remember: m = milli, M = mega; µ = micro, n = nano.

5. Converting with powers of ten

To convert a prefixed unit to the unprefixed unit, multiply by its power of ten. To convert between two prefixes, subtract their exponents.

value in target prefix = value × 10(source exponent − target exponent)

Example: 3.5 km = 3.5 × 10³ m = 3500 m.

6. Prefixes in area and volume

When a prefixed length unit is squared or cubed, the power applies to the whole conversion factor.

  • 1 cm = 10⁻² m
  • 1 cm² = (10⁻²)² m² = 10⁻⁴ m²
  • 1 cm³ = (10⁻²)³ m³ = 10⁻⁶ m³
  • 1 mm³ = 10⁻⁹ m³

7. Writing prefix-unit combinations

A prefix symbol is attached directly to the unit symbol, with no space between them. The combination represents one unit symbol.

  • Correct: km, mA, µs, GHz, nm.
  • Do not combine multiple prefixes: use nm, not mµm.
  • Prefix symbols are not standalone units; they must accompany a unit.

8. Choosing a suitable prefix

Choose a prefix that keeps the numerical value convenient to read, often between 0.1 and 1000 in science problems. Always check the sign and size of the exponent before converting.

Quick memory: kilo is +3, milli is −3, micro is −6, nano is −9, and mega is +6.
