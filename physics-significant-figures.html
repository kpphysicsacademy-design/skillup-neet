🔢 Significant Figures

Learn how significant figures communicate measurement precision, how to count and round them, and how to apply the rules in calculations.

1. Meaning of significant figures

Significant figures are the meaningful digits in a measured value. They include all certain digits and the first uncertain (estimated) digit. They communicate the precision of a measurement.

4.52 cm → 3 significant figures
A measurement should not suggest greater precision than the instrument supports.

2. Non-zero digits and zeros between digits

Every non-zero digit is significant. A zero between two non-zero digits is also significant.

  • 347 has 3 significant figures.
  • 5.06 has 3 significant figures.
  • 7005 has 4 significant figures.

3. Leading zeros

Zeros to the left of the first non-zero digit are not significant. They only locate the decimal point.

0.0045 → 2 significant figures

Likewise, 0.000801 has 3 significant figures because the zero between 8 and 1 counts.

4. Trailing zeros and scientific notation

Trailing zeros to the right of a decimal point are significant. Trailing zeros in a whole number without a decimal point can be ambiguous.

  • 2.300 has 4 significant figures.
  • 100.0 has 4 significant figures.
  • 9.00 × 10² clearly has 3 significant figures.
Use scientific notation to make the intended precision of a whole number clear.

5. Rounding to significant figures

Start counting at the first non-zero digit. Keep the required number of digits, then inspect the next digit. For the usual school-level rule, a next digit of 5 or more rounds the last retained digit up.

6.284 to 3 significant figures = 6.28
0.007856 to 2 significant figures = 0.0079

6. Multiplication and division

For multiplication or division, the result is normally reported with the same number of significant figures as the measured input having the fewest significant figures.

2.5 × 3.42 = 8.55 ≈ 8.6

The input 2.5 has only 2 significant figures, so the result is reported to 2 significant figures.

7. Addition and subtraction

For addition and subtraction, round the result to the least precise decimal place among the inputs—not to the fewest significant figures.

12.11 + 0.3 = 12.41 ≈ 12.4

The least precise input is given to the tenths place.

8. Exact numbers and common cautions

Exact counted quantities and defined conversion factors do not limit the significant figures in a calculation. Do not confuse decimal places with significant figures.

  • Exact count: 12 students (exact).
  • Measured value: 12.0 cm (3 significant figures).
  • Keep guard digits during intermediate calculations and round the final result.
