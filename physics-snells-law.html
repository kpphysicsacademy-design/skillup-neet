💡 Reflection of Light

1. What does Snell’s law describe?

Snell’s law gives the relationship between the angle of incidence and angle of refraction when light crosses the boundary between two transparent media. The angles are measured from the normal.

n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r
The incident ray, refracted ray and normal lie in the same plane.

2. Refractive index

The absolute refractive index of a medium is the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to its speed in that medium.

n = c / v

Here c is the speed in vacuum and v is the speed in the medium. A higher refractive index means a lower speed of light.

3. Relative refractive index

The refractive index of medium 2 with respect to medium 1 is the ratio of the speed of light in medium 1 to that in medium 2.

n₂₁ = n₂ / n₁ = v₁ / v₂

Therefore Snell’s law can also be written as sin i / sin r = n₂₁.

4. Predicting the direction of bending

If n₂ is greater than n₁, light bends towards the normal and r is less than i. If n₂ is less than n₁, light bends away from the normal and r is greater than i (for ordinary transmitted rays).

Compare refractive indices before deciding the direction of bending.

5. Solving numerical problems

Identify n₁, n₂ and i, substitute them into Snell’s law, and isolate sin r.

sin r = (n₁ / n₂) sin i

Then use the inverse sine to find r. Check that the sine value is between 0 and 1.

6. Frequency, speed and wavelength

At a stationary boundary, frequency remains unchanged. Speed and wavelength change in proportion.

v = fλ   and   λ₂ / λ₁ = n₁ / n₂

Snell’s law can be derived from the change in wave speed at the interface.

7. Critical angle and total internal reflection

For light travelling from a higher-index medium to a lower-index medium, the critical angle C is the incidence angle for which r = 90°.

sin C = n₂ / n₁   (n₁ > n₂)

For i greater than C, total internal reflection occurs and there is no transmitted refracted ray.

8. Quick revision and common errors

  • Always measure i and r from the normal.
  • Use the refractive index of the medium the ray is entering as n₂.
  • For n₂ > n₁, r < i; for n₂ < n₁, r > i.
  • Frequency remains constant across a stationary boundary.
  • At normal incidence, i = r = 0° and the ray does not bend.
Do not measure angles from the surface; doing so gives the complementary angle.
