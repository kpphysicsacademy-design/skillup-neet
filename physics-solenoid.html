📘 Virtual Learn: Solenoid

Understand the field pattern, direction rule and magnetic field equation before your mastery quiz.

4 steps

1. What is a solenoid?

A solenoid is a long cylindrical coil made of many closely wound turns of insulated wire. When current flows, it produces a magnetic field similar to a bar magnet.

Key idea: Inside a long solenoid, the magnetic field is strong and nearly uniform. Outside it is very weak.

2. Right-hand grip rule

Curl the fingers of your right hand in the direction of conventional current through the turns. Your thumb points along the magnetic field inside the solenoid and towards its north pole.

Viewed from an end: anticlockwise current makes that end a north pole; clockwise current makes it a south pole.

3. Magnetic field inside a long solenoid

Apply Ampere’s circuital law using a rectangular Amperian loop. For a long solenoid, the external field is negligible and the internal field is nearly constant.

\(B=\mu_0 nI=\mu_0\dfrac{N}{L}I\)
n = turns per unit length = N/L (m⁻¹).
μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T m A⁻¹.
With a linear magnetic core: \(B=\mu_r\mu_0 nI\).

4. Interactive solenoid experiment

Adjust current and turn density. Observe the field strength change in real time.

\(B=\mu_0nI\)
Calculated field: 2.51 mT
Double I or n → double B. Reverse current → reverse field direction.
Worked example: n=2000 m⁻¹, I=2 A gives B=5.03 × 10⁻³ T = 5.03 mT.
