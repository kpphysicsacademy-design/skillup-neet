🪞 Spherical Mirrors

Understand concave and convex mirrors, principal rays, image formation, sign convention and numerical problems.

1. Spherical mirrors

A spherical mirror has a reflecting surface that forms part of a sphere. A concave mirror reflects from its inward-curved surface; a convex mirror reflects from its outward-curved surface.

Concave mirrors are converging mirrors; convex mirrors are diverging mirrors.

2. Important terms

  • Pole (P): geometric centre of the reflecting surface.
  • Centre of curvature (C): centre of the parent sphere.
  • Principal axis: line through P and C.
  • Principal focus (F): point where paraxial parallel rays converge or appear to diverge.
  • Aperture: effective diameter of the reflecting surface.

3. Focal length and radius of curvature

For a spherical mirror under the paraxial approximation, the principal focus lies halfway between the pole and centre of curvature.

f = R / 2   or   R = 2f

This relation applies to the magnitudes; signs depend on the Cartesian sign convention.

4. Principal rays and image construction

  • A ray parallel to the principal axis reflects through F (concave) or appears to come from F (convex).
  • A ray through C (or directed towards C) retraces its path.
  • A ray through F (or directed towards F) reflects parallel to the axis.
  • A ray striking the pole reflects with equal angles to the principal axis.
Two principal rays are enough to locate the image in a ray diagram.

5. Mirror formula and magnification

Using the Cartesian sign convention, distances are measured from the pole. Distances measured in the direction of incident light are positive.

1/f = 1/v + 1/u
m = hᵢ/hₒ = −v/u

Here u is object distance, v is image distance, and m is linear magnification. Negative m indicates an inverted image.

6. Image formation by a concave mirror

  • Object at infinity: image at F, real, inverted and highly diminished.
  • Beyond C: between C and F, real, inverted and diminished.
  • At C: at C, real, inverted and same size.
  • Between C and F: beyond C, real, inverted and enlarged.
  • At F: at infinity.
  • Between F and P: behind mirror, virtual, erect and enlarged.

7. Image formation by a convex mirror

For a real object at any finite distance, a convex mirror forms an image behind the mirror between P and F. The image is always virtual, erect and diminished.

Convex mirrors are used as rear-view and security mirrors because they provide a wide field of view.

8. Sign convention and quick revision

  • Pole P is the origin; principal axis is the reference axis.
  • Distances to the left of the pole are negative; to the right are positive.
  • Heights above the axis are positive; below are negative.
  • Concave mirror: f is negative. Convex mirror: f is positive.
  • Always use consistent signs in the mirror formula.
Check the sign of v and m to identify whether the image is real/virtual and inverted/erect.
