⚙️ Standard Moments of Inertia

A quick, exam-ready guide to common rigid bodies and their moments of inertia about specified axes.

1. Particle and system of particles

I = Σmᵢrᵢ²   or   I = ∫r² dm

The perpendicular distance from the axis is squared. Always identify the axis before selecting a standard formula.

2. Standard formula table

Body and axisMoment of inertia
Thin ring, central perpendicular axisMR²
Thin ring, diameter½MR²
Uniform disc, central perpendicular axis½MR²
Uniform disc, diameter¼MR²
Solid cylinder, symmetry axis½MR²
Hollow cylinder (thin), symmetry axisMR²
Solid sphere, diameter⅖MR²
Thin spherical shell, diameter⅔MR²
Uniform rod, centre, perpendicularML²/12
Uniform rod, end, perpendicularML²/3
Rectangular lamina, centre, perpendicularM(a²+b²)/12
Rectangular lamina, edge parallel to bMa²/3

For a rectangular lamina, a and b are its side lengths. For a thin ring about a diameter, use the perpendicular-axis theorem.

3. Useful theorems

Parallel axis: I = ICM + Md²
Perpendicular axis: Iz = Ix + Iy

The perpendicular-axis theorem applies to a plane lamina with all three axes passing through the same point.

4. Quick comparisons

  • Ring vs disc (same M, R; central perpendicular axis): ring has twice the disc's I.
  • Solid sphere vs thin spherical shell (same M, R): shell has larger I.
  • Rod about end vs centre: end-axis I is four times the centre-axis I.
  • Moving mass farther from the axis increases I because distance is squared.

5. Worked examples

Rod: M = 3 kg, L = 2 m, axis through centre.

I = ML²/12 = 3×4/12 = 1 kg m²

Disc: M = 4 kg, R = 0.5 m, central axis.

I = ½MR² = ½×4×0.25 = 0.5 kg m²

Solid sphere: M = 5 kg, R = 0.2 m.

I = ⅖MR² = 0.08 kg m²

6. Exam checkpoints

  • SI unit: kg m²; dimension: [ML²].
  • Check whether the axis is a diameter, symmetry axis, centre or end.
  • For a plane lamina, perpendicular-axis theorem connects in-plane and normal axes.
  • Use parallel-axis theorem to shift from the centre-of-mass axis.
