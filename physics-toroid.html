📘 Learn: Magnetic Field in a Toroid

A toroid is a long solenoid bent into a closed circular ring. Its turns are wound around a doughnut-shaped core.

1. Structure and magnetic field

A toroid has N closely spaced turns carrying current I. The magnetic field inside its core follows circular paths around the toroid’s central axis. Use the right-hand grip rule to determine its direction.

  • Inside the core: field is approximately confined to the magnetic material.
  • Central hollow region: field is approximately zero for an ideal toroid.
  • Outside the windings: field is approximately zero for an ideal toroid.

2. Derivation using Ampere’s circuital law

Choose a circular Amperian loop of radius r inside the core. By symmetry, the field has constant magnitude along this loop and is tangential everywhere.

∮ B · dl = μ₀ Ienclosed

The loop encloses all N turns, each carrying current I, so Ienclosed = NI.

B(2πr) = μ₀NI
B = μ₀NI / (2πr)

For a linear magnetic core with relative permeability μr:

B = μ₀μrNI / (2πr)

3. What affects the magnetic field?

  • More turns (N) → stronger field.
  • More current (I) → stronger field.
  • Greater mean radius (r) → weaker field at that radius.
  • Greater core permeability (μr) → stronger field in a linear material.

Remember: B is directly proportional to N, I and μr, and inversely proportional to r.

4. Worked example

Question: A toroid has 500 turns, carries 2 A and has mean radius 0.10 m. Find the air-core magnetic field.

Given: N = 500, I = 2 A, r = 0.10 m, μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T m A⁻¹.

B = (4π × 10⁻⁷ × 500 × 2) / (2π × 0.10)
B = 2.0 × 10⁻³ T = 2.0 mT

5. Important exam points

  • The field inside an ideal toroid is not exactly uniform across its radial thickness; B varies as 1/r.
  • At a fixed radius, the field is constant along a circular Amperian path.
  • Reversing the current reverses the magnetic field direction.
  • The SI unit of magnetic field is tesla (T).
