Magnetic field inside a toroid

A toroid is a solenoid bent into a closed circular ring. Explore how its magnetic field depends on current, turns and the mean radius.

KEY IDEA

Apply Ampere’s circuital law

Choose a circular Amperian path of radius \(r\) inside the toroidal core. By symmetry, the magnetic field has constant magnitude along this path and is tangential to it.

\[\oint \vec B\cdot d\vec l=\mu_0 I_{\text{enclosed}}\]

The path encloses \(N\) turns, each carrying current \(I\), so \(I_{\text{enclosed}}=NI\). Therefore:

\[B(2\pi r)=\mu_0NI\]
\[\boxed{B=\frac{\mu_0NI}{2\pi r}}\]

Remember: field increases with current and number of turns, and decreases as the mean radius increases. For a core with permeability \(\mu=\mu_0\mu_r\), use \(B=\frac{\mu_0\mu_rNI}{2\pi r}\) in the linear material approximation.
