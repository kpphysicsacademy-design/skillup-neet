Torque

Understand how a force turns an object around a pivot, and how force, distance and direction affect rotation.

1. What is torque?

Torque is the turning effect of a force about a fixed point or axis. A force can produce a larger turning effect when it is applied farther from the pivot.

Everyday example: A door is easier to open when you push near its handle, far from the hinges.

2. Torque formula

τ = rF sin θ

Here, r is the distance from pivot to the point where force is applied, F is the applied force, and θ is the angle between the position vector and force.

  • SI unit: newton metre (N m)
  • Torque is an axial vector; its direction follows the right-hand rule.
  • Only the component of force perpendicular to the position vector produces torque.

3. Lever arm and special angles

The moment arm is the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force. Torque can also be written as τ = F × perpendicular moment arm.

θ = 90°Maximum torque: τ = rF
θ = 0° or 180°Zero torque: force acts along the radius
θ = 30°Torque = ½rF

4. Direction and sign

For a body rotating in the plane of the page, use the right-hand rule to identify the torque direction. With anticlockwise chosen as positive:

  • Anticlockwise torque is positive.
  • Clockwise torque is negative.
  • Net torque is the algebraic sum of all torques about the chosen pivot.

5. Couple and rotational balance

A couple consists of two equal, opposite and parallel forces acting along different lines. Its net force is zero, but it produces a turning effect. The couple moment is Fd, where d is the perpendicular separation between the forces.

For a rigid body about a fixed axis, net torque is related to angular acceleration by τnet = Iα. If the moment of inertia is finite and non-zero, zero net torque means zero angular acceleration.

6. Worked examples

Example A

A 10 N force acts perpendicularly at 0.5 m from a pivot.

τ = rF = 0.5 × 10 = 5 N m.

Example B

A 20 N force acts at 30° to a 2 m position vector.

τ = 2 × 20 × sin 30° = 20 N m.

7. Remember

  • Increase force or perpendicular distance to increase torque.
  • A force through the pivot produces no torque about that pivot.
  • Always check the angle before substituting into τ = rF sin θ.
  • Torque and energy share the unit N m, but they are different physical quantities.

Quick concept check

A 15 N force acts perpendicular to a wrench 0.4 m long. What is the torque magnitude?

Show answer

6 N m. Use τ = rF = 0.4 × 15.
