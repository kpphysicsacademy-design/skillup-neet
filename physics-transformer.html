🔌 Transformer

Learn the principle, construction, working, turns ratio, losses, efficiency and applications of an AC transformer.

1. Principle of a transformer

A transformer is a static electrical device that transfers AC electrical energy from one circuit to another at the same frequency, usually changing voltage and current levels.

It works on the principle of mutual electromagnetic induction: changing current in the primary winding produces changing magnetic flux that links the secondary winding and induces an emf.

\[\varepsilon_s=-N_s\frac{d\Phi}{dt}\]

2. Construction and working

A basic transformer has two insulated windings — primary and secondary — wound on a common laminated soft-iron or silicon-steel core.

  • Primary winding: connected to the AC input.
  • Secondary winding: connected to the load.
  • Core: provides a low-reluctance path for magnetic flux.

Alternating primary current creates changing core flux, inducing emf in both windings.

3. Transformer turns and voltage ratio

For an ideal transformer, induced voltage is proportional to the number of turns:

\[\frac{V_s}{V_p}=\frac{N_s}{N_p}\]

Here p and s denote primary and secondary. If secondary turns exceed primary turns, output voltage is higher; if fewer, output voltage is lower.

4. Step-up and step-down transformers

Step-up transformer: \(N_s>N_p\), hence \(V_s>V_p\). For an ideal transformer, secondary current is lower.

A transformer changes voltage and current in opposite proportions; it does not increase power in an ideal model.

5. Current and power relations

For an ideal transformer, input power equals output power:

\[V_pI_p=V_sI_s\]
\[\frac{I_s}{I_p}=\frac{N_p}{N_s}=\frac{V_p}{V_s}\]

Voltage ratio follows turns ratio, while current ratio is the inverse. Real transformers have losses, so output power is less than input power.

6. Transformer losses

  • Copper loss: heating in windings due to resistance, \(I^2R\).
  • Eddy-current loss: circulating currents induced in the core; reduced by laminating the core.
  • Hysteresis loss: energy dissipated during repeated magnetisation; reduced using suitable soft magnetic core material.
  • Flux leakage: some magnetic flux does not link both windings; good core design reduces it.

7. Efficiency and AC operation

Transformer efficiency is the ratio of output power to input power:

\[\eta=\frac{P_{out}}{P_{in}}\times100\%\]

Transformers operate with changing magnetic flux. A steady DC supply does not produce continuous changing flux after the switching transient, so it cannot provide sustained secondary emf and may cause excessive primary current.

The secondary AC frequency is the same as the primary frequency.

8. Applications and quick revision

  • Step-up transformers raise voltage for efficient long-distance transmission.
  • Step-down transformers lower voltage for distribution and electronic devices.
  • Turns ratio: \(V_s/V_p=N_s/N_p\).
  • Ideal power: \(V_pI_p=V_sI_s\).
  • Current ratio: \(I_s/I_p=N_p/N_s\).
  • Laminated core reduces eddy-current loss.
  • Transformer changes voltage/current, not frequency.
Remember: transformers require changing magnetic flux and work with AC in normal operation.
