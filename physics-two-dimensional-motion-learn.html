🧭 Two-dimensional Motion

Explore motion in a plane using two perpendicular axes. Learn how vectors split into components and how x- and y-motion work together.

📘 Learn🎮 Simulator🧠 Practice

1. What is two-dimensional motion?

When an object's position changes in a plane, its motion is described using two coordinates, usually x (horizontal) and y (vertical). Examples include a ball thrown at an angle, a boat crossing a river and a moving insect on a wall.

Position: r = x i + y j

The unit vectors i and j indicate the positive x- and y-directions.

2. Scalars and vectors

Scalar

Magnitude only. Examples: distance, speed, mass and time.

Vector

Magnitude and direction. Examples: displacement, velocity and acceleration.

3. Resolve a vector into components

For a vector A making angle θ with the positive x-axis:

Aₓ = A cos θ    Aᵧ = A sin θ

The signs of components depend on the quadrant. In quadrant II, x is negative and y is positive.

Example: A = 10 m at 30° above +x.
Aₓ = 10 cos 30° = 5√3 m
Aᵧ = 10 sin 30° = 5 m

4. Resultant of perpendicular components

R = √(Aₓ² + Aᵧ²)    tan θ = Aᵧ / Aₓ

Use the component signs to select the correct quadrant for the direction angle.

Example: 3 m east and 4 m north give R = √(3² + 4²) = 5 m, directed at tan⁻¹(4/3) ≈ 53.1° north of east.

5. Position, velocity and acceleration

r = x i + y j
v = vₓ i + vᵧ j
a = aₓ i + aᵧ j
  • Average velocity = displacement / elapsed time.
  • Instantaneous velocity is tangent to the path.
  • Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity; it can change speed, direction or both.
  • For constant acceleration: x = x₀ + uₓt + ½aₓt² and y = y₀ + uᵧt + ½aᵧt².

6. Independent motion along x and y

The component equations can be solved separately, but both directions share the same time t. In ideal projectile motion (neglecting air resistance), horizontal acceleration is zero while vertical acceleration is −g if upward is positive.

aₓ = 0    aᵧ = −g

Quick revision

  • Two-dimensional motion needs two coordinates.
  • Resolve vectors using cosine for the adjacent component and sine for the opposite component when θ is measured from +x.
  • Perpendicular resultant: use Pythagoras.
  • Velocity direction is tangent to the trajectory.
  • Analyse x and y separately, using the same time interval.
