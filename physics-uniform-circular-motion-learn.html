📌 Quick Cheat Sheet

Uniform circular motion means constant speed on a circular path, but continuously changing velocity direction.

Angular speedω = 2π/T = 2πf (rad/s)
Linear speedv = ωr = 2πr/T (m/s)
Centripetal accelerationa = v²/r = ω²r (m/s²)
Net inward forceF = mv²/r = mω²r (N)
Period & frequencyT = 1/f (s); f in Hz

Example: v = 6 m/s, r = 3 m → a = 36/3 = 12 m/s² inward.

NEET tip: centripetal force is the name for the net inward force, not an extra force. Instantaneous velocity is tangential; acceleration is inward and perpendicular to it.

Learn: what changes in circular motion?

At each point the velocity is tangent to the circle. Because its direction changes, the object accelerates toward the centre despite constant speed. The inward force may be supplied by tension, friction or gravity, depending on the physical system.

One revolution covers distance 2πr but has zero net displacement. The average velocity over a full revolution is zero, although average speed is 2πr/T.
