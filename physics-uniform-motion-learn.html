➡️ Uniform Motion

Understand motion at constant velocity using simple examples, equations and position–time graphs.

📘 Learn🎮 Simulator🧠 Practice

1. What is uniform motion?

An object is in uniform motion when it covers equal displacements in equal intervals of time. In straight-line uniform motion, its velocity remains constant in both magnitude and direction.

Example: a train moving along a straight track at a steady 15 m/s.

2. Speed and velocity

Speed is the distance travelled per unit time. Velocity is displacement per unit time and includes direction. In uniform straight-line motion, velocity is constant and speed is constant.

Speed = Distance / Time
Velocity = Displacement / Time

3. Equation of uniform motion

If an object starts at position x₀ and moves with constant velocity v, its position after time t is:

x = x₀ + vt

If the initial position is zero, displacement is s = vt. The slope of a position–time graph gives velocity.

4. Position–time graph

  • A straight sloping line represents constant velocity.
  • A positive slope represents motion in the positive direction.
  • A negative slope represents motion in the negative direction.
  • A horizontal line represents zero velocity (object at rest).
  • A steeper line means a greater speed.

5. Acceleration in uniform motion

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. Since velocity does not change in uniform straight-line motion:

a = 0 m/s²

6. Worked example

A cyclist moves at a constant 6 m/s for 12 s. Find the displacement.
Given: v = 6 m/s, t = 12 s
Use: s = vt
Answer: s = 6 × 12 = 72 m.

7. Quick facts

  • Uniform motion has constant velocity.
  • Distance travelled per equal time interval is equal when direction stays fixed.
  • Displacement is directly proportional to time for constant velocity.
  • The position–time graph is a straight line.
  • Acceleration is zero.
