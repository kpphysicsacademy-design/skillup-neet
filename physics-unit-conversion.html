🔄 Unit Conversion

Convert measurements confidently using conversion factors, unit cancellation and dimensional reasoning.

1. What is unit conversion?

Unit conversion expresses the same physical quantity in a different unit. The measured quantity does not change; only its numerical value and unit representation change.

\(Q = n\times u\)

For example, 2 km and 2000 m represent the same length.

2. Conversion factors

A conversion factor is a ratio of two equal quantities. Its value is one, so multiplying by it changes the unit without changing the physical quantity.

\(2\,\text{km}\times\frac{1000\,\text{m}}{1\,\text{km}}=2000\,\text{m}\)

Choose the ratio so the original unit cancels.

3. Length, mass and time

  • 1 km = 1000 m; 1 m = 100 cm = 1000 mm
  • 1 kg = 1000 g; 1 g = 1000 mg
  • 1 h = 60 min = 3600 s
  • 1 day = 24 h = 86,400 s

4. Area and volume conversions

Square the linear conversion factor for area and cube it for volume.

  • 1 cm² = 10⁻⁴ m²
  • 1 cm³ = 10⁻⁶ m³
  • 1 L = 10⁻³ m³; 1 mL = 1 cm³
Do not use the length factor directly for area or volume.

5. Speed conversions

Convert both the length and time units. For speed in km/h to m/s, multiply by \(\frac{5}{18}\). For m/s to km/h, multiply by \(\frac{18}{5}\).

\(72\,\text{km h}^{-1}=72\times\frac{5}{18}=20\,\text{m s}^{-1}\)

6. Temperature conversions

Temperature scales use offsets as well as scale factors.

  • \(K=\,^{\circ}C+273.15\)
  • \(^{\circ}C=K-273.15\)
  • \(^{\circ}F=\frac{9}{5}\,^{\circ}C+32\)

Do not treat Celsius-to-kelvin conversion as a simple prefix conversion.

7. CGS and SI conversions

  • 1 dyne = 10⁻⁵ N
  • 1 erg = 10⁻⁷ J
  • 1 N = 10⁵ dynes
  • 1 J = 10⁷ ergs

These relations are useful when converting between centimetre–gram–second and SI systems.

8. A reliable conversion method

  1. Write the given value with its unit.
  2. Choose an equality relating the old and new units.
  3. Arrange the conversion factor so unwanted units cancel.
  4. Apply squared or cubed factors for area and volume.
  5. Check the magnitude and write the final unit.
Always check whether the answer is physically reasonable before rounding.
