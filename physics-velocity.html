Velocity

A quick lesson: meaning, formula and one example.

📘 Learn🎮 Simulator🧠 Practice

📌 Quick Cheat Sheet

Velocity describes the rate of change of displacement. It is a vector with SI unit m/s and dimension [LT⁻¹].

ConceptFormula or fact
Average velocity (1D)v(avg) = (x₂ − x₁) / Δt
Instantaneous velocityv = dx/dt
Average speedTotal distance / Δt
Round tripAverage velocity = 0 if Δt > 0
Sign in 1DPositive or negative denotes direction

Example: x₁ = −4 m, x₂ = +8 m, time = 3 s → displacement = +12 m and average velocity = +4 m/s.

Return trip: 20 m forward then 20 m backward in 10 s → average velocity 0 m/s, but average speed 4 m/s.

NEET tip: average velocity uses displacement, not distance. Choose a positive direction and keep units consistent. Instantaneous speed equals the magnitude of instantaneous velocity.

1. Meaning

Velocity is the rate of change of displacement with time. It tells how fast an object moves and its direction.

Velocity = Displacement ÷ Time

SI unit: m/s • Dimension: [LT⁻¹] • Vector quantity.

2. Important formulas

Average velocity = Δx ÷ Δt
Instantaneous velocity = dx ÷ dt

For constant acceleration: v = u + at.

3. Simple example

A student moves from 3 m to 23 m in 5 s.
Displacement = 23 − 3 = 20 m
Average velocity = 20 ÷ 5 = 4 m/s.

Remember

  • Velocity is a vector; speed is a scalar.
  • Positive and negative signs show direction.
  • On a round trip, average velocity is zero.
  • Velocity changes if speed or direction changes.
