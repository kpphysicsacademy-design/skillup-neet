📘 What is velocity?

Velocity is the rate of change of displacement with time. It tells both how fast an object moves and in which direction.

Average velocity = Displacement ÷ Time
1. Vector quantity

Velocity has magnitude and direction. For example, 8 m/s east is a velocity; 8 m/s alone describes speed.

2. SI unit

The SI unit is metre per second (m/s). Its dimensions are [LT⁻¹].

3. Average velocity

Divide net displacement by the total elapsed time. Do not use total distance in this formula.

4. Instantaneous velocity

Velocity at a particular instant. It is the limiting value of average velocity as the time interval approaches zero.

5. Sign and direction

Choose a positive direction. Positive velocity is along it; negative velocity is in the opposite direction.

6. Uniform velocity

When velocity is constant, both speed and direction remain unchanged. Acceleration is zero.

IMPORTANT RELATIONSHIPS

🧮 Formula toolkit

v̄ = (x₂ − x₁) / (t₂ − t₁)

Average velocity = change in position ÷ elapsed time.

v = dx / dt

Instantaneous velocity is the rate of change of position with time.

v = u + at

For motion with constant acceleration, final velocity equals initial velocity plus acceleration multiplied by time.

Graph connection: The slope of a position–time graph gives velocity. A steeper slope means a greater velocity magnitude; a negative slope means motion in the negative direction.
WORKED EXAMPLE

✍️ Calculate average velocity

A student moves from x = 3 m to x = 23 m in 5 s.
Displacement = 23 − 3 = 20 m
Average velocity = 20 ÷ 5 = 4 m/s in the positive direction.

💡 Remember

  • Distance and displacement are not interchangeable.
  • An object returning to its start has zero average velocity for the complete trip.
  • Velocity can be zero at an instant even when acceleration is non-zero.
  • Velocity changes if either speed or direction changes.
