1. What is a voltmeter?

A voltmeter is an instrument used to measure the potential difference (voltage) between two points in an electric circuit. Its two terminals are connected across the component being measured.

Voltage is a potential difference: it is measured between two points, not at a single point alone.

2. Circuit connection and resistance

A voltmeter is connected in parallel with the component. An ideal voltmeter has infinite resistance and draws no current. A practical voltmeter has very high resistance to minimize the loading effect.

Ideal voltmeter: Rᵥ → ∞

Because the meter is in parallel, the potential difference across the voltmeter is the same as the potential difference across the component.

3. Why high resistance matters

A real voltmeter draws a small current. If its resistance is not sufficiently high compared with the component resistance, it changes the equivalent resistance of the branch and can alter the voltage being measured. This is called the loading effect.

Iᵥ = V/Rᵥ

For a fixed measured voltage V, increasing the input resistance Rᵥ reduces the current drawn by the meter.

4. Convert a galvanometer into a voltmeter

A sensitive galvanometer can be converted into a voltmeter by connecting a large resistance, called a multiplier, in series with its coil.

V = Iᵍ(G + R)
R = V/Iᵍ − G

G is galvanometer resistance, Iᵍ is full-scale deflection current, V is the required voltage range and R is the multiplier resistance.

A larger voltage range requires a larger series multiplier so the galvanometer current does not exceed its full-scale value.

5. Worked example

A galvanometer has resistance 100 Ω and full-scale current 1 mA. Find the series resistance needed to make a 10 V voltmeter.

R = V/Iᵍ − G
R = 10/0.001 − 100 = 9900 Ω

Connect a 9.9 kΩ resistor in series with the galvanometer.

6. Voltmeter range and input resistance

At full-scale deflection, the total resistance of the converted voltmeter is:

Rtotal = V/Iᵍ

For a given galvanometer, a higher voltage range requires a higher total resistance. The voltmeter is still connected in parallel across the circuit element.

7. Polarity and measurement practice

  • For a positive DC reading, connect the positive meter terminal to the higher-potential point.
  • Choose a range greater than the expected voltage before taking a reading.
  • Do not connect a voltmeter in series with the load.
  • Use a meter with sufficiently high input resistance to reduce loading.
